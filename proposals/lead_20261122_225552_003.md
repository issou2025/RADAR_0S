*Project description:*
## The workflow

I come from the layer tradition, AutoCAD, ArchiCAD, Rhino, MicroStation, not Revit. That's not a niche: layer-based CAD carries a large share of architecture practice worldwide, and many firms never organize a model the Revit way. In that tradition a firm's standard (AIA NCS, ISO 13567, a national scheme, or the firm's own) easily runs to **hundreds of layers**, and they carry real meaning: `A-WALL-FULL` vs `A-WALL-PRHT`, existing vs new vs demolition, fire-rated outlines, annotation sets. Layers drive:

- **per-layer graphics**, color, linetype, lineweight, plot on/off
- **per-view control**, freeze/thaw per viewport, layer states per deliverable
- **exchange**, jurisdictions and consultants that require DWG sets with standard layer names, and backgrounds organized entirely by layers

ArchiCAD is the instructive middle: full BIM *and* layers together. Groups + phase filters cover the time dimension beautifully (existing/new/demo reads like a well-set phase filter), but hundreds of named, standardized, graphics-carrying classifications don't fit "a named bucket every element belongs to" as it stands.

## Scale

Stadium-scale architecture means thousands of distinct elements and millions of meshes, every seat an instance. The kernel already speaks 250k- and 1M-element budgets and the 3D viewer instances above a few thousand solids; whatever a layer concept becomes, per-layer visibility, graphics and picking in that regime must be indexed reads, never full-model scans.

## The question

Three honest shapes:

1. **Layers at the exchange boundary only**, in-model organization stays categories + groups + phases; layer standards live in a DWG/IFC mapping (classification to standard layer name + graphics on export; source layer to category on import).
2. **Layers as a first-class kernel concept**, user-defined classification at scale (hundreds of entries, importable standards), per-view display, per-layer graphics. If so: a generalization of groups, or its own shared concept?
3. **An architecture add-on** over a thin kernel classification point.

I'm an architect in the layer tradition, happy to write the concrete workflows (permit deliverables, consultant coordination, background cleanup) in as much detail as useful. What shape did you have in mind?
