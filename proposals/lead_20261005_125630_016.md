# Bake the parts, animate the motion live

Witch's current approach of code-built 3D primitives baked into normal-mapped pixel sprites already matches the best-documented precedent for this niche: Dead Cells. The leading techniques from 2020–2026 do not suggest replacing it. They suggest changing **what** gets baked and **where** the motion comes from. For shapes, shipped games get variety from **a handful of skeleton templates with sockets, parameter ranges and curated part pools** (No Man's Sky's "blueprints", Spore's rigblocks). Smooth-blended SDF bodies and palette-swap masks add an organic look and cheap colour variants. For animation, the cheapest approach that holds up for any body plan is the 2024 "follow-the-leader" distance chain for spine and tail, plus analytic two-bone IK legs stepping on a per-leg phase schedule. That approach can be driven directly by the movement system's velocity, acceleration and turning. Learned controllers (motion matching, PFNN and similar) cost about a millisecond per character on desktop, which rules them out for hundreds of creatures on phones. For trees and plants, **Weber–Penn-style per-level parameters work as a compact "species genome"**. Crowns built as a few lit blobs with leaf-clump stamps on top, plus whole-pixel wind offsets, read as pixel art without shimmer. The recommendation is: describe each creature and plant as data, bake parts (not whole frames) into texture atlases at load time on the GPU, and assemble and animate them each frame as a light 2D rig driven by movement. Keep a cheap pre-baked frame cycle for swarms and tiny creatures. Many primary sources (Spore, No Man's Sky, Dead Cells, t3ssel8r, Crysis) were blocked during research and are known here only through search-result summaries. Those are flagged below, and nothing in this report has been benchmarked on a phone.

## Few skeletons, many parts: how shipped games make creature variety

The clearest lesson from shipped procedural creatures is that skeleton count stays small and variety comes from proportions and parts. No Man's Sky art director Grant Duncan said "it's quite surprising how few different types of skeletons there are". The team built its creatures on artist-made templates it called "blueprints", then **scaled individual bones, swapped heads, shells and wings from a large pool, and recoloured skin by biome** ([Game Informer, 2014](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2014/12/24/a-look-at-no-mans-skys-creature-features.aspx), via search summary; [NMS Archive wiki](https://nomanssky-archive.fandom.com/wiki/Procedural_generation)). The wiki calls this "procedural distortion of archetypes", with animations that adapt to the rig at any scale. Spore went further for its player-built creatures. A metaball torso gave the organic core, and **"rigblocks" (hand-modelled parts with parameterised deformation handles) snapped onto it** ([Jane Ng portfolio](http://janeng.com/portfolio2/spore/creature_rigblocks.htm); [Chris Hecker, liner notes](https://chrishecker.com/My_Liner_Notes_for_Spore), via search summary). Dwarf Fortress's procedural megabeasts show a third, simpler recipe: **base animal, plus added or removed features, plus material, plus colour**. This maps directly onto fantasy variants such as a "crystal stag" ([DF Wiki: Forgotten beast](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:Forgotten_beast)).

For Witch this means the roughly 80 creature kinds should not be 80 generators. They should be **5–8 body templates** (quadruped, biped, serpent or worm, bird or flyer, insectoid or many-legged, blob or slime, perhaps floater). Each species is a template plus parameter ranges plus part choices. Colour and shape variants are small perturbations inside those ranges. Full grammars (L-systems, shape grammars) are well established for evolved research creatures ([Lai et al., CGF 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.142661)). A 2024 survey notes, though, that grammars are hard to author ([arXiv 2410.15644](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15644)), and the research found no shipped game that uses one for whole creature bodies. A shallow "part graph" is the practical version: body, then sockets, then parts, where a head can expose its own sockets for horns, ears and eyes. L-systems belong only in branching sub-parts. Antlers whose branching depth grows with size level would give babies nubs and legends full racks, which is a natural visual progression.

The organic look is the second shape question. Witch's primitives currently stack, and SDF modelling's "smooth minimum" operator blends nearby shapes "as if they were made of clay". Inigo Quilez's reference article on it was rewritten in March 2024 ([iquilezles.org/articles/smin](https://iquilezles.org/articles/smin/)), and his "Happy Jumping" creature is a fully procedural animated SDF animal with public source ([Shadertoy](https://shadertoy.com/view/3lsSzf)). Dreams and Claybook ship entire SDF worlds ([Claybook GDC 2018 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/Aaltonen_Sebastian_GPU_Based_Clay.pdf)), but Witch does not need real-time SDF. Because it bakes, it can **ray-march a smooth-blended body once per sprite and read normals straight from the SDF gradient**, which feeds its existing normal-map lighting. Hard unions should stay on horns, antlers and wings, since blending blurs the silhouette at 30 px. Nobody has measured the cost of this bake on phones.

Readability at 30–110 px is a design constraint, not an afterthought. Shape-language guidance says round reads as harmless, square as sturdy and triangle as dangerous, and that a good silhouette stays recognisable in full shadow ([RocketBrush](``https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent)).`` Caves of Qud builds its sprites from **monochrome layers recoloured per piece at runtime** (via search summary of a [Steam modding guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1455732142)). That pattern carries over to Witch: bake a material-ID mask next to albedo and normals, and colour variants become a palette swap in the shader with no new art. Free hue noise muddies pixel clusters, so variants should use curated 3–5-shade ramps per material. No Man's Sky's mocked odd combinations are a warning against free part-swapping. Witch should instead tag parts (`horn.curved`, `wing.feathered`), allow tags per template, add exclusion rules, and give each family a style key (fae are round with leaf-wings; undead are angular with exposed bone). One more inference is worth building: an **automatic silhouette test after each bake**. It shrinks each variant's alpha mask to about 24 px and compares it with other species, rejecting variants that look too much like another species or drift too far from their own.

Machine-learning 3D generators are real but a poor fit for this role. TRELLIS.2 (Microsoft, 2025, MIT licence) turns an image into a textured GLB in about 3–17 s on an H100 and needs a 24 GB GPU ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Hunyuan3D 2.1 is similar, but secondary sources report that its licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 repo](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1); [triposr.org](https://triposr.org/blog/hunyuan3d-vs-trellis)), which should be checked against the repo's LICENSE file. Both produce dense meshes with no sockets, rig or size parameters. Pixel-art diffusion tools such as Retro Diffusion make true low-resolution pixel art with reference-image consistency ([Runware blog](https://runware.ai/blog/retro-diffusion-creating-authentic-pixel-art-with-ai-at-scale)), but normal maps, three-quarter views and consistency across 80 species × 4 sizes remain the weak points. Their place is concept art and silhouette reference, not the generator.

## Chains, phases and springs animate any body for a few hundred flops

The 2024 open-source "animal-proc-anim" project by argonaut animates a fish, a snake and a lizard with nothing but **a 2D chain of distance constraints, where each segment follows the one in front** ([GitHub, MIT](https://github.com/argonautcode/animal-proc-anim)). It already has JS and TypeScript ports, including one that adds FABRIK legs with angle limits ([OnlyShoky/Procedural-Animation](https://github.com/OnlyShoky/Procedural-Animation)). Add legs as **analytic two-bone IK**, which is a closed-form law-of-cosines solve with no iteration and cheaper than FABRIK or CCD for two-bone limbs ([Aristidou & Lasenby 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1524070311000178)). Each foot stays planted until it drifts too far, then steps inside its phase window. Gait for any leg count needs only two numbers. The **duty factor** is the share of the cycle a foot is on the ground: above 0.5 is a walk, below 0.5 a run. The **per-leg phase offset** sets the stepping order ([UCSD CSE169 slides](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/classes/sp16/cse169-a/slides/CSE169_13.pdf), via search summary). Offsets of 0 and 0.5 give a trot, alternating tripods give an insect, and `i/N` gives a millipede wave. Tails, ears and antennae become a few verlet particles with one constraint pass, Thomas Jakobsen's 2001 Hitman method, which he called "simple, fast, and stable" ([Jakobsen PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/academic/class/15462-s13/www/lec_slides/Jakobsen.pdf)).

The direct precedent for animating creatures nobody has designed yet is Spore. Its SIGGRAPH 2008 paper records animator-made motion "in a morphology-independent form" and applies it at runtime to each body with an IK solver ([Hecker et al.](https://www.chrishecker.com/Real-time_Motion_Retargeting_to_Highly_Varied_User-Created_Morphologies), abstract via search summary). Witch can borrow the idea cheaply. Author a few **semantic moves** (lunge, leap arc, recoil, burrow dive) as curves on abstract channels such as body pitch, body height, front-reach target, tail lift and spine compression. Then map those channels onto each body plan's IK targets when the creature spawns. Rain World's GDC 2016 talk shows the same "art plus code" philosophy for squishy, stretchy creatures driven by AI ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023475/Animation-Bootcamp-Rainworld-Animation)). Its model is a few physics circles per creature with cosmetic limbs attached, which suits a crowd game. That reading comes from the talk's framing, not a transcript. David Rosen's GDC 2014 talk makes the indie case that very few keyframes plus procedural blending give fluid motion ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)).

Learned approaches fail on cost and on data. PFNN needs **0.8–1.8 ms per character on a desktop CPU** ([Holden et al. 2017](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/35467734/phasefunction.pdf)), so 300 creatures would take 240–540 ms per frame, about 15–30 times a 16 ms budget before any phone slowdown. MANN, motion matching and DeepMimic all assume a fixed skeleton with motion-capture data ([MANN, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3197517.3201366); [DeepMimic](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2018-TOG-deepMimic/index.html)), which generated creatures cannot have. Active ragdolls in the Gang Beasts style need a jointed rigid-body solver per limb, so they suit one boss or a death flop, not a swarm. The ideas are still worth taking: PFNN's single phase value drives everything, MANN blends gait parameters smoothly with speed, and Daniel Holden's spring-based inertialization smooths the snap between charge, leap and land ([theorangeduck](https://theorangeduck.com/page/propagating-velocities-through-animation-systems)).

The research's own estimate (not a measurement) puts a 10-segment, 6-legged creature with a 4-particle tail at **under about 600 flops per frame, or about 0.3 MFLOP for 500 creatures**, well under a millisecond in a modern JS engine. The real enemy is garbage collection, so joint data should live in flat `Float32Array` pools. At pixel scale, motion reads through 1–3 px silhouette changes, a 2-frame anticipation crouch and one-frame smears. Twelve in-between frames on a tiny sprite look identical ([sprite-ai.art guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/animation-principles), a vendor guide). Procedural squash, stretch and afterimages supply this without new art. Chain lag gives momentum for free: a charge stretches the body, and a hard turn whips the tail.

## Bake once on the GPU, assemble live, and respect iOS memory

Dead Cells is Witch's closest relative. A homebrew tool **rendered each 3D frame tiny and without antialiasing, saved a normal map beside it, and lit it with a toon shader**, so one artist could ship fluid pixel animation without redrawing ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-), via search extract; [GameAnim](https://www.gameanim.com/2018/01/31/dead-cells-3d-pipeline-2d-animation/)). Guilty Gear Xrd adds that "2D feel" comes from held poses at about 15 fps and art-directed shading, not physical accuracy ([Game Developer, GDC 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/see-i-guilty-gear-xrd-i-s-striking-2d-3d-art-deconstructed-at-gdc-2015)). The alternative is t3ssel8r's live approach: render the 3D scene into a low-resolution target, draw 1-px outlines at that resolution, snap the camera to the texel grid and feed the leftover snap error into the upscale ([David Holland article](https://www.davidhol.land/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering/), via search extract). Witch's fixed camera suits that recipe, because snapping only has to handle translation. Even so, the notes record that **shimmer is solvable only for orthographic translation**, and a creature turning smoothly still crawls unless its facing is quantised ([Texel Splatting, arXiv 2603.14587](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14587v1)).

| | Pre-baked whole frames | Live 3D to low-res target | **Baked parts + live 2D rig (recommended)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draw cost for hundreds | One instanced quad batch | Skinning wall at ~300–500 units on desktop three.js | One instanced quad batch (more quads per creature) |
| Memory | Grows with directions × frames × variants | Small textures, but meshes and bones | Grows with directions × parts, not frames |
| Reacts to velocity and turning | Only by picking frames | Fully | Fully, in 2D |
| Pixel stability | Perfect | Needs snapping; turning crawls | Perfect if parts snap to whole pixels |
| Phone risk | Atlas memory | CPU and fragment cost, unmeasured | Atlas memory plus JS rig cost, unmeasured |

The live-3D column's warning comes from a three.js developer with up to 500 units. The bottleneck was CPU work (AnimationMixer, matrix updates), not the GPU ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/optimization-of-large-amounts-100-1000-of-skinned-meshes-cpu-bottlenecks/58196)), and core InstancedMesh cannot do skinning ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/instancedmesh-animations-like-with-skinnedmesh/12388)). Babylon's baked vertex-animation textures report thousands of animated instances, but on an unnamed device ([Babylon blog](https://babylonjs.medium.com/creating-thousands-of-animated-entities-in-babylon-js-ce3c439bdacf)). The phone constraint that bites is memory. iOS Safari commonly caps textures at **4096 px**, and iOS has logged a **224 MB total canvas memory** limit ([WebGL Fundamentals](https://webglfundamentals.org/webgl/lessons/webgl-cross-platform-issues.html); [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/112218)). Pixel art cannot use lossy GPU compression, since ASTC and ETC smear single pixels. The research's own estimate is that a 32 px creature with 8 directions × 8 frames of albedo and normal costs about 0.5 MB. Eighty species × several variants × four sizes of whole frames therefore gets heavy fast, which is a strong argument for baking parts and caching only species that are on screen.

Baking at load is feasible in the browser if pixels never leave the GPU. Render into a framebuffer that *is* the atlas page, spread across idle frames, and key the cache on (genome hash, style version). Worker OffscreenCanvas with WebGL only arrived with Safari 17. Handing results back via `transferToImageBitmap` is fast on Chrome but **"significantly slower" on Firefox and Safari, because it reads back every pixel** ([Flutter issue #145420](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/145420); [WebKit bug 183720](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=183720)). Workers should therefore do CPU jobs (SDF meshing, pose solving), not the GL bake. The research found **no published case study** of a browser game baking procedural sprite atlases at runtime, so Witch would be early here.

## Trees grow from parameters, crowns read as blobs, wind moves whole pixels

Vegetation has a much deeper procedural literature than creatures. Weber and Penn's 1995 per-branch-level parameters ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/218380.218427)) underpin Blender's Sapling and the browser-native **ez-tree** (MIT, 15 presets, more than 50 parameters, wind, LOD, GLB and PNG export) ([GitHub](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/04/ez-tree-is-a-lightweight-free-open-source-tree-generator/)). The parameter vector is compact enough that TreeSketchNet trains a network to output it from sketches ([ACM, 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3579831)), which supports treating it as a species genome. Space colonization (Runions et al. 2007) fills a chosen crown envelope with attraction points and grows branches toward them, using three knobs: attraction distance, kill distance and segment length ([paper](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.large.pdf), via search summary; [Jason Webb JS](https://github.com/jasonwebb/2d-space-colonization-experiments)). That makes it the tool for **signature silhouettes**: umbrella, weeping, columnar, spiral or hollow-ring crowns. L-systems (*The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants*, free online, [PDF](https://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/abop/abop.pdf); JS library [lindenmayer](https://github.com/nylki/lindenmayer)) suit ferns, flower spikes, vines and coral fungi, where fractal regularity is the point. Recent ML work, including a SIGGRAPH Asia 2025 transformer that generates growing trees ([arXiv 2502.04762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04762)), confirms skeletons and parameters as the right abstraction but cannot run in a phone browser.

The pixel-art question is how to stop a generated crown looking like noise. t3ssel8r's answer is **jagged sphere blobs that carry shadow and normals, with leaf sprites scattered over them and lit by the blob's normal rather than their own**. He found random placement too messy compared with hand-drawn trees and moved toward blue-noise spacing ([gamesinprogress](https://www.gamesinprogress.com/indie-game-developers/t3ssel8r/procedural-pixel-art-trees), via search summary). The stylised-3D "fluffy tree" trick of transferring sphere normals onto leaf cards is the same idea ([Habrador](https://blog.habrador.com/2018/05/how-to-make-stylized-witness-trees-in.html)). For Witch, the blob is a useful unit three ways: one lighting volume, one wind body, and one fade unit when the crown hides around the player. Witch already splits crown from trunk, and blobs refine that split.

Wind follows the Crysis model: **main bending of the whole plant plus detail bending per leaf**, driven by per-vertex weights ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis), via search summary). CryEngine documents the channel packing: red for edge stiffness, green for phase offset, blue for overall stiffness ([CRYENGINE docs](https://docs.cryengine.com/display/SDKDOC2/Detail+Bending)). SpeedTree does the same in vertex shaders with no bones, and its wind level of detail steps from full to branch-only to global ([SpeedTree docs](https://docs.speedtree.com/doku.php?id=wind_overview)). The pixel-art twist is to move **whole pixels, never fractions**. Pixel Wind (MIT, TypeScript, 2026) shifts leaf-clump-sized regions by integer offsets of at most 1–3 px and samples the source so no new colours appear ([GitHub](https://github.com/getcakedieyoungx/pixel-wind)). Sea of Stars reviewers single out subtle plant motion as what makes its world feel alive ([Noisy Pixel](https://noisypixel.net/sea-of-stars-review/)). For grass that reacts to creatures, pass the nearest creature positions to the shader as a uniform array. On low-end Android, one developer found instanced vegetation was vertex-bound and cut density and wind there ([keaukraine](https://keaukraine.medium.com/efficient-webgl-vegetation-rendering-b09a7fa904cc), via search summary).

Keeping about 80 areas distinct comes down to data layering. Starbound, the best-documented shipped pixel example, pairs a stem type with a foliage type plus a hue shift, and admits some pairs clash ([Starbounder wiki](https://starbounder.org/Trees)). SpeedTree builds ages and seasons from one species setup (via search summary). For Witch, that means a species genome for structure plus an area layer for palette, density, scale ranges and the allowed list of 3–6 species. Fantasy flora is modifiers on a real species (emissive tips, crystal leaf stamps, spiral phyllotaxis). Seasons are a palette ramp swap plus a leaf-density multiplier.

## What Witch should adopt

### The rendering choice: baked parts, live 2D rig, baked-frame fallback

Witch should keep baking, but bake **parts per facing direction** instead of whole walk frames. The parts are body or spine segments, head, tail pieces, wings in two or three flap poses, and short leg segments. Each is baked as albedo, normal and material ID at 8 facing directions. Mirroring can cut this to 5, provided the normal map's X channel is flipped. The current "turned away" variant becomes simply the back-facing directions. At runtime, a typed-array rig simulates the spine chain, legs and tail in the ground plane and projects joint positions through the fixed 35°/30° view. Each part is drawn as an instanced quad snapped to whole pixels and sorted by projected depth, so far legs sit behind the body. Facing is quantised to the 8 directions, which avoids the turning crawl. Squash, stretch and lean are per-quad transforms, also pixel-snapped. Thin legs can be 1–2 px lines between IK joints in the palette's dark tone.

For swarms, creatures under about 8 px in projected leg length, and the far LOD tier, Witch should bake a **4–8-frame cycle per direction** and pick the frame from the gait phase, so the swarm shares one phase clock with a random offset per creature. "Legend" bosses could later get extra baked poses or even live SDF.

All baking happens at load or on first sight, on the main GL context, into atlas pages capped at 4096², cached by genome hash plus style version and evicted LRU. The projection of IK into the three-quarter view and the part-sorting scheme are the research's inference, not sourced practice, and the whole option needs a prototype measured on a mid-range phone.

### Creature description schema

```ts
// One record per species; variants and size levels are derived, not authored.
interface CreatureSpecies {
  id: string;                         // "crystal_stag"
  family: string;                     // "fae" → style key: shape language, palette family, allowed parts
  template: "quadruped" | "biped" | "serpent" | "avian" | "insectoid" | "blob" | "floater";
  shapeLanguage: "round" | "square" | "triangle";
  body: {
    spine: { segments: number; length: Range; radius: Curve };   // radius profile head→tail
    blend: number;                     // SDF smooth-union radius (0 = hard primitives)
    neck?: { length: Range; radius: Range };
    tail?: { segments: number; length: Range; taper: number; stiffness: number };
  };
  legs?: { pairs: number; attachAt: number[]; upper: Range; lower: Range; thickness: Range };
  sockets: Record<"head" | "horns" | "ears" | "back" | "wings" | "tailTip", PartChoice[]>;
  // PartChoice = { tag: "antler.branching", weight, params: {...}, mirror: boolean }
  exclude?: string[][];               // e.g. [["horn.*","antler.*"]]
  materials: Record<"skin" | "belly" | "horn" | "accent" | "glow", PaletteRampId>;
  pattern?: { kind: "spots" | "stripes" | "mask"; seedRange: Range };
  sizeLevels: {                        // proportion curves, not uniform scale
    baby:   { scale: number; headRatio: number; legRatio: number; partDepth: number };
    young:  { ... }; adult: { ... };
    legend: { scale: number; extraParts: string[]; paletteShift: PaletteRampId };
  };
  locomotion: {
    gait: { offsets: number[]; dutyWalk: number; dutyRun: number; strideLength: number };
    maxBendDeg: number; lean: number; stretch: number; mass: number;
    moves: SemanticMoveId[];           // "charge", "leap", "burrow", "coil-strike"...
  };
  variants: { id: string; paletteOverrides?: {...}; paramNudges?: {...}; rarity: number }[];
}
```

The designer authors families, templates and species once. Variants are nudges and palette swaps inside the ranges. A seeded RNG turns (species, variant, size, individual seed) into a concrete creature, and that tuple's hash becomes the bake cache key.

### Plant species genome schema

```ts
interface PlantSpecies {
  id: string;
  form: "tree" | "shrub" | "fern" | "flower" | "fungus" | "vine" | "grass";
  generator: "weberPenn" | "spaceColonization" | "lsystem" | "profileCap";
  trunk: { height: Range; radius: Range; taper: number; lean: Range; gnarl: number };
  levels: {                           // Weber–Penn style, ez-tree shaped
    children: Range; angle: Range; length: Range; taper: number; twist: number; gnarl: number;
  }[];
  crown?: { envelope: "sphere" | "cone" | "umbrella" | "weeping" | "column" | "spiral" | "ring";
            blobs: Range; attractors?: number; killDist?: number };
  lsystem?: { axiom: string; rules: Record<string, string>; iterations: Range };
  leaves: { stampSet: string; density: number; spacing: "blueNoise" };  // 5–12 px clump stamps
  materials: Record<"bark" | "leaf" | "flower" | "glow", PaletteRampId>;
  wind: { mainAmp: number; detailAmp: number; stiffness: number; maxPx: 1 | 2 | 3 };
  ages: { sapling: number; mature: number; ancient: number };  // growth-stage scale + level cap
  modifiers?: ("emissiveTips" | "crystalLeaves" | "invertedGravitropism" | "spiralPhyllotaxis")[];
}
interface AreaFlora {
  areaId: string; species: { id: string; weight: number }[];   // 3–6 per area
  paletteShift: PaletteLUT; density: number; scale: Range; season?: "spring" | "summer" | "autumn" | "winter";
}
```

Each bake writes trunk and crown blobs as separate sub-rectangles, with per-blob phase and stiffness. That gives Crysis-style wind in one vertex shader, offsets rounded to whole pixels, per-blob fades for hiding the crown near the player, and a damped "shake" impulse when a charging creature hits a tree.

### Animation driven by movement

The movement system already knows velocity, acceleration and turn rate, so animation should read them, not have them hand-authored per species. The table is the research's synthesis of the sourced techniques above, not a published system.

| Movement signal | Animation response |
|---|---|
| Speed | Gait frequency rises; duty factor slides from walk to run value; stride length scales with leg length |
| Forward acceleration | Body pitches into the motion and stretches along velocity (area-preserving, pixel-snapped); braking squashes and plants feet |
| Turn rate | Spine chain bends up to `maxBendDeg`; body leans into the turn; tail whips via chain lag |
| Charge wind-up | 2–4 frame anticipation: pull back 1–2 px and crouch, then afterimages or a dither streak during the dash |
| Leap | Ground shadow stays down while sprite rises; stretch on take-off, squash and dust puff on landing; legs tuck via the "leap" semantic curve |
| Burrow | Sink through a dirt mask, spine segments disappear in order, dirt mound tracks the head |
| Pack and swarm | Shared phase clock with per-creature offset; far LOD switches to baked cycle frames at 20–30 Hz |
| State changes | Critically damped springs (inertialization) blend semantic channels between moves |

### Main risks

The largest risk is **readability**. Procedural legs and blended bodies have to read at 30 px, and no source studies silhouette clarity at that scale, so the silhouette test and designer review gates are essential. Second is **phone performance and memory**. Every per-frame cost here is an estimate, iOS memory limits come from forum reports rather than Apple documentation, and load-time bake speed on phones is unmeasured. Stage 4 and stage 5 must be measured on real devices before the next stage begins. Third is **"part soup"**. The No Man's Sky reaction shows that unconstrained combination produces creatures that look random, and only tag rules and family style keys prevent it. Fourth, the **three-quarter projection of ground-plane IK** and the part-depth sorting are untested inferences, so expect fiddly edge cases with legs crossing the body in back-facing views. Fifth, **evidence quality**: the Spore, No Man's Sky, Dead Cells, t3ssel8r and Crysis details come from search summaries because those pages were blocked. They should be read in full before anyone quotes exact numbers. Last, if ML tools are used for concepts, **licences matter**: TRELLIS.2 is MIT, while Hunyuan3D's reported territory exclusion needs checking.

## Conclusion

The research shows Witch's niche is real but sparsely documented. No shipped game has published a pipeline that generates many creatures from 3D rigs and renders them as pixel art, and none bakes procedural sprite atlases in the browser. The ingredients exist separately: templates and part pools (No Man's Sky, Spore), cheap chains and IK (argonaut, Jakobsen), offline 3D-to-pixel baking (Dead Cells), and blob crowns with whole-pixel wind (t3ssel8r, Pixel Wind). Witch's advantage is that its fixed camera and pixel scale make each of these cheaper than in the original games.

The important change is moving motion out of the art. Today, 2 walk frames carry all of a creature's movement. Under this plan the movement system that drives fights also drives the bodies: a charging pack stretches, leans and whips its tails because of the numbers that move it. The designer gets variety from data, and fights look as dynamic as they play. The first prototypes to build are the serpent rig and a device memory test, since they are the cheapest way to show whether the plan holds on a phone.
