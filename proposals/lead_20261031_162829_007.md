## 2. Highlight: the bbox

The `bbox` column, `Struct({xmin, ymin, zmin, xmax, ymax, zmax})` in the BIM CRS, gives questions about where elements are a one-line answer, with no geometry library. The book should show that in its own section of the BimDataFrame chapter, with this example.

### Elements below ground level

In bedrock-engineer/bedrock-experiments (`speckle/rvt_advanced_sample_project_to_r2.py`, branch `dev`), the Revit Advanced Sample structure model is georeferenced in Amsterdam Noord (EPSG:7415, site origin at RD 122096, 488756 and NAP +3.30 m, rotation -160°). z = 0 of that CRS is the site origin's height, which is where `specklewidgets.geo.osm_ground_map` draws the ground map. The elements below ground level are then one filter:

```python
below_ground = str_bdf.with_frame(
    str_bdf.frame.filter(pl.col("bbox").struct.field("zmax") <= 0)
)
```

That isolates 304 of the 939 elements: 294 Structural Foundations (piles and pile caps, down to -7.2 m), 7 short Structural Columns between the pile caps, 2 Structural Rebar, and the ground floor slab (-300 mm to 0). Shown in a `GeoSpeckleViewer` with the ground map at `opacity: 0.35`, the foundations are visible through the map with the 3D BAG around them.

Points the section should make:

- `zmax <= 0` keeps elements entirely below ground. The pile caps end exactly at 0, so a strict `< 0` would lose them; the same rule lets the ground floor slab in, whose top is at ground level. `zmin < 0` instead gives everything that reaches below ground, columns from the pile caps up included.
- z = 0 is ground level here only because the site origin was placed at ground level. In general, compare against the ground level in the BIM CRS.
- The filter runs on the BIM CRS, so it needs no transformation and works on a model that isn't georeferenced yet, as long as the height of the ground in the model's own coordinates is known.
- The same column is the GeoParquet covering that `write_parquet` declares, so a GeoParquet reader skips row groups with it, and `specklewidgets.geo` takes its plan extent from it.

Other short bbox examples worth a line each: the elements within a plan rectangle (`xmin`/`xmax`/`ymin`/`ymax`), elements by storey height without the `container`, and the model's extent (`min` of the mins, `max` of the maxes).
