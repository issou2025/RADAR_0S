**The rule, wall by wall** (the five Revit 2024 files with Revit's export: Core Interior, the office as built and at permit, the two terrain files). A wall's loop is its sketch lines in its vertical plane; its pockets are the stretches of the loop between two hull corners that the hull edge cuts off; the opening's Tag is the highest line id of its stretch. A pocket is closed by the hull edge (1387731: four corners) or, where the edge's ends stand on two adjacent sides of the loop's bounding rectangle and neither at a corner, through the rectangle's corner between them (55840: nine points, 301.65 ft2, where the stretch and the edge hold 71.23).

It predicts none on the other 15, and none of Revit's openings of no cutter is on one of them.

**What the first build got wrong (2026-11-09).** I wrote the first #1608 commit with every pocket closed by its hull edge, because the scorer's corners and boxes were the same for 55840 either way: they are sets of distinct coordinates and a bounding box, and the stretch alone and the stretch with the rectangle's corner have the same ones. The test of #227 (`tests/wall_profile_openings.rs`) reads the opening's area, and failed in the first CI run: 71.233 ft2 against Revit's 301.649. Revit's opening is nine points, the eight corners and (29.917, 76.0) in the profile's own along-and-up feet. The terrain wall's opening has no fifth corner. The rule is now the closure above; it is read from those two walls, and a pocket whose hull edge ends otherwise (at a corner of the rectangle with the other end on a side it does not share) is not written. `unfilled_openings_vs_ifc` now compares the volume of each opening's mesh, which tells the two closures apart.

**Why it is Revit's rule** is not read from its source: the exporter's `AddClippingsToBaseExtrusion` and the extrusion calculator it uses are in `RevitAPIIFC`, closed. The open-source `WallExporter.cs` (`TryToCreateAsExtrusion`, `ExportWall`) shows the shape of it: the wall is a footprint extrusion with clippings, and the openings that call returns ("cut pairs") are written by `OpeningUtil.CreateOpeningsIfNecessary`. Read that way, the hull's sloped edges are the clip planes (they run on to the wall's end, which is what #1473 sees as Revit keeping the slope past the profile's end) and a pocket is what no plane can cut. That is a reading of ten measured walls.

**What it asks of rvt-rs.** An opening per pocket equals Revit's on every wall above. But the box less the pockets is not the profile where the hull has a sloped edge: today the regions between the hull and the rectangle are written as openings too, and those are the ten openings Revit does not write (the office's eight and the terrain's two, the last with the pocket merged in). The writer has `IfcBooleanResult` and no half-space clipping, and Revit writes the body of all 11 exported profile walls as a `Tessellation` (`IfcPolygonalFaceSet`, the final shape) with the pocket's opening beside it where it has one, so no half-space is needed to match it. rvt-rs writes the terrain walls and 55840 as a plain box extrusion with the openings left to the reader, and the office's eight as faceted breps (`IfcFacetedBrep`, two of them in three pieces and two with extruded pieces besides), each also carrying the extra opening; whether those breps have the opening cut out of them was not read. A cut at the hull's edges also reaches #1604's `take_top_at_profile` (the highest point of the hull is the top) and RE-358's `extend_profile_stubs` (#1473), so it is one design for all three, gated on the walls' body volume and top across the 1,560 walls of the five files; only two of them carry a Revit opening.

**Not measured:** the order rule of the Tag (highest id of the pocket, or last of its lines in the sketch's order: the sketch lists were ascending by id on 55840, 34362 and 1387731, so the two coincide); a pocket on a wall outside Revit 2024; arcs in a profile (RE-151 leaves a sketch with an arc alone).

**Done when:** rvt-rs writes the profile openings Revit writes, with Revit's Tag, host and box within 0.01 ft, on the five Revit 2024 files and none Revit does not, by pockets against the convex hull, with the hull's sloped edges cutting the body in a way that is not an opening, and the walls' tops, heights and volumes still Revit's on all 1,560; or a measured negative says where the pocket rule fails.
