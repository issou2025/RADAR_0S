## Reported finding

The body of `Global/ElemTable` is reportedly an **ownership tree**, with a versioned record layout:

- Revit 2014: **28-byte records**, with the owner encoded as the trailing `u32`.
- Revit 2026: **40-byte records**, with ownership represented as `u64`.

The report notes that current rvt-rs documentation describes this body as partial.

## Reported evidence

The layouts were reportedly observed during clean-room analysis of a Revit 2014 manufacturer family and a Revit 2026 project. Discussion #112 does not include the raw offsets, sample records, endianness proof, owner/root sentinel semantics, or a full field map; those details must be derived or obtained and independently reproduced before implementation.

## Why it matters

An ownership tree could provide structural relationships that are more reliable than heuristic partition scans. If confirmed, it may improve element hierarchy recovery, parent/child traversal, version-aware `ElemTable` parsing, and the interpretation of the existing ElementId-to-offset index.

This is related to, but not a duplicate of, closed issue [#25](https://github.com/DrunkOnJava/rvt-rs/issues/25). #25 delivered ElementId-to-partition-offset linkage; this issue is specifically about independently validating and representing the reported **ownership semantics and versioned body records**.

## Independent verification still required

- Locate record boundaries and reproduce the exact 28-byte and 40-byte strides.
- Verify that the trailing values are owners rather than correlated identifiers.
- Determine endianness, root/no-owner representation, duplicate-owner behavior, and whether every record participates in one tree.
- Test releases between 2014 and 2026 to identify the actual format transition instead of assuming only two layouts.
- Compare ownership edges against independent model or stream evidence.

The hypothesis would be falsified or narrowed if the trailing values do not form stable ownership relationships or if record stride/field width varies by factors other than Revit release.

## Repository-side acceptance criteria

- [ ] Add a probe that prints record offsets, stride, candidate element ID, candidate owner, and validation diagnostics without hardcoded private paths.
- [ ] Reproduce or refute the reported 2014 and 2026 layouts on provenance-safe fixtures.
- [ ] Establish the version gate from corpus evidence rather than extrapolating from two releases.
- [ ] Validate tree invariants, including roots, missing owners, cycles, duplicate IDs, and out-of-range references.
- [ ] If confirmed, expose a version-aware ownership representation without regressing the existing ElementId-to-partition-offset index.
- [ ] Add synthetic byte-pattern tests for both widths plus malformed/cyclic cases.
- [ ] Add a dated, confidence-scored reconnaissance addendum and update documentation that currently calls the body partial.

Evidence and fixtures should follow the repository's [reverse-engineering contribution requirements](https://github.com/DrunkOnJava/rvt-rs/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md#reverse-engineering-findings).
