# Project Proposal — Quantity Takeoff

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Blinds: position isn't persisted and 'open' near the top runs at full speed, so a blind can be driven into the head rail**

*Project description:*
## Symptom

Every so often a blind that is already up gets driven up again. The servo stalls, its current draw pulls down the shared supply so all blinds drop out for a moment, and the blind doesn't recover until someone resets it by hand. Things can run fine for a long time before this happens.

## Hardware facts

- The servos and all three controllers run from **one power supply**. The HA open/close routines move the blinds one at a time to avoid overcurrent.
- The end sensors are **small reed switches**, triggered by a small magnet in the blind. The active zone is only a few millimetres. Depending on orientation, a magnet sliding past a reed can also close it in more than one spot.
- After a lock-up, the blinds usually work again once the power is cycled.

## How the blinds decide where they are today

- `Blinds.__init__` starts with `_position = POSITION_DOWN` ([blinds.py L244](https://github.com/heikkileivo/circuitpython-ha-blinds/blob/cd8c9cc/devices/blinds/blinds.py#L243-L260)).
- At boot, [`find_out_current_state()`](https://github.com/heikkileivo/circuitpython-ha-blinds/blob/cd8c9cc/devices/blinds/blinds.py#L459-L489) reads the two limit sensors. If neither is active it prints `Position is unknown...` but **leaves `_position` at DOWN**. Nothing is persisted: `store_position()` is never called, and the NVM restore is disabled with `if False:`. `_direction_unknown` and `target` are set but never used.
- The state it works out is never published at boot. `report_state` runs before MQTT exists (`on_connected` is still `None`), and the resulting `AttributeError` is swallowed, so HA keeps showing whatever it saw last.
- `open()` and `close()` never look at `_position`. The only guard is a one-time sensor read in `operate()` (`Already at stopped state.`). `open()` always ends in `POSITION_UP`, whatever actually happened.

## Why "open" can hit the head rail

1. **The only thing that says "already up" is the up-sensor at that moment.** If the blind rests just below the sensor, `open` drives it up. With a reed switch and a zone only a few millimetres long, **"overshot above the sensor" reads exactly the same as "just below it"**. A full move takes about 26 s for about 28 turns of a 20 mm spindle, so full speed is very roughly 60–80 mm/s. Half a second of stop lag therefore carries the magnet well past the zone. A blind that overshot above the sensor is sitting just under the head rail, and the next `open` drives it straight into the rail.
2. **This happens every few days to the Left blind.** In the HA history, when an open command arrives while a blind is already open, Right and Middle finish in 5–15 ms (the guard kicks in). Left takes **1.5–1.8 s**, meaning it drove up again: Sep 2, Sep 4, Sep 5, Sep 8, Sep 11. On Sep 8 it had fully opened at 06:59:32 and was already off the sensor 42 s later. It stops right at the edge of the sensor zone and settles out of it once torque is released: `enable_torque = False` runs after every move, and there is no holding torque at all overnight while the outlet is off.
3. **That nudge runs at full speed.** `Servo.start()` jumps straight to full speed (`ramp_speed_to()` exists but is unused). The slowdown to approach speed happens after `max_revs - approach_revs` revolutions **counted from the start of this move**, which assumes every move starts at the opposite end. From anywhere else (just below the top, after a STOP, after a reboot mid-travel), the blind reaches the end stop at full speed.
4. **Stopping in time depends on polling lag.** `poll_pin()` looks for a rising edge by sampling between servo reads. Each servo register read blocks for about 100–200 ms (`time.sleep(0.018)` per byte in `Reader.read_byte`), and `count_revolutions` reads constantly. Then `Servo.stop()` writes the stop and checks it, retrying up to 3 times. Usually the total lag is short enough. When it isn't (a UART retry, a missed sample), the blind overshoots into the head rail at full speed. That fits "works for a long time, then suddenly doesn't".
5. **Some failures leave the motor running with nothing watching it:**
   - If `stop()` can't confirm the stop, the torque-off is a single write that is never checked. If that write is lost, the motor keeps running ([L388-390](https://github.com/heikkileivo/circuitpython-ha-blinds/blob/cd8c9cc/devices/blinds/blinds.py#L387-L395)).
   - Any exception inside `operate()` goes through `except Exception` and then `finally`, and neither stops the motor. `ServoException` isn't even an `Exception` subclass; see #6.
   - After **any** controller reset (watchdog, brownout, or a power dip the servo rides through), boot never tells the servos to stop. `main()` only reads servo settings, so the servo keeps running its last speed command.
   - The stall check in `count_revolutions` needs 10 identical position readings, about 1.5–3 s of stall current. That is far too slow to protect a shared supply.
6. **Once it has happened, the next command does it again.** After the dip resets the controllers, the blind that hit the rail is above the sensor. Neither sensor is active, so `_position` becomes DOWN (wrong), and the next open drives up at full speed again. Add the brownout-to-safe-mode behaviour from #9, and only a power cycle breaks the loop.

The connectivity outages (#3) make this more likely. When the 21:00 close is missed, the blinds stay up overnight with no holding torque, and the morning open starts with a full-speed nudge.

## A shared-supply event in the history

On Sep 5, after the 21:00 close and tilt, all three controllers restarted **at the same moment, about 21:03:35**. Right and Left published `uptime=10` 11 ms apart, and Middle 2 s later. There was no outlet toggle and no HA or broker event, and the meters weren't affected.

**The controllers came back but the blinds didn't work.** From 21:04 to 21:18 all three were connected and publishing uptime every 10 s, yet they didn't respond to commands; no cover state changed at all. After the manual power cycle at 21:18:54 they worked again (tilt changes at 21:19, then the outlet was switched off at 21:20). The owner reports this is **the common form of the problem**: the blinds are powered but don't respond until the power is cycled, and afterwards they're fine.

What differs between the two restarts: the 21:03:35 dip reset only the **controllers**; the manual power cycle also reset the **servos**. That points at servo state that survives a controller reset. The firmware never re-initialises the servos at boot (see point 5), so whatever a servo was doing (running, stalled with torque on, or in its own overload protection) carries on after the controller restarts. This means a controller-only reset (watchdog, `safemode.py`, `microcontroller.reset()`) can't clear it.

A hypothesis to confirm with diagnostics: at 21:01 the 21:00 automation sent tilt 75 to **all three blinds at once**. The one-at-a-time protection in HA covers open/close only; the tilt steps in "Close blinds at 21:00", "Close blinds by sun azimuth", "Shut down blinds in the evening" and the tilt knob/number automations all target the three blinds together. `drive_tilt()` keeps torque on until the servo reports it has stopped moving, with no timeout (#6). A tilt servo that can't quite reach its target stalls at full torque indefinitely, and three of those at once load the shared supply.

## Proposed fix

**State**
- [ ] Persist to `microcontroller.nvm`: the last settled position (UP/DOWN/STOPPED), the revolution-based position estimate, and a "move in progress + direction" marker. Write the marker before a move starts and clear it after a confirmed stop. A few writes per move is negligible flash wear.
- [ ] At boot, **re-initialise both servos (lift and tilt) first**: speed 0, torque off, both confirmed. Then ping each servo and read its voltage, temperature and status. If a servo doesn't answer or reports an error, publish a fault so HA can cycle the outlet. The controller can't power-cycle the servos itself.
- [ ] After that, work out the state from the sensors plus NVM:
  - An active sensor always wins.
  - If neither sensor is active and NVM says "settled UP", treat it as UP (it just settled off the sensor).
  - If a move was in progress, report UNKNOWN. Never default to DOWN.
  - Publish the state once connected (see #7).

**Motion**
- [ ] Slow down based on the position estimate, not on revolutions since the move started. See #10 for a gradual speed profile.
- [ ] **Re-seat after every stop.** If the end sensor isn't active once the motor has stopped (it overshot the reed zone), crawl back at approach speed until it is, within a small budget. The blind then always rests with its sensor active, and the "already there" guard stays reliable.
- [ ] **Approach the top from below only.** When NVM says UP but the up-sensor is inactive, first crawl **down** (the safe direction) a bounded distance looking for the sensor. If it activates, the blind had overshot above: stop there. If it doesn't, the blind was below: crawl up at approach speed until the sensor activates. Never approach the head rail at more than approach speed from an unknown position.
- [ ] Ramp up to speed instead of jumping straight to full speed (`ramp_speed_to`). This also cuts the current spike at start on the shared supply.
- [ ] Read the limit sensors with `keypad.Keys` (imported in `code.py` but unused). It scans the pins in the background with debouncing, so an edge isn't missed while Python is blocked in a UART read. Check the level too, not only the rising edge.
- [ ] Detect stalls from `PRESENT_LOAD` / `PRESENT_CURRENT` (the addresses are already defined in `packet.py` but unused), and stop within about 100–200 ms.
- [ ] Always stop the motor and disable torque in `finally`, confirm the torque-off, and escalate if it can't be confirmed.
- [ ] For the Left blind: keep torque on after an open (the servo acts as a brake), or move the sensor so the blind doesn't settle off it.

**Diagnostics** (needed to confirm the Sep 5 hypothesis)
- [ ] Publish the servo supply voltage (`PRESENT_VOLTAGE`; the minimum seen during the last move and at idle), servo temperature, peak load/current during the last move, and a UART error count. Publish them as JSON attributes of **one** diagnostic sensor. The blinds' discovery payload is 1,892 bytes today, against the ~2.7 KB limit.

**HA side** (not in this repo)
- [ ] Stagger the tilt commands in the automations the same way open/close already is.

**Servo configuration / hardware** (not code)
- [ ] Set the servo's torque limit and overload protection so a stall can't pull the shared supply down. Consider more supply headroom, or bulk capacitance at each controller.

## How to verify

- Nudge test: with a blind up, pull it about 1 cm below the sensor by hand and send OPEN. It should re-seat at approach speed and stop without touching the head rail.
- Reset a controller mid-travel. The servo should stop right away at boot, HA should show `unknown` (or the correct state), and the next command must not run into an end stop at full speed.
