## What happens now

A family is parameters, types and forms in its own axes (`family.rs`). A component places a type at one point, with an angle, an offset and a level (`Model::component_pieces`), and its shape changes only through its parameters.

So one family can't:

- follow points the user clicks: a facade panel between four points of a grid, a brace between two nodes, a canopy over posts of different heights;
- be reshaped by dragging one of its corners;
- share its points with its neighbours, so that panels stay joined.

The workaround is a family or a mass for each panel.

## Proposal

Revit's Generic Model Adaptive workflow, in BIMCraft's own code. A family is reference points on a work plane, some made adaptive and numbered, lines through them and forms made from the lines. An instance is placed by clicking its adaptive points in order, and dragging one of them reshapes it.

### Data

- `Family.adaptive: Option<adaptive::Definition>`: nodes with persistent family-local ids (points, lines, polylines, cubic Beziers, patch, extrude, loft). A point is hosted on the world, on a numbered adaptive frame, on another point's frame or plane, on a curve (by parameter, normalised length or distance) or on a form's face, edge or vertex. Its offsets and rotations are numbers or formulas of the family's parameters.
- `Component.adaptive: Option<Placement>`: the frames of the instance's adaptive points, in its own axes.
- A frame is an origin and right-handed unit axes: `Fchild = Fhost * T(offset) * Rx * Ry * Rz`.
- **`FORMAT` 12**, with a step in `Model::migrate` (empty adaptive fields). An older build would drop the definition, which is a misreading, not a field it can do without.

### Geometry

Polygonal, in BIMCraft's own kernel: patch, extrude (a warped boundary stays warped, it is not flattened) and loft, with voids cut by the existing CSG. There are no NURBS.

Caps: 1,024 nodes, a dependency depth of 128, 4,096 vertices and 20,000 triangles per mesh, 400,000 triangles per evaluation. A failure is a diagnostic naming the node, its error and its dependency path, never a panic, and the independent results survive.

### Commands

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `adaptive_capture` | Takes the frames of existing adaptive instances (by id or GUID) as a placement in project coordinates. |
| `adaptive_place` | Places instances on given frames, up to 1,000 in one undo step, each held in its own axes. |
| `adaptive_move` | Moves one adaptive point of an instance. |
| `adaptive_evaluate` | Returns an instance's definition, placement, resolved parameters, meshes and diagnostics. |
| `family`, `component`, `modify` | Carry the definition and an instance's frames. |

### Desktop

- **Family Editor, Adaptive tab:** a 3D modelling view on a work plane, with Point, Line, Make Adaptive and Make Reference Point (numbered in the order selected, as Revit does), Create Solid Form and Create Void Form, Modify with a gizmo, Delete and Undo. Properties sits beside the view.
- **In a plan:** Component places an adaptive family by clicking its points in order, snapping to another instance's points. A selected instance shows numbered grips that drag its points.

### Beyond Revit

Revit's workflow is the starting point, so that anyone who knows it feels at home, but it is not the ceiling. Where Revit is limited or painful we'll improve on it rather than copy it, and propose each change here first. Some of it is already in:

- **A failure says why.** An evaluation that fails names the node, the error and the dependency path that led there. The results that don't depend on it survive, and a failed instance stays saveable and reopenable.
- **Everything is a command.** Agents, scripts and MCP can capture frames and place hundreds of panels in one undo step, without clicking each point.
- **Orientation is explicit.** A point on a curve says how it turns (four rules, documented), and every offset and rotation can be a formula.
- **An instance is reproducible.** `adaptive_evaluate` returns everything needed to replay it on its own: definition, frames, resolved parameters.

**Out of scope for now:** nested adaptive families, live constraints between instances, divided surfaces, analytic host surfaces, and a graph editor.
