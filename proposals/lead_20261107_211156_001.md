## Mission

Qualify the transition from measured building geometry to an **editable, semantically coherent, uncertainty-preserving as-built representation**—and decide when the evidence is sufficient to propose a digital-twin mutation.

This is the building-specific reconstruction/admission layer between:
- [#1607 SURVEY-EVIDENCE-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/1607), which owns survey observations, CRS/frame identity, control adjustment, registration/change-estimate provenance and uncertainty;
- [#4043 BUILT-ASSET-ROBOT-LOOP-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/4043), which owns the existing-building inspect → repair → re-verify lifecycle;
- [#4062 BUILT-ASSET-CORPUS-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/4062), which owns the first defect/repair synthetic fixtures and independent Rust oracle;
- [#3331 COMPONENT-DIGITAL-THREAD-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/3331), which owns component identity and lifecycle evidence continuity.

**Ownership boundary:** #1607 retains authority over the quality and provenance of survey/registration evidence. This issue starts only after a survey product is qualified for its declared use. It owns building-element association, semantic/topological consistency, reconstruction completeness, change attribution and the admission decision. It does not create a second point-cloud registration engine, CRS engine, metrology contract, component registry, IFC authoring stack or change-event ledger.

## Why this is a real research opportunity

### Research baselines

1. **BIMNet (Liu et al., Automation in Construction, May 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106096)** proposes a real-world IFC-labelled scan-to-BIM dataset with 25 indoor scans, 116,507,604 points, 382 rooms and 8,710 m². It explicitly evaluates geometric and topological reconstruction quality, and notes that generic indoor-scene datasets are not a substitute for building-element ground truth. It is a candidate external benchmark, subject to access and licence review—not a dataset we assume is freely reusable.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106096
2. **Ersöz & Bosché (Automation in Construction, Jan 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106636)** propose geometric matching confidence from coverage, distance and distribution indices, validated on 46 point-cloud/BIM pairs and more than 4,000 elements. Their treatment of self-occluded surfaces is especially relevant: unseen surfaces must not be counted as observed mismatch or successful coverage.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106636
3. **Patil, Sowmya & Kalantari (ISPRS Annals, published 3 July 2026, DOI 10.5194/isprs-annals-XI-1-2026-437-2026)** report that door detection and window detection differ materially across tested point-cloud models; the best reported door detection rate is 96.9%, while the best window detection rate is 75.0%. They further caution that semantic segmentation performance does not necessarily imply accurate geometric reconstruction. The reported rates are dataset/task-specific, not universal SOTA.
   - https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-annals-XI-1-2026-437-2026
4. **Jayasinghe & Brilakis, *Adapting occlusion completion methods for scan-to-BIM automation*, Automation in Construction 189 (September 2026), article 107052, DOI: 10.1016/j.autcon.2026.107052.** The authors identify bounding-box leakage as a major source of misleading point-cloud completion performance, propose a corrected benchmark without that leakage, and report roughly 25% lower EMD and 40% lower Chamfer Distance for their method on the evaluated datasets. Treat these as study-specific results, not a transferable SOTA guarantee. The essential lesson for this benchmark is to withhold reference bounds from candidate inputs and split by building/scene/capture lineage.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2026.107052
5. **ISPRS Benchmark on Indoor Modelling.** The ISPRS site maintains six indoor point clouds captured with different sensors and scene conditions, including low-clutter TUB scans, high-clutter Fire Brigade with occlusion gaps/curtain walls, UVigo, UoM where windows are hidden by blinds, and the non-Manhattan Grainger Museum. It provides a valuable cross-sensor and cross-geometry evaluation set alongside BIMNet. Confirm the available reference/BIM package, provenance and reuse terms before freezing a reproducible adapter.
   - https://isprs.org/resources/datasets/benchmarks/IndoorModeling/dataset.aspx
6. A 2026 representation-centred review of existing-building Scan-to-BIM argues that uncertainty and provenance can be obscured during regularization, fitting and IFC instantiation, while object identity and relationships can be lost when geometry is reorganized. This frames the core interface problem: a plausible model is not necessarily a faithful, updateable representation.
   - https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/20/4095
7. A 2026 review of 58 documented Scan-to-BIM cases finds increasing AI-driven automation but continuing need for domain datasets and end-to-end evaluation.
   - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2025.106755

The Luminous opportunity is the **decision and evidence layer across representation changes**: preserving identity, uncertainty, coverage and topology, distinguishing real change from acquisition/registration artifacts, and refusing a model update when evidence is insufficient.

## Paradigm shifts

1. **From point-cloud fit to evidence-sufficient model state.** A low residual is not enough. Require coverage, viewpoint visibility, independent checkpoints, correspondence confidence and task-specific geometry tolerances.
2. **From one reconstructed solid to multiple linked representations.** Preserve raw observations, measured surfaces, fitted geometry, semantic building elements and topology as distinct but linked artifacts. Never overwrite measured evidence with a regularized model.
3. **From semantic labels to persistent element identity.** A detected wall/door/window is not automatically the same asset element as the prior model. Use explicit correspondence hypotheses and retain split/merge/replacement/unknown states.
4. **From geometric difference to causal change hypotheses.** A delta may be real construction/repair, changed furniture/door pose, occlusion, registration drift, sensor error or an incorrect match. Keep these outcomes distinct.
5. **From forced reconstruction to calibrated abstention.** The correct output can be partial model + unknown region + request for another observation. Do not hallucinate hidden geometry or silently fill missing evidence.
6. **From whole-scene accuracy to decision-value-driven sensing.** Ask whether another scan/view/checkpoint would alter element association, defect triage, repair choice or acceptance. Reuse #1892 for value of information; do not create a duplicate observation scheduler.
7. **From static handover to reversible evidence-qualified mutation.** A model change is a candidate, revision-bound transaction with before/after evidence and invalidation rules—not a direct overwrite of authoritative asset state.
8. **From random splits to leakage-resistant evaluation.** Hold out entire buildings, scenes, capture sessions and source lineages. Never expose ground-truth bounding boxes, reference geometry or other target-derived features to the candidate completion model. Evaluate uncertainty and downstream meshing/element reconstruction, not only point-cloud similarity.

## V0.1 bounded benchmark

Start with small deterministic room/panel assemblies and synthetic point samples. No learned model or live sensor needed for the first oracle.

### Scene and change families

- Plane/wall, floor, ceiling, corner, aperture, door, window, replaceable panel, frame and fastener features.
- Correct geometry; missing surfaces; sparse sampling; self-occlusion; multi-view overlap; duplicate scans; nonuniform density.
- Correct, ambiguous and incorrect element correspondence; moved/replaced/split/merged components.
- True additions/removals/displacements versus benign furniture, open/closed door state, temporary equipment and changed sensor viewpoint.
- Registration bias, small rotation/translation perturbation, local drift, coordinate-frame mismatch, unit mismatch and stale design revision.
- Semantic label errors where the fitted geometry is plausible but the element class or relation is wrong.
- Topology adversaries: disconnected wall joins, missing opening boundary, duplicate element, impossible containment/adjacency, orphaned component and broken parent-child identity.
- Calibration/uncertainty adversaries: overconfident fit, under-covered interval, correlated observations and unsupported extrapolation behind an occluder.

### Separate reference planes

- Approved design / prior-model revision.
- Independently surveyed as-built geometry and control/checkpoints.
- Candidate observations and capture/visibility masks.
- Evaluator-only truth for the synthetic suite.
- Reconstruction candidate and proposed model delta.

Candidate code must never read evaluator truth. Point samples and derived views from one capture keep their common lineage. Use independent checkpoints not used for registration, fitting or tuning.

### Metrics—never collapse to one score

**Geometry:** point-to-surface and surface-to-point distance distributions; median/p95/p99; normal/angular error; element dimensions and datum error; completeness/coverage by visible and occluded region; independent-checkpoint error.

**Semantics and topology:** per-class precision/recall; component-level association precision/recall; split/merge correctness; graph-edge precision/recall for adjacency, containment, connection and opening boundary; orphan/duplicate/missing-element counts.

**Change/admission:** true-change precision/recall by change type; false-change rate on no-change pairs; false-mutation rate; unknown/abstention rate; unsupported-region hallucination count; stale-revision and invalidation detection; disposition correctness.

**Uncertainty:** interval coverage and width; calibration/reliability by class and geometry type; risk-versus-coverage curve; decision stability under perturbation; uncertainty propagation from survey input to reconstructed object and final admission.

**Workflow:** accepted model updates per total crew-hour; review time; added scan/measurement cost; rework caused by false or missed mutations; time to resolve an unknown region. Keep software runtime and human review burden separate.

### Required dispositions

At minimum: ADMIT_CANDIDATE_DELTA, PARTIAL_WITH_UNKNOWN, REQUEST_ADDITIONAL_OBSERVATION, REAL_CHANGE_CANDIDATE, NO_CHANGE_SUPPORTED, CONFLICTED_CORRESPONDENCE, STALE_OR_INVALID_EVIDENCE, OUT_OF_DOMAIN, REJECTED_TOPOLOGY, and NOT_EVALUATED.

No disposition in this benchmark grants structural acceptance, legal survey status, code compliance or authority to perform physical work.

3. Each relevant commercial product/version/configuration (Leica Cyclone 3DR, Trimble RealWorks, Autodesk ReCap Pro/Revit).
4. Reproducible published methods, including the BIMNet and point-cloud/BIM matching protocols where source access permits.
5. Luminous pipeline, with all versions and external adapters frozen.

Freeze exact version/licence/configuration, input digests, registration inputs, reference checkpoints, human edits and exclusions. A vendor feature checklist is not a benchmark result. Do not compare metrics across different scans, tolerances, visible surface denominators or truth sources.

## Advancement gates

**P0 — synthetic oracle**
- 100% expected disposition and reason-code agreement on the frozen finite suite.
- 0 unsupported hidden-surface assertions.
- 0 model mutations on stale frames/revisions or conflicted identity.
- Geometry, semantic and topology errors detected independently; no single metric can hide a hard failure.

**P1 — public/existing dataset replay**
- Reproduce a source baseline before claiming improvement.
- Publish geometric, topological, semantic and uncertainty results separately.
- Respect data access and licensing; no unverified claim of dataset availability.

**P2 — guarded lab article**
- Independently measured reference geometry and held-out checkpoints.
- Blind adjudication of proposed element correspondences and changes.
- Predeclared uncertainty budget and metrology suitability.
- Compare human, instrumented human and supervised robot workflows only when task and resource boundaries match.

**P3 — prospective operation**
- Separate site/building holdouts, revision churn, real occlusion/environment strata and downstream repair outcomes.
- Demonstrate lower false-mutation/rework burden without degrading completeness, dimensional quality, worker safety or maintainability.

Do not set numeric superiority targets until a baseline reproduction and uncertainty analysis establish comparable operating conditions. The first improvement target is Pareto dominance: no worse independent geometric quality, while reducing false model mutations and preserving more uncertainty/provenance with no hidden increase in human review burden.
