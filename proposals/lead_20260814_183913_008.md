# Problem Statement

The native Apple reader has repeatedly violated its most important reading invariant: visible text must never be cut off. In paginated mode, glyphs have been clipped at the bottom of the viewport; an incomplete line has sometimes been hidden by the page indicator or mini-player; and the existing probe could report success because it ignored the very partial fragments that were visible.

Showing and hiding reader chrome has also been unstable. Repeated or rapid middle-screen taps can flicker text, recompute pagination against transient geometry, introduce large blank gaps, and restore the book at a different passage (including moving backwards or eventually returning to the beginning). The reader currently distributes one viewport transition across multiple presentation owners, allowing stale or intermediate geometry to affect TextKit.

The reader must provide a true horizontal page turn in paginated mode, preserve safe-area boundaries when chrome is hidden, maintain the intended compact expanded-player spacing, and reliably open/import the Lord of the Rings development book without PDF-import crashes.

# Solution

Make the reader viewport a stable, transactionally applied product surface. A chrome toggle captures the exact visible reading position before any geometry changes, applies the final chrome geometry once, recalculates only after that final geometry exists, and restores the captured viewport without substituting a page-cache boundary. Pagination uses glyph-aware line geometry so every rendered glyph is either wholly visible on a page or moves to the next page. Explicit page navigation alone uses canonical page boundaries and a horizontal page-turn animation.

The same behavior is verified through the native reader using the seeded Lord of the Rings EPUB, including native typography at small sizes and rapid chrome toggles. Development automation leaves the simulator app open on that book after verification.

# Implementation Decisions

- Treat the final physical viewport, including safe area, reader navigation, page indicator, and mini-player, as the single source of truth for text layout.
- Introduce a concrete `ReaderViewportTransition` module to own chrome-transition intent, anchor capture, generation/token handling, final geometry commit, and stale-completion rejection. Keep it concrete until a second real platform or host variation requires an abstraction.
- Make the root presentation owner coordinate the single layout transaction. Reader content and intermediate hosts supply or apply their geometry, but do not independently animate, prepare, complete, or repaginate the same toggle.
- Capture the raw viewport `contentOffset` before any chrome geometry changes. During a chrome transition, restore that raw offset exactly; do not replace it with a remembered progress offset, a fraction, or a paginated page offset.
- Restrict canonical pagination offsets to explicit page turns. Progress restoration and reflow may use a semantic reading anchor, but may not make chrome round trips drift to another passage.
- Defer TextKit invalidation, pagination construction, scroll clamping, indicator updates, and overflow-mask decisions until the final viewport geometry has been committed. Do not measure an interpolating transition viewport.
- Preserve the visible text surface during a transition, using a stable surface or snapshot as necessary, and reveal the committed layout only after it is valid.
- Deepen `ReaderPaginatedTextLayout` into the sole pagination-decision module. Its result owns content height, canonical page offsets, protected fragment geometry, and validation data; the reader controller applies that result rather than duplicating geometry rules.
- Define each protected text fragment conservatively from both its line-fragment rectangle and rendered glyph bounds. A page start may occur only at a protected-fragment boundary.
- Allocate trailing content height needed for the final canonical page start; never append an arbitrary maximum scroll offset that can land inside a fragment.
- If a single protected fragment exceeds a page capacity, surface an explicit oversized-fragment condition and use a non-clipping fallback instead of creating a partial page.
- Maintain a page-overflow treatment only as a visual safety layer; it cannot be the mechanism that makes a clipped page acceptable.
- In paginated mode, use a horizontal left-to-right/right-to-left page-turn transition. Do not use a vertical movement or a 180-degree rotation as the reading-page transition.
- Keep chrome-hidden text bounded by safe areas. Chrome-visible geometry may reserve navigation, indicator, and mini-player space, but must still resolve from the final host geometry.
- Keep expanded-player artwork placement stable and make the lower controls/progress/text/button stacks use 8-point vertical spacing.
- Keep the Lord of the Rings development seed path idempotent: add the book only when absent. Keep document import failure-safe for EPUB and PDF.

# Testing Decisions

- The highest-value behavioral seam is `ReaderViewportTransition`: test its resulting stable viewport, anchor, generation, and clipping contract rather than its internal constraint ordering.
- Add native XCTest coverage for glyph-aware pagination with native serif text, small and large sizes, sans/serif/italic styles, dynamic-type extremes, diacritics, emoji, CJK, attachments where supported, portrait, and landscape.
- Assert that every fragment intersecting a page viewport is fully contained, that every fragment belongs to at least one page, and that no canonical page offset is an interior fragment offset.
- Expose a debug/UI pagination probe that counts all intersecting protected fragments and reports clipped-top, clipped-bottom, clipped-line count, page, and reading anchor. It must fail on partial fragments rather than filtering them out.
- Use the seeded Lord of the Rings EPUB in native UI automation on the supported iOS 17 simulator configuration. Exercise page advance/back, the first and final page, page indicator visibility, mini-player visibility, and scrolling mode separately.
- Test normal, rapid, and interrupted chrome toggles from multiple pages. Sample during and after animation; assert no clipped-line count, no visible blank-gap regression, final chrome state matches the last tap, and the same raw offset/visible anchor returns after each round trip.
- Test native small-font LOTR layout specifically to detect both clipping and excessive blank page space.
- Test the horizontal page-turn direction and ensure paginated mode never uses a vertical scrolling transition.
- Test EPUB and PDF selection/opening for no crash. Keep development seeding and simulator-open behavior in automation support, not production reader behavior.
- Do not substitute Python tests that inspect Swift source. Reader regressions belong in the Apple unit and UI test targets.

# Further Notes

- The non-negotiable invariant is: text never clips in any supported font, size, orientation, safe-area configuration, or reader mode.
- Chrome toggling is a viewport-size change, not a page-navigation event. It must preserve the exact visible content offset and must not use pagination-cache boundaries to restore position.
- The observed regression sequence included clipped bottom glyphs, false-green clipping probes, whitespace after repeated chrome toggles, flicker, forward/backward drift, and eventual return toward the start of the book. The acceptance suite must reproduce those histories, not only a single settled toggle.
- Existing architectural inspection identified the reader controller, intermediate reader host, and root container as separate historical owners of one chrome transition. Consolidating that ownership is required to make the behavior durable.
- A previous direct runtime validation showed stable repeated restoration only when the raw offset was retained (`2263 -> 2263 -> 2263`); retain this as a behavioral regression case, not as an implementation-specific assertion.
- Baseline corrective work is represented by commit `ffe7a8f9` (`Fix native reader chrome viewport stability`). This specification defines the durable contract and remaining regression coverage around that work.
