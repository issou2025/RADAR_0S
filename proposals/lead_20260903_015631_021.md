# Project Proposal — Structural Drawings

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Extract OSM water from our own planet PBF and tile to z0-10, so the coast and coastal borders are the same geometry**

*Project description:*
Our map's coastline comes from Natural Earth 1:10,000,000, tiled to z7. Our admin boundaries come from OpenStreetMap, tiled to z10. Two sources at different generalisations, so at high zoom the sea does not follow the borders drawn over it. This issue extracts real water polygons **from the same planet PBF the admin boundaries came from** and tiles them to **z0–10**, so coast and border are the same geometry rather than two approximations of it.

## Decisions taken (SG, 2 Sep 2026)

1. **Extract from our own PBF, not from pre-built water polygons.** Same edition as the admin features. A pre-built set from a different OSM snapshot would be *close*; the same edition is *identical* — see "What same-edition buys" below.
2. **Extract once at full fidelity, uncapped.** Extraction produces geometry and has no zoom; zoom exists only at tiling time. The extraction is the expensive, specialised job and it happens once.
3. **Tile z0–10.** Matching `osm`. Capacity to go deeper is not expected for a long time, so z10 is the working target, not a staging post.
4. **`ohm` is out of scope.** It serves a different purpose and does not need to stay in lockstep with water.

## Evidence — why this is an import and not a styling fix

The `osm` authority ingests `natural`, `water`, `waterway` and `landuse` tag keys, so water **records** exist in our corpus. Almost none carry a polygon. Live index, `osm:` namespace, records vs. areal geometries:

| osm tag key | records | areal geometries |
|---|---:|---:|
| `boundary=*` — **control** | 1,217,862 | **792,827** (65%) |
| `place=*` | 7,849,985 | 179,508 |
| `landuse=*` | 2,624,049 | 7,516 |
| `natural=*` | 2,591,048 | **123** |
| `waterway=*` | 6,046,926 | **2** |
| `water=*` | 549,873 | **0** |

`boundary` is the positive control: it *must* come back substantially areal, because the admin tiles are built from it, and it does. Water comes back 0, 2 and 123. **We extracted water nodes from the PBF and never water polygons.**

Two code facts confirm no styling or banding change could reach it:

- `_build_staged_feature` returns `None` for any doc with no `boundary` value when `require_boundary` is set, and `require_boundary = bucket in _FIXED_BUCKETS` = `{osm, ohm, osm_misc}`. Water docs are rejected before geometry resolution and before `assign_band` is reached — so they are not even counted by the "matched no band (dropped)" diagnostic.
- The per-feature properties written into the osm tile source are `place_id · namespace · boundary(if set) · name · name_<lang> · name_local · start · end · start_def · end_def · aat(if set) · population(if set) · fcode(if set) · tippecanoe:minzoom(if set)`. **No OSM tag key or value is emitted at all**, so a `where` predicate would have nothing to select water on even if the features arrived.

## Where the present water comes from

`whg-ne-basic` is built by `tiler/utils/create_NE_mbtiles.sh` in the **tileboss** repo, from Natural Earth 1:10m physical shapefiles fetched by `fetch_NE_vector_layers.sh`:

```
physical/ne_10m_ocean                    -Z0 -z7   "ocean"
physical/ne_10m_lakes                    -Z0 -z7   "lakes"
physical/ne_10m_rivers_lake_centerlines  -Z0 -z7   "rivers"
```

1:10,000,000 data capped at z7 by an explicit tippecanoe flag. A commented-out earlier layerset used `-z9`; raising the cap would not help, because **the generalisation is the problem, not the zoom cap.**

## What same-edition buys

Not a closer approximation — a different property. Coastal admin boundaries in OSM **share the coastline way**: the `boundary=administrative` relation references the same way IDs as `natural=coastline`. From one PBF edition, coast and coastal border are **vertex-identical** — the same geometry rendered twice, aligned by construction at every zoom, including overzoomed. From two editions they are merely close.

✅ **MEASURED 2 Sep, so this is no longer an argument about OSM practice.** All-tags extract from the pinned planet at Neum: 611 coastline ways, 64,158 coastline nodes, 153 admin boundaries touching the coast. Per boundary — member ways that are themselves `natural=coastline` ways, and boundary nodes that are coastline nodes:

```
boundary                lvl  ways  coastline   nodes  on coast   share
Općina Mljet              7    97         97   9,394     9,394   100.0%
Grad Dubrovnik            7   109         88   6,324     6,153    97.3%
Općina Jelsa              7    36         27   6,028     6,007    99.7%
Grad Korčula              7    46         43   4,455     4,326    97.1%
Općina Ston               7    64         56   4,049     4,034    99.6%
Općina Lastovo            7    69         42   2,041     2,041   100.0%
```

**Across the top 20 boundaries: 55,393 of 56,776 nodes lie on a coastline way — 97.6%.** Općina Mljet is 97 of 97 member ways and 9,394 of 9,394 nodes: its administrative boundary is *composed of* coastline ways, with nothing else in it. This is a measurement over shared **node IDs**, not a visual judgement — two lines that look coincident may merely be close; two lines built from the same node IDs are the same geometry, and no rendering can separate them. **Water assembled from these ways is aligned with these boundaries by construction.**

⚠️ **Scope of the claim:** one bbox, on a coast chosen as a worst case for Natural Earth. 97.6% is the Croatian Adriatic, not the planet. It establishes that the mechanism is real and dominant where it matters, not a global rate.

**This also sharpens what the known-bad control shows.** The Mljet outline photographed sitting well out in Natural Earth's sea is **100% coastline nodes** — so that screenshot is not an admin boundary disagreeing with a coast. It is **the actual coastline disagreeing with Natural Earth's rendering of it**, the same feature drawn twice from two sources.

⚠️ **This makes the PBF edition a hard input, and the filename is a trap.** The source is `/ix1/ishi/data/authorities/osm/planet-latest.osm.pbf` (87.5 GB, mtime **2026-07-30 18:07 EDT**), and the admin re-extraction ran the following day (`temporal-20260731T160000Z`), so the current file is very probably the edition the boundaries came from. But `planet-latest` is a **mutable name**: `fetch_authorities` refreshes it. ✅ **It does NOT overwrite in place** — an earlier revision of this issue said so and was wrong, misleadingly and expensively. Verified in `processing/fetch_authorities.py`: both download paths (`_download_with_curl`, `_download_with_resume`) write to `dest + ".part"` and finish with `temp.replace(dest)`, an **atomic rename**, and nothing opens the destination directly for writing.

✅ **Better still, the edition is INSIDE the file, and it survives derivation.** `osmium fileinfo` reports `osmosis_replication_timestamp=2026-07-20T00:00:00Z` (generator `planet-dump-ng 1.2.4`) — so the **data edition is 20 July 2026**; the 30 July mtime is merely when the download finished. Measured 2 Sep: the tags-filtered derivative **carries the same replication timestamp in its own header**, with data timestamps running to `2026-07-19T23:58:43Z`. So every artefact derived through `osmium` self-identifies its edition and can be checked against the planet **without reference to a filename, an inode, or anyone's notes**. Use the replication timestamp as the primary edition identifier; keep the inode as a tamper check on the pin.

**Pin it with a hardlink beside it on `/ix1`, and record size + mtime + inode.** A refresh repoints the *name* at a new inode while the old inode survives as long as your hardlink references it — exact bytes, same filesystem, **zero extra space**. ⚠️ **Do not copy 87.5 GB to `/vast`**, which the "overwritten in place" wording wrongly implied: `/vast` is 1 TB **shared with production ES** and has hit flood-stage read-only in this project before. The inode is also the right discriminator — after a refresh the name points at a different inode, which detects it positively instead of inferring from mtime. If the PBF is refreshed between the admin extract and the water extract, the whole rationale for this approach is silently void — with no error and no visible symptom.

## Why z0–10

Alignment is a *relative* property: two layers agree at every zoom, including overzoomed, when they come from the same source at the same maxzoom and the same simplification. `osm.mbtiles` is **z0–10** (read from built metadata, not inferred from the band spec — individual bands stop shallower by design, `continental` z0–4 and `country` z0–8, but the joined tileset is z0–10). **Water is therefore built `-Z0 -z10`.**

Deeper is not affordable and is not planned. Measured from the deployed tileset, `osm` grows ×2.19 from z9 (206 MB) to z10 (451 MB); at ~2–3× per zoom, z11 ≈ 1.0 GB and z12 ≈ 2.3 GB for admin alone, against **8.6 GB free** on the tileserver. Should that change, water and admin move **together** — and note that raising the maxzoom is a **re-tile of both, not a re-extraction**, because step 1 is uncapped.

**Inland water is straightforward; the ocean is the specialised part.**

- **Inland water** (`natural=water`, `water=*`, `waterway=riverbank`, `landuse=reservoir`) is assembled with **`osmium.area.AreaManager`** (pyosmium 4.x — note `MultipolygonManager` is the pyosmium 3.x name and does **not** exist in 4.2.0). These are closed ways and `type=multipolygon` relations, and the relations genuinely need area assembly — see Step 2's control.
- **Speed is not a reason to prefer the CLI.** pyosmium binds the same C++ libosmium: decode, indexing and area assembly all run in C++. The only Python cost is a callback per object crossing the interpreter boundary, and `FileProcessor.with_filter(...)` applies filters **C++-side** so only matching objects cross — a small fraction of the planet for water tags.
- **Ocean is not a tag you can filter.** `natural=coastline` is carried on **open way fragments** that must be stitched into closed rings planet-wide, with antimeridian and pole handling. That is precisely what `osmcoastline` exists to do, and it is neither installed nor packaged. **Options: build it from source** (C++; needs libosmium, protozero, GDAL, GEOS, SQLite, Boost — and note the existing broken `osmium` binary is itself a Boost version mismatch, so this build class has already failed once here), **or write the ring assembly in pyosmium**, which is not advisable at planet scale.

⚠️ **An earlier revision of this issue called the source build fragile, citing the broken `osmium` binary as evidence that "this build class has already failed once here". That was wrong and is withdrawn.** The broken binary is a stale **user** install in `~/.local/bin` linked against **system** Boost — not a conda build, and not evidence about one. Every `osmcoastline` dependency is in conda-forge at a current version (listed above), including `boost-cpp 1.85.0`, which is precisely the version the stale binary is looking for. A cmake build inside the `whg` env is an ordinary proposition, not a research project.

⚠️ **If the `osmcoastline` build proves intractable, the honest fallback is the pre-built water polygons** from `osmdata.openstreetmap.de`. That is a real degradation — it forfeits the vertex-identity above — but at z10 one pixel is ≈153 m at the equator (360°/1024 tiles ≈ 39.1 km, ÷256), so snapshot drift is sub-pixel by roughly two orders of magnitude and **invisible at our target maxzoom**, against the kilometre-scale NE error being removed. Falling back is a legitimate outcome, not a failure — but it should be a stated decision, not a quiet substitution.

## The work — all on CRC Slurm

Processing runs on CRC, not the tileserver, whose resources are stretched.

**Step 0a — environment. ⚠️ Do NOT `export` `LD_LIBRARY_PATH`.** Prefix it per command instead:

```
LD_LIBRARY_PATH=$CONDA_PREFIX/lib osmium tags-filter ...   # osmium needs it
ogr2ogr ...                                                # must NOT have it
```

An earlier revision said `export`, and that is wrong: under the conda lib path `ogr2ogr` dies with `symbol lookup error: /lib64/libldap.so.2: undefined symbol: EVP_md2, version OPENSSL_3.0.0` — system libldap against conda's OpenSSL 3. **`osmium` and `ogr2ogr` cannot share one exported loader path**, and an `export` poisons every later process in the shell. This bites hardest at Step 1, since `osmcoastline` is a GDAL/OGR consumer: build it against **conda's** GDAL/GEOS so it is internally consistent and lives on the `osmium` side of the split. Nothing needs installing for `osmium` itself.

**Working volume: `/ix1`, not `/vast`.** Measured 2 Sep: `/ix1/ishi` 5120 GB total / **1837 GB free**; `/vast/ishi` 1024 GB / 226 GB free and **shared with production Elasticsearch**. The pinned PBF already lives on `/ix1`, and the tile campaign writes there too. Put the filtered PBF, intermediates and the final `water.mbtiles` on `/ix1`; put tippecanoe's temp (heavy random I/O, the one part that suffers on NFS) on `$SLURM_SCRATCH`; keep `/vast` footprint at zero. ⚠️ `/ix1`'s documented NFS pathology is about **thousands of small files** — the opposite of this access pattern — so do not shard intermediates there.

⚠️ **If you nonetheless guard `/vast` free space, stat `/vast/ishi`, never bare `/vast`.** `df /vast` reports the **3.9 PB shared pool**; `df /vast/ishi` reports our **1 TB quota**. Both print `Mounted on /vast`, so the readings are near-indistinguishable in a log and a guard on the wrong one compares millions of GB against its floor and never fires. ES watermarks on the 1 TB quota: low 153.6 GB free, high 102.4 GB, **flood 51.2 GB = indices go read-only**.

**Step 0b — pin the input.** Hardlink the PBF beside itself on `/ix1` and record size + mtime + **inode**. Zero extra space; see "What same-edition buys" for why a hardlink is sufficient and a copy is not wanted. Nothing downstream is meaningful if the edition changes mid-flight.

**Step 0c — ⚠️ tags-filter the planet FIRST. This is the step that makes the rest tractable.**

```
osmium tags-filter planet-latest.osm.pbf \
    w/natural=coastline \
    nwr/natural=water nwr/water nwr/waterway nwr/landuse=reservoir \
    -o water-source.osm.pbf
```

**Why this is structural and not a convenience.** Area assembly needs the *location of every node referenced by every candidate way*. Run against the 87.5 GB planet that means either a dense in-memory node index (well over 100 GB of RAM — not available) or a disk-backed sparse index (workable but slow over billions of nodes). Run against a tags-filtered extract, the node index fits comfortably in memory and the pass is a different order of job.

`osmium tags-filter` **includes referenced objects by default** — `-R` is `--omit-referenced`, the flag to switch that *off* — so the filtered file carries the member ways and nodes needed to build the areas, and `osmium check-refs` will confirm it. One filtered PBF serves both Step 1 (it retains `natural=coastline`) and Step 2.

✅ **RESOLVED 2-3 Sep — the `w/` prefix is correct as written, and the one row it loses is provably inert.** Control 1 reconciled **13 of 14 rows exactly** (union row: 60,876,916 ways and 1,093,872 relations, ratio 1.0000) with a single loss: `natural=coastline` **relations**, planet 187, filtered 0.

```
natural=coastline relations                 187   (type=* : multipolygon, ALL 187)
  distinct member ways TAGGED coastline     587   → 587 of 587 present in filtered file
  distinct member ways NOT tagged            41   →   4 of 41 present via other tags
  actual gap                                 37 ways = 0.0029% of 1,284,756
```

`natural=coastline` is a **linear way tag**, so a multipolygon carrying it is invalid tagging — these are 187 mapping errors, not a convention. **And the gap cannot be closed by re-filtering:** osmcoastline keys on the tag being *on the way*, so those 37 untagged ways would be ignored whether or not they were in the file. Adding `r/natural=coastline` would enlarge the file and change nothing. Closing it would mean synthesising way tags from relation membership — machinery to honour 187 tagging errors. **Our coastline input is exactly as complete as the standard OSM coastline product**, which every alternative (including the osmdata.openstreetmap.de fallback) is built from.

Recorded in `require_control1.py` as a waiver **pinned to the evidence**: it names the row and asserts `planet == 187 and filtered == 0`, refusing if either moves, so the exception expires when its justification does. Self-tested in both directions — real numbers PASS (13 exact + 1 waived); planet forced to 188 REFUSES.

⚠️ **Verify the filter kept what it should before trusting the smaller file.** Same discipline as everywhere else: count `natural=water` ways and relations in the filtered file and confirm they match the planet's counts. A tags-filter expression that silently matches nothing produces a small, valid, wholly useless PBF — and every subsequent step would succeed on it.

**Step 0d — cut a small regional extract** with `osmium extract --bbox` for Step 2's control test, so the multipolygon check runs in minutes rather than hours.

**Step 1 — ocean.** Establish the `osmcoastline` route (build from source) or take the documented fallback. Produce ocean polygons from the pinned PBF.

⚠️ **What the ocean job looks like while healthy — write this down before someone kills a working job.** Measured 3 Sep on the planet run:

```
[ 2:33]  reading + closing rings           ~5 min
[ 5:17]  "Closed 3 rings ... 0 NODES where the coastline could not be closed"
[ 5:17 → 1h20m+]  Create polygons (--max-points=1000 splitting)
```

**The last phase runs for over an hour with NO database writes and no log output** — `water_polygons` is written at the end of the phase, not incrementally — so a silent `coastline.db` and a still log are the *expected* state, not a hang. To confirm it is alive rather than deadlocked, check that CPU time tracks elapsed time:

```
srun -M htc --jobid=<id> --overlap ps -o pid,etime,time,pcpu,rss,stat,comm
   577062  01:16:56  01:18:25  101%  7.3 GB  Rl  osmcoastline     ← compute-bound, healthy
```

⚠️ The `-M htc` is required — plain `srun --jobid` fails with "Invalid job id" on this federation.

🛑 **`--srs=4326` SILENTLY RESCALES `--close-distance` BY FIVE ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE. Pass `-c` explicitly.**

```
osmcoastline --srs=4326 -c 0.0001 ...     # ~11 m — pass this
osmcoastline --srs=4326 ...               # inherits default -c 1 = ~111 km at the equator
```

`--close-distance` is expressed in **the units of the output SRS**. osmcoastline's default SRS is 3857, where the default `-c 1` means **one metre** and is entirely sane. We need `--srs=4326` because tippecanoe wants degrees — and that same `-c 1` then means **one degree, ~111 km**. Nothing about `--srs` announces that it has rescaled an unrelated default; the log prints `Closing if distance between nodes smaller than 1.` without naming a unit. **The two options interact and neither documents the other.**

The blast radius is bounded but real: rings are assembled by matching node IDs, so the tolerance is only reached for ends that remain open after that — in the 3 Sep run, `Closed 3 rings`. A metre-scale closure is harmless; a kilometre-scale one means **coastlines joined that are not connected**, and the build must be redone (~80 min). Measure what was actually closed before deciding; do not rebuild on the size of the tolerance alone, and do not accept it on the size of the count alone.

**Step 2 — inland water,** with pyosmium, from the same pinned PBF.

⚠️ **The multipolygon control, on a small extract and before the planet run.** Count `natural=water` **closed ways** and `natural=water` **multipolygon relations** separately, run the intended export, count **polygons out**, and include a lake known to have islands as a positive control.

- polygons ≈ ways + relations → assembly is working
- polygons ≈ ways only → **relations are being dropped silently**

⚠️ Simple ponds dominate `natural=water` numerically, so dropping *every* relation still yields a large, healthy-looking count — while losing precisely the big complex lakes that matter most visually. **No total can see this; only the ways-vs-relations split can.**

**Step 3 — tile** `-Z0 -z10`, one tileset, separate layers for ocean / lakes / rivers so the style can treat them differently.

**Step 3 acceptance — the size gate belongs here, not at deploy.** The tileset is built on CRC with 226 GB of `/vast`, so its size is known before anything is transferred. State `water.mbtiles` size; **if `size > (free − 4 GB)`, i.e. > ~4.6 GB, do not transfer** — take the fallback, in this order:

1. **Apply a MINIMUM-AREA THRESHOLD to inland water.** ⚠️ Measured over all 23,145,011 assembled inland areas (21.71 GiB), 3 Sep — the `size @ z10` column is the feature's width in pixels at z10, where 1 px ≈ 153 m at the equator:

   ```
   drop below   features cut   % count      bytes cut   % bytes   size @ z10
      0.1 ha       6,819,052     29.46%   2,768,757,394   11.88%     0.21 px
      1.0 ha      17,017,873     73.53%   8,549,071,830   36.67%     0.65 px
      5.0 ha      20,835,667     90.02%  12,435,438,588   53.34%     1.46 px
     10.0 ha      21,729,464     93.88%  13,956,051,471   59.86%     2.07 px
     50.0 ha      22,750,266     98.29%  17,104,797,217   73.37%     4.62 px
    100.0 ha      22,928,801     99.07%  18,221,893,243   78.16%     6.54 px
   ```

   **73.5% of the planet's inland water is under one hectare — 0.65 px at z10, i.e. invisible.** Dropping it removes 36.7% of bytes and nothing anyone can see. At 5 ha it is 90% of features and over half the bytes. This lever is **orthogonal to the acceptance criterion** (the coast is untouched, large lakes are untouched) and is **cartographically correct rather than a degradation** — unlike capping zoom, which makes everything worse to save space. No cartographer draws a duckpond at 1:150,000.

   ⚠️ **Count and bytes decouple, and it cuts both ways.** 73.5% of features carry only 36.7% of bytes, because a duckpond is 4 vertices and a fjord is 40,000. **It understates the lever** — feature *count* drives tippecanoe's working set, sort time and per-tile budgeting, not bytes, so the tiling-cost saving should exceed the byte figure; quote bytes as the conservative number. **And it caps the lever** — no threshold gets past ~90% of bytes, because the remainder is large lakes with enormous vertex counts, exactly what must be kept. If the tileset is far too big, **simplification is the next axis, not a lower threshold.**

   ⚠️ **The first build applies NO threshold, deliberately.** Measure, then decide: the criterion concerns the coast, which this does not touch, and there is no size problem to solve until one is measured. If the tileset clears the gate, a threshold becomes a **cartographic improvement to offer SG** — argued on "we are drawing invisible ponds", not on bytes.

   🛑 **One dead lever: "drop `waterway=riverbank`" — 3 ways planet-wide.** The tag was deprecated in favour of `natural=water` + `water=river`; recorded so it is not re-proposed.

   ✅ **"Drop the `rivers` layer" is NOT dead — an earlier revision of this issue said it was, and that was measuring a defect rather than the corpus.** A `classify()` branch-ordering bug (`natural=water → lakes` firing before the waterway test) filed every river polygon as a lake, making the layer look empty at 4,856 features. Corrected:

   ```
   layer      before        after           bytes
   lakes  23,140,155   22,249,159   19,399,142,415  (18.07 GiB)
   rivers      4,856      895,852    3,915,581,966  ( 3.65 GiB)   184x
   ```

   Cross-check: 22,249,159 + 895,852 = 23,145,011, exactly the assembly total. **So dropping `rivers` buys 3.65 GiB, 16.8% of inland bytes** — a real lever.

   ⚠️ **But it is NOT free the way the area threshold is, and its position in the ladder is a cartographic call for SG.** The area threshold removes water nobody can see at z10; dropping `rivers` removes a whole visible category. Losing rivers entirely may well be worse than every inland water body being one zoom level coarser, in which case it belongs *below* the inland-z9 rung rather than above it.

   ⚠️ **The 895,852 figure carries a classification caveat of roughly ±130,000**, pending the same cartographic call — `water=wastewater` (126,188, treatment and settling basins) is **standing** water and is currently filed as river; `lock` (4,136) and `moat` (2,716) are arguable; `basin` (328,274) is currently in lakes though some basins are riverine. Re-splitting is two minutes from `inland.geojsonl`.

2. **Then cap *inland* water at `-z9`, keeping ocean at z10.** Lakes losing a zoom level costs detail nobody is measuring.
3. **Only then consider capping ocean** — and if it comes to that, say so plainly and **renegotiate the acceptance criterion with SG** rather than assessing it as passed. Ocean at z9 is overzoomed at z10, carrying ~306 m/px against an admin border's ~153 m/px: a two-pixel discrepancy *at the coastline*, which is exactly where the criterion looks.

- Step 2's ways-vs-relations split run on a small extract, with an island-bearing lake as positive control, before the planet run.

**After building — post-conditions**

- `water.mbtiles` metadata reads `z0-10`, matching `osm.mbtiles` metadata.
- Inland-water polygon count reconciles against ways + multipolygon relations, not against a bare total.
- **At the deepest zoom where admin lines actually render — z9.9 today** — over a coastal **administrative** boundary (`boundary=4` or deeper), coast and boundary are visually coincident. This is the check the present NE backdrop fails.

  ⚠️ **The check MUST first confirm that admin lines are being drawn in the test viewport, and record that it did.** A "no disagreement anywhere" reading is worthless if no boundary is rendered — and that state **passes equally well on a completely failed water import**. A criterion that passes when the layer is missing is worse than no criterion.

  ⚠️ **Why not "z10" and not "national", as an earlier revision said.** Both were unevaluable as written, measured on the live style 2 Sep:
  * **MapLibre's layer `maxzoom` is exclusive**, and every `osm` line band is capped at `maxzoom: 10`, so at **z ≥ 10.00 no admin lines are drawn at all** — confirmed visually, z9.9 renders the ladder and z10.00 shows coast and raster only. This is style-respec item 5 arriving one zoom earlier than it reads: the dead zone is *at and above* z10, not above it.
  * **`boundary=2` is not available to test.** In the osm tile covering Neum the boundary histogram is `4:18, 5:13, 6:32, 7:118, 8:452, 9:885, 11:5` — no `boundary=2` at all — and `osm-line-country` is capped at `maxzoom: 8` regardless. Coastal `boundary=4` edges follow the coast and come from the same PBF edition, so they test the same property.

# Full style respecification — `whg-context`

Read of the current style, 2 Sep 2026: **10 sources, 36 layers**. Six findings, in descending order of how much they cost. Items 1–4 are defects; 5–6 are judgement calls where the current choice may be deliberate.

## 1. ⚠️ Gazetteer fills paint over the sea

```
 5  ocean        fill  (natural_earth)
 6  lakes        fill  (natural_earth)
...
27  osm_misc-fill-*    fill-opacity 0.18
30  po-fill            fill-opacity 0.16
32  clio-fill          fill-opacity 0.16
34  nl-fill            fill-opacity 0.16
```

Every gazetteer fill draws **above** water. A polity therefore tints the ocean, and because these are semi-transparent the effect **compounds**: `clio` alone now carries **13,907 polygons**, many overlapping, each adding another 16% wash to the same water. This is a large part of what reads as "messy".

**Fix:** draw water **after** the gazetteer fills, as a mask — ocean and lakes are opaque ground truth, not a backdrop. Cheapest correct version: move layers 5–6 to just below the label layers. Requires the new z0–10 water source to be worth doing above z7.

⚠️ **There is a third option, and it is the only one that is actually *correct* rather than a trade-off: split the gazetteer fills into land and marine classes**, so a thalassocracy or a maritime polity legitimately tints the sea while a land polity does not. Masking with water suppresses both alike. This is a substantially larger change — it needs a per-feature marine classification that does not exist in the corpus — and it is named here so it stops being rediscovered later as a bright idea. The reordering above is the pragmatic fix; this is the right one.

## 2. Five water layers are frozen at z7 precision

`ice`, `water_shadow`, `river`, `ocean`, `lakes` all read `natural_earth` → `whg-ne-basic`, **maxzoom 7**. They keep rendering above z7 by overzoom — *verified on the live map to z8.6* — but gain no detail, so the coast is a 1:10 m outline held constant while the map sharpens around it.

**Fix:** repoint **FOUR of the five** at the new `water` source for z8+, keeping `whg-ne-basic` below z8 where its generalisation is an asset.

🛑 **DO NOT REPOINT `ice`. Following this list literally removes the Antarctic ice shelves from the map.** `ice` comes from `physical/ne_10m_antarctic_ice_shelves`; its OSM equivalent would be `natural=glacier`/`landuse=glacier`, which is **deliberately not in Step 0c's filter** since this issue is scoped to water. There is no `ice` in `water.mbtiles` and there will not be. A literal execution would look like a *successful* edit — four of five layers rendering correctly — while deleting a layer that works today under the description of an improvement. **Leave `ice` on `whg-ne-basic`:** ice shelves at 1:10m have no coastal border to disagree with, which is this issue's entire premise, so they lose nothing by staying. (Adding `natural=glacier` and rebuilding is possible — a re-filter plus re-assembly plus re-tile — but it buys sharper Antarctic ice that serves no stated criterion. SG's call if ever wanted.)

✅ **For the four that DO move, this is a change of `source` ONLY — do not rename `source-layer`.** Verified against the live style and the built tileset's declared `vector_layers`:

```
style layer     source-layer   in new water.mbtiles?
ocean        →  ocean          yes — source change only
lakes        →  lakes          yes — source change only
river        →  rivers         yes — source change only  ⚠️ layer id is `river`, source-layer is `rivers`
water_shadow →  ocean          yes — source change only  (styling layer drawn off the ocean source-layer)
ice          →  ice            ⚠️ NO — leave on whg-ne-basic
```

⚠️ The `river` / `rivers` mismatch is a live trap: the **layer id** is singular and the **source-layer** is plural, and a probe for a layer named `rivers` returns "absent" for something that is present and working.

## 3. ⚠️ Two DEM sources declared; no layer uses either

`terrarium-local` (mbtiles, z0–8) and `terrarium-aws` (S3, z9–15) are both declared. **No `hillshade` layer exists and there is no `terrain` block.** Whatever relief texture the map has is not coming from this style.

**Fix:** add one `hillshade` layer, `terrarium-local` below z9 and `terrarium-aws` above. This is the texture that replaces the faded raster, and it **cannot mis-register** — relief is elevation-derived and has no coastline. Demonstrated in `tileboss/demo/backdrop-comparison.html`.

## 4. ⚠️ The raster is drawn four zoom levels past its own data

`basemap` source declares `maxzoom: 6`; the **layer** sets neither `maxzoom` nor an opacity ramp. From z7 up MapLibre upscales z6 tiles indefinitely — the blur visible in the demo's left pane.

**Fix:** `raster-opacity: ["interpolate",["linear"],["zoom"], 5, 1, 7.5, 0]`. Keep Natural Earth where it is excellent — the world view — and stop it where it is only noise.

## 5. Everything stops at z10, and z10+ is the worst view in the style

All 30 `osm`/`ohm`/gazetteer layers carry `maxzoom: 10`. Above z10 there is **no vector content at all**, leaving only the raster at its most upscaled. The map is at its least accurate exactly where users zoom to inspect a place.

**Judgement call:** the tilesets themselves are z0–10, so there is no more data — but MapLibre overzooms vector tiles cleanly, and boundary lines survive it well. **Consider removing `maxzoom` from the line/fill layers** so geometry persists above z10 rather than vanishing. Labels are a separate decision — overzoomed labels can collide.

**This reinforces the water import rather than competing with it.** Water and admin would both be z0–10, so above z10 they overzoom *identically* and stay aligned — the mismatch does not reappear, it simply scales. That holds **only while the two maxzooms match**, which is the coupling again: do item 5 and the water import together, and move both maxzooms together or neither.

## 6. `osm` and `ohm` are twenty near-identical layers

Layers 7–26 are the same five-band ladder (continental / country / state / district / local, line + label) written twice with one word changed. It works, and it is exactly the shape that drifts: a change to one ladder silently not applied to the other.

**Judgement call:** the band structure is already declared in `metadata.whg:tilegen.buckets` and consumed by `processing.tilegen_bands`. Generating these twenty layers from that same block — the way the tile builder reads it — would make the style and the tiler provably agree instead of agreeing by convention. Not urgent; worth doing when the ladder next changes.

## Proposed draw order

```
background
basemap raster        opacity ramp 1 -> 0 across z5-7.5, maxzoom 6
hillshade             terrarium-local <z9, terrarium-aws >=z9      <- NEW
ice / rivers          whg-ne-basic <z8, water source >=z8
gazetteer fills       osm_misc, po, clio, nl        (unchanged opacities)
ocean / lakes         MOVED ABOVE the fills — water is ground truth  <- MOVED
boundary lines        osm + ohm ladders
labels                osm + ohm ladders
```

**One caveat on the reordering:** it makes water opaque over the gazetteers, so a polity that legitimately extends over water — a thalassocracy, a maritime claim, an island polity's territorial extent — will be clipped visually. If that is wanted, water should instead go above the fills at reduced opacity, or the fills should be split into land and marine classes. **That is a cartographic decision, not an engineering one, and it should be SG's.**
