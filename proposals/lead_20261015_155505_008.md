# Proposal: buying bitcoin peer-to-peer in Lightning Piggy

*Prepared 2026-10-15 for Ben Weeks, in reply to the 2026-10-11 request "a proposal for buying Bitcoin P2P (Peach, RoboSats, Nostr-native)".*

## TL;DR

Do this in three phases. **Phase 1** is a "Get bitcoin" guide with deep links out to existing apps. It is small, low risk, and adults only. **Phase 2** is a **buy-only Mostro client** built on NIP-69 order events. Mostro already uses Nostr, Lightning hold-invoice escrow and Nostr Wallet Connect (NWC), so it fits our stack better than any other option. It is medium-to-large, about 5 to 7 PRs, and should wait for legal sign-off. **Never** run an order book, take custody of escrow, or touch fiat. Hide the whole feature from child profiles.

**NIP-69** ([spec](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/69.md)) is the shared standard. It defines kind 38383 addressable order events with tags for `k` (buy or sell), `f` (ISO 4217 currency), `amt`, `fa`, `pm` (payment method), `premium`, `layer`, status `s` (`pending`, `in-progress`, `success`, ...) and `expires_at`. Mostro, LNP2PBot, RoboSats and Peach are all listed as publishers, so one NIP-69 reader could in principle show a combined order book. Trade execution still differs per platform, though.

**Correction to the brief on chat transport:** Mostro **protocol v2 uses NIP-44 kind-14 messages**. The older NIP-59 gift-wrap transport (v1) is now **deprecated**, and each node advertises which version it speaks in its kind 38385 info event ([protocol](https://mostro.network/protocol/)). Our NIP-17 and NIP-44 code still applies, but we should target v2 and not build on gift wraps.

## 2. Regulatory and store exposure

- **UK FCA financial promotions.** Since 2023, any communication that invites UK consumers to buy cryptoassets is a "financial promotion". It must come from, or be approved by, an authorised firm, and third parties that publish illegal promotions can also be in breach ([FCA s21 approvers](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation/s21-approvers/printable/print); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/fca-publishes-final-rules-and-supplementary-draft-guidance-on-financial-promotions-for-cryptoassets2)). The FCA published its final rules for the full crypto regime on 30 June 2026, effective for firms authorised from **25 October 2027**. "Arranging deals" in crypto is likely to become a permissioned activity. Practical effect:
  - **Neutral, educational wording** ("ways people get bitcoin, with the risks") is lower risk than "Buy now on X" buttons, prices or premiums.
  - An **embedded order book** that matches UK users with sellers is close to "arranging". Get UK fintech counsel to sign off before Phase 2 ships to UK users, and consider geo-gating it.
- **Apple 3.1.5(iii)**: apps that "facilitate transactions ... on an approved exchange" may only do so where the developer has "appropriate licensing". **5.1.1(ix)**: crypto exchange apps must be submitted by a legal entity ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). A P2P trade client is likely to be reviewed as exchange functionality. Expect rejection risk on iOS, so plan an Android-first rollout and a feature flag per platform.
- **Google Play.** The Cryptocurrency Exchanges and Software Wallets policy requires FCA registration for UK-targeted exchanges. Non-custodial wallets are exempt ([Play help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16329703); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/335134/google-non-custodial-wallets-exempt-new-crypto-app-rules-play-store)). The Financial Features Declaration will need updating. Adding P2P trading could move us out of the "non-custodial wallet" category.
- **Kids.** Under-18s should not be trading with strangers or sending fiat to them. Our age rating must stay honest (Apple 2.3.6), and the feature needs a hard gate (see §4).

## 3. Recommended phases

### Phase 1: "Get bitcoin" guide (small, 1 to 2 PRs)

- A static, educational screen under Wallet, titled "Ways to get bitcoin". It covers earning, asking family, P2P apps and regulated UK exchanges, each with a plain-English note on risk and custody.
- **Deep links out** to Mostro, Peach, RoboSats and Bisq through app-store or website URLs. Show no prices, premiums or "buy" CTAs.
- A **"Receive into this Piggy"** helper that creates a Lightning invoice, a Lightning address or an on-chain address, so the user can paste it into the external app. Boltz can then move funds to on-chain if needed.
- Adult profiles only. Hidden behind a remote feature flag.

### Phase 2: in-app Mostro buy flow (medium-to-large, about 5 to 7 PRs)

Start **buy-only** and **Android-first**:

1. `mostroService`: subscribe to kind 38383 orders and kind 38385 node info. Filters must be bounded (`limit` plus `since`), coalesced through `useCoalescedMap`, and armed with `useFocusEffect`.
2. Node picker with a curated allow-list of trusted Mostro nodes, showing fees and disputes from each node's info event.
3. Order book UI: GBP sell orders, filtered by payment method (Faster Payments, Revolut), with reputation shown.
4. Take-order flow using protocol v2 messages (NIP-44 kind 14) and a **fresh per-trade key** so the user's main npub isn't linked to trades. The buyer only needs NWC `make_invoice`.
5. Trade chat and status timeline, reusing our NIP-44 and DM components. Includes the "fiat sent" action and dispute entry.
6. Safeguards (parental gate, limits, warnings) and Maestro flows on a signet or regtest Mostro node.
7. (Optional, later) Sell side. This needs a wallet that can pay and hold a long-lived HTLC.

**How this fits our NWC wallets.** The brief assumed the user's wallet must support hold invoices. It doesn't: **the Mostro node creates the hold invoice, not the user's wallet.** NIP-47 has no `make_hold_invoice` method anyway, only an `accepted` state in `lookup_invoice` ([NIP-47](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/47.md)). So:
- **Buyers** just need `make_invoice`, which every NWC wallet we support already has. That makes a buy-only flow compatible with all of them.
- **Sellers** must *pay* a hold invoice that may stay unresolved for hours. The NWC `pay_invoice` call will time out while the payment is still in flight, so we would need to reconcile with `lookup_invoice` or `list_transactions`. Alby Hub and other self-hosted nodes (LND, CLN or LNbits on black-panther) handle this well. Custodial or LSP-backed wallets vary and need a test matrix before we offer selling.

**Main risks**
- Regulatory: "arranging deals", app-store rejection.
- Thin GBP liquidity: users see empty order books.
- Fiat chargeback scams aimed at sellers, and fraud aimed at naïve buyers.
- Trust in node operators.
- Extra relay and crypto load on our single JS thread. Follow the performance rules, and remember this sits on the hot path that epic #1036 is meant to fix.
- Protocol churn: v1 to v2 already happened.

### Phase 3: what NOT to do

- No **custodial order book**, no escrow held by Lightning Piggy, no running our own Mostro node for the public. That makes us the "arranger" and a target for disputes.
- Never hold, route or reconcile **fiat**, and never act as a payment agent.
- No price feeds or "best rate" marketing aimed at UK users without an s21-approved promotion.
- No KYC data collection (we don't need it, and it is a liability).
- No P2P for child profiles, even with parental approval to buy on the child's behalf. Parents buy in their own profile, then send to the child's Piggy.

## 4. Safeguards for kids' accounts

- **Hidden entirely** for child profiles: no menu entry, no deep link, and the route is guarded in `AppNavigator`.
- The adult gate requires a parent-account signer and, on first use, a self-declared age of 18+ plus a risk acknowledgement.
- Per-trade and daily GBP caps on the parent's side (configurable, with conservative defaults), plus warnings about chargeback scams.
- Kids receive bitcoin the way they already do: a parent sends sats to the child's Piggy, optionally with a NIP-17 note ("pocket money").
- Audit trail: the trade history stays on the device and never goes to public relays beyond the protocol minimum.
