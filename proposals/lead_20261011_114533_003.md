# Project Proposal — Structural Drawings

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Damage tracking and partial redraw: from nami signals to partial present**

*Project description:*
## Summary

Hydrolysis + Cherenkov redraw the whole surface for any change. A blinking caret, a spinner or one changed label costs the same GPU fill, bandwidth and present as a full-screen animation. This issue proposes end-to-end damage tracking: nami signal changes produce screen-space damage in Hydrolysis, Cherenkov redraws only that damage into retained contents, and the host presents only the damaged region where the platform supports it. A WebRender-style tile cache comes later on the same plumbing.

This is the "damage tracking / partial present" item of the Cherenkov roadmap (#2194) and the bit-exact engine damage contract stated in #1601, written as one concrete, staged plan. No existing issue covers it end to end.

All code references are on `dev` @ `a54589ef9`.

## Motivation

- **Power and bandwidth on idle and small-change frames.** On tile-based mobile GPUs, memory traffic is the main power cost (#2192). A full-surface redraw for a 1–2% change writes and resolves the whole target. On Android, where on-chip blending is not shipped yet (#2191), that traffic is not even partly hidden. The engine target is `Rgba16Float` (`gpu/src/render/mod.rs:147`), so each redrawn pixel costs twice as much as with an 8-bit target.
- **f16 is staying for Liquid Glass windows.** #2556 plans to keep windows that use Liquid-Glass-style materials in f16 + EDR for the whole session. For those windows the pixel savings cannot come from a smaller format, only from not redrawing pixels.
- **WebRender's lesson.** WebRender started as "re-render everything each frame", and still added picture caching because "even if WebRender is fast enough to render complex content every frame at 60fps, there are valuable power optimizations we could get from not redrawing some things continuously" ([Mozilla Gfx, 2018][wr-pc]). Integrating picture caching with DirectComposition was later described as "a very important part" of shipping WebRender on Intel Windows devices, "yielding big performance and power usage improvements" ([moz://gfx #52][nl52]).
- **The alternative to heuristic layerization.** The same post explains why Gecko's cc-style layerization was dropped: "figuring these things out takes longer than it would have taken to just re-draw everything", "creating and destroying layers is very costly", and the heuristics "are hard to maintain" ([wr-pc]). Damage tracking plus content-hashed tiles gets the pixel savings with a simple cost model. Many content-sized system layers chosen by heuristics are not needed for that. #2556 can then stay focused on format, dynamic range and the system-compositor hand-off.

## Current state

**Cherenkov has no damage rects.**
- The tests state it directly: "a changed surface lowers again in full (the engine has no damage rects for this to leak through)" (`cpu/tests/backdrop.rs:1736-1737`, `gpu/tests/backdrop.rs:2536-2537`), and "the whole surface re-renders, not a damage rect" (`cpu/tests/backdrop.rs:1768`).
- The dirty unit is a whole surface. `render` collects surfaces with `sf.changed || wants_redraw` (`gpu/src/render/mod.rs:4243-4246`). Each dirty surface is then lowered and uploaded in full (`mod.rs:4282-4326`).
- Two cheap paths exist:
  - A frame with nothing dirty submits nothing (`gpu/tests/review.rs:446`, "nothing dirty, nothing submitted").
  - When the only change is new external frames on promoted planes, lowering and drawing are skipped (`mod.rs:4249-4268`).
- Scissor rects come from each pass's region, not from damage (`mod.rs:8305`, `mod.rs:8612`, `resolve.rs:62-66`).
- No `buffer_age`, incremental present or swap-with-damage exists anywhere in the engine or hosts. Our wgpu fork (`water-rs/wgpu@11e8595`) has none either. Its DX12 path calls `Present`, not `Present1`.

**The record crate already knows which commands changed.**
- `DisplayList::apply` returns command-level `Dirty` ranges (`record/src/display_list.rs:229-260`, `462-532`).
- Lowering patches only the dirty command spans (`src/lowering.rs:145-160`).
- This saves CPU only. The GPU still redraws the whole surface.

**Static layers can leave the engine.** `Recorded` leaf layers are promoted to system-compositor planes after a quiet period. The wait starts at two frames and is capped at `QUIET_LIMIT = 8` (`gpu/src/render/planes/static_layer.rs:38-41`). This is the only current way to avoid redrawing static pixels, and it is coarse (whole layer, small plane budget).

**Hydrolysis marks nodes but still flushes everything.**
- A nami signal change marks the node that read it (`renderer/signals.rs:339-343`).
- The dirty bits are `STRUCTURE` / `LAYOUT` / `PAINT` / `PLACE` / `PRODUCER` / `COMMIT` (`renderer/mount/cell.rs:5-9`, `35-53`).
- However, "any mark still runs the full flush" (`signals.rs:341-342`): Hydrolysis "redraws its whole window scene on every frame it runs … none of this is damage tracking" (`renderer/tests/gpu_surface_idle.rs:3-4`). Recording "runs every frame" (`renderer/tree/flush.rs:11-12`), and "the refresh path now runs full layout every frame" (`renderer/frame.rs:286-288`).
- Only `RetainedSubview` does a partial descent (`renderer/tree/nodes.rs:451-455`, `renderer/frame.rs:515-519`).
- Layers are re-recorded wholesale through `tx[layer].record(...)` (`renderer/mount/layers.rs:1541-1544`). No region is passed to Cherenkov.
- The unused dirty-node and dirty-collection sets were removed in #2381 and #2402. The node `Dirty` bits are the remaining foundation.

## Proposal (staged)

Each stage is useful on its own and lands behind the same correctness rule.

**Correctness rule (all stages):** damage tracking is an invisible optimization. A frame rendered with damage, caching and partial present on must be **bit-identical** to the same frame rendered with them off. This is the wording of #2194 and #1601. CI checks it by rendering every golden and incremental scene twice, partial and full, and comparing the bytes, across multi-frame sequences rather than single frames.

### 1. Hydrolysis: incremental flush that produces damage

- Make the node `Dirty` bits drive the flush. Re-record only subtrees marked `PAINT`/`STRUCTURE`, re-run layout only along `LAYOUT` chains, and apply `PLACE` as layer-property edits. Generalise the `RetainedSubview` partial descent (including the material-group scope replay from #2268) to any marked node.
- Emit, per layer, the changed command ranges (the record crate's `Dirty`) and the **screen-space damage**: `old_bounds ∪ new_bounds` for every changed command or moved layer, after transform and clip.
- Inflate damage by each effect's footprint:
  - blur radius;
  - shadow offset plus blur;
  - stroke and antialiasing margin;
  - filter kernels.
- **Backdrop materials propagate damage upward.** If damage intersects the region a glass element samples, that element's whole output rect becomes damaged. Flutter hit the same problem in its partial-repaint work and handles backdrop filters with a "readback region" that is repainted whole when anything inside it changes ([flutter/engine#28801][fl-28801]).

### 2. Cherenkov: accept damage and redraw only it

- API: per surface, take damage rects from the host, or derive them inside the engine from `Dirty` command ranges plus command bounds (deriving it inside the engine keeps the contract invisible to callers).
- Keep the previous frame's contents: a retained target, or the swapchain image when its buffer age is known (stage 3).
- Restrict passes to damage: scissor to the damage rects, `LoadOp::Load` instead of `Clear`, and cull instances and passes that do not intersect damage. Isolation, filter and backdrop passes are clipped to the inflated damage, not to their full `pass.region`.
- Damage must stay conservative. When in doubt (resize, scale or headroom change, GPU replacement, first frame, plan change), mark the whole surface.

### 3. Partial present per platform, with full present as the fallback

| Platform | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Android (Vulkan) | `VK_KHR_incremental_present` (`VkPresentRegionsKHR`). The extension exists so "the presentation engine [can] avoid wasting time presenting parts of the surface that have not changed" ([spec][vk-ip]). |
| Android (SurfaceControl planes) | `ASurfaceTransaction_setDamageRegion` (API 29+): "If unspecified, the complete surface is assumed to be damaged" ([NDK][ndk-sc]). Java equivalent: `SurfaceControl.Transaction.setDamageRegion`, intended for cases "such as for a small blinking cursor or a loading indicator" ([SDK][sdk-sc]). |
| GLES / EGL (Linux, old Android) | `EGL_KHR_swap_buffers_with_damage` for surface damage (a compositor hint; the whole back buffer must still be valid) ([spec][egl-swd]). `EGL_EXT_buffer_age` / `EGL_KHR_partial_update` to learn how old the back buffer's contents are and repair only what changed since then ([buffer_age][egl-age], [partial_update][egl-pu]). |
| Windows (DXGI flip model) | `IDXGISwapChain1::Present1` with `DXGI_PRESENT_PARAMETERS` dirty rects. The app "must update every single pixel in each rectangle" and "must not update any pixel outside of the dirty rectangles" ([docs][dxgi-pp], [flip model][dxgi-flip]). |
| Apple (`CAMetalLayer`) | No present-with-damage API ([gfx-rs/wgpu#2869][wgpu-2869] lists it as unsupported). Partial updates on Apple come from redrawing only damaged pixels in a retained target (stage 2) and from splitting static content into `CALayer` + IOSurface tiles (stage 4 / #2556), which is how Firefox's native compositor maps picture-cache tiles onto Core Animation ([bug 1617498][bz-1617498]). |

wgpu exposes none of this today. `Surface::present()` takes no damage, [gfx-rs/wgpu#682][wgpu-682] (present with damage region) was closed without an API, and the extended presentation API discussion [gfx-rs/wgpu#2869][wgpu-2869] is still open. So stage 3 needs either a fork patch on the Vulkan, DX12 and GLES HALs (to pass rects through `present`) or a host-side present path. Buffer-age queries need the same treatment.

### 4. Tile cache (WebRender-style picture caching)

- Split each surface (or each large slice of it) into fixed-size tiles. Each tile keeps a dependency/content hash of the primitives, clips, images, transforms and opacity bindings that touch it. A tile is invalidated only when its hash changes. The union of invalidated sub-regions gives a per-tile dirty rect, used as the scissor and as partial-present input. This is WebRender's design ([`picture.rs` docs][wr-picture-rs], which describe fixed-size 2048×512 tiles). WebRender also moved invalidation to post-transform raster space to stop spurious invalidation from scrolling and snapping ([bug 2018498][bz-2018498]). We should start in device space for the same reason.
- Static tiles can map onto system-compositor layers (`CALayer` + IOSurface, child `SurfaceControl`). WebRender's `Compositor` trait does this: "picture cache slices will be composited by the OS compositor", with dirty rects passed so that compositors that support partial present can "skip copying unchanged areas" ([docs.rs][wr-compositor]). Which tiles or groups hand off, and in which format and dynamic range, belongs to #2556. This issue supplies the invalidation and dirty-rect side.
- Video and other large, frequently changing content can stay as separate compositor surfaces, so it never invalidates tiles (WebRender's "compositor surfaces"; #2185).

### 5. Instrumentation

- Per-frame metric: damaged pixels / surface pixels, tiles invalidated, passes culled. Report it in `engine.stats()` and the perf harness.
- Debug overlay that tints damage rects and invalidated tiles per frame, plus an "invalidation reason" log like WebRender's `InvalidationReason`.

## Hard parts

- **Large-footprint effects.** Blur, shadows and backdrop materials expand small changes into large damage. Glass over animated content can make most of the window dirty. Damage must be inflated correctly, never under-estimated. Flutter shipped an artifact bug when an image filter's footprint was missed ([flutter/engine#28801][fl-28801]).
- **Full-screen animations** (transitions, scroll, parallax) make damage ≈ the whole surface. The design must cost almost nothing when damage is 100%, so it never loses to today's path. Scrolling may later want scroll-offset presentation (`DXGI` scroll rects) or tile reuse.
- **Retained-buffer memory.** A retained f16 target is one extra full-surface `Rgba16Float` texture per window (≈ 8 bytes/pixel). Measure it against the bandwidth saved, especially on Android. With buffer age, the swapchain image can be reused instead.
- **Swapchain buffer age.** With double or triple buffering, the back buffer is N frames old. The damage to repair is the union of the last N frames' damage, not just this frame's. Unknown age (0) means a full redraw. Some drivers are unreliable here: Flutter backed out partial repaint on Android/GLES after device-specific rendering bugs and said it would revisit it separately with the Vulkan Impeller backend ([flutter/flutter#123353][fl-123353]). We need an allow-list or capability probe and a kill switch.
- **On-chip tile blending (#2192).** Partial passes with `LoadOp::Load` read the attachment back from memory, while on-chip epochs prefer `Clear`/`DontCare` and memoryless intermediates. The composition planner has to treat damage-restricted passes as a first-class case. Small scissored passes should cost load bandwidth only for the tiles they cover, but this needs measuring on Apple and Mali.
- **External frames and producers.** `PRODUCER` updates on engine-composited layers damage their layer bounds. Plane-only updates already skip lowering (`mod.rs:4249-4268`) and add no engine damage.
- **Multiple windows and planes.** Damage is tracked per surface and per part. Promoting or demoting a plane damages the area it covered.

## Acceptance criteria

- Partial renders are bit-identical to full renders across the golden corpus and the incremental scene sequences, on GPU and CPU backends, enforced in CI.
- An idle frame with a blinking caret, or a single short text change in a typical app screen, redraws < 5% of surface pixels (from the damage metric) and presents with damage on platforms that support it.
- Measured GPU time and energy per frame drop for those scenarios on a Pixel (ODPM) and an iPhone (Power Profiler), with paired A/B rounds as in #2192.
- No regressions in the existing perf gates, including full-screen animation scenes, where the overhead of tracking damage must be within noise.
