## Reported finding

The parameter store reportedly contains **two record shapes** and supports:

- `BuiltInParameter` identifiers relevant to extrusion and revolution;
- shared-parameter name resolution through `PartAtom` GUIDs.

The report also identifies an exact-numeric trap: Revit reportedly computes internal feet as `mm / 25.4 / 12`, which can differ by **one ULP** from `mm / 304.8`. Exact-bit searches therefore need to try both encodings.

## Reported evidence

The two record layouts, specific `BuiltInParameter` numeric IDs, sample `PartAtom` GUID links, offsets, and raw bytes are not included in Discussion #112. They must be obtained or independently derived and reproduced; this issue must not invent them. The one-ULP relationship is a concrete reported oracle that can be checked independently with exact `f64` bit comparisons.

## Why it matters

Correct parameter-store decoding would help associate geometry-driving values with elements and resolve shared-parameter names. The floating-point distinction matters because approximate arithmetic can make a valid value invisible to exact-bit probes, while normalizing the two encodings too early can destroy useful provenance.

This overlaps the capability goal in [#35](https://github.com/DrunkOnJava/rvt-rs/issues/35), but is not a duplicate: #35 tracks end-user parameter exposure, while this issue tracks independent reproduction of the reported **wire shapes, identifier/GUID linkage, and bit-exact unit encoding**.

## Independent verification still required

- Identify and distinguish the two record shapes from allowed byte evidence.
- Derive record boundaries, field widths, endianness, and type/version gates.
- Verify the relevant `BuiltInParameter` IDs rather than importing unspecified numbers.
- Demonstrate shared-parameter name resolution through `PartAtom` GUIDs on at least one independent sample.
- Compare exact `f64::to_bits()` results for both millimetre-to-feet expressions and locate both forms in representative data.

The hypothesis would be falsified or narrowed if the shapes cannot be distinguished consistently, GUID resolution does not hold across samples, or exact values follow a different conversion path.
