# Project Proposal — Structural Drawings

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**PRD: Papercut art pass for the Forest (3/4 view, dark lighting, juice, paper HUD)**

*Project description:*
## Problem Statement

The game plays well, but it looks like a prototype. Everything is drawn with flat circles and rectangles: the player is an orange disc, a goblin is a coloured circle, a tree is a green circle with a brown ring, and the HUD is squares and bars. There is no sense of place, no character in the enemies, and little feedback when you hit something. You can't tell a marsh from a grove except by tint. Nothing about the screen makes someone want to play.

I want striking, distinctive graphics that give the game its own identity. I also want hits and kills to feel punchy, while the playfield stays as readable as it is today.

## Solution

Give the game a **papercut vector** look in a **dark forest lit by pools of light**, starting with floor 1 (the Forest).

- Every sprite looks cut from layered paper. Each shape casts a soft drop shadow down and to the right onto the layer beneath it. There is a faint paper grain and slightly irregular cut edges.
- The camera is **3/4 top-down**, like Binding of Isaac. Characters show their faces and walls show their front faces.
- Characters animate at a **stop-motion** 8–10 fps: 2 idle frames, 4 move frames, 3 attack frames and a 1-frame hurt pose.
- Rooms are dark, with a vignette. The player, projectiles and glowshrooms light the area around them, warming and brightening the colours, and the lights flicker gently. Shadows always fall the same way; the lights never move them.
- Projectiles become glowing paper shapes. Telegraphs keep their current timing and the existing "one telegraph, no tints, no mid-attack warnings" rule, restyled only.
- Juice:
  - enemies flash white on hit, with a short hit-stop
  - screen shake, capped so it stays readable
  - enemies burst into torn paper scraps on death
  - floating dust or fireflies and breathing light pools
- The HUD joins the style: paper hearts, a cut-out key and bomb, the minimap on a paper scrap, and a matching boss bar.

All art is authored as SVG in code, with no asset packs, no AI image generation and no licensing. Phaser bakes the SVGs into textures at load time.

**Process:** before anything goes into the game, one forest room is drawn as a static mockup page (tiles, dressing, player, 2–3 forest enemies, the Treant, the HUD). The style is iterated there until it is approved, then built into the game.

**Gameplay is untouched.** Hitboxes, physics bodies, collisions and all timings stay exactly as they are. Sprites are anchored at their feet, so the 3/4 art can rise above the body.

## Implementation Decisions

**Art direction (settled)**
- Papercut vector with layers and subtle grain: soft offset drop shadows, faint grain, slightly irregular edges. No heavy torn edges and no sticker borders.
- 3/4 top-down perspective. Facing uses a left/right mirror flip plus separate up and down drawings where needed.
- Fixed shadow offset (down-right). Lights only brighten and warm; they never cast or move shadows.
- Frames per character: idle 2, move 4, attack 3, hurt 1, played at ~8–10 fps.
- Projectiles are glowing paper shapes that act as light sources. Telegraph timing and the one-telegraph rule are unchanged.
- The first pass covers floor 1 (Forest) completely: player, goblin, seed spitter, boar, wasp, Treant, all forest tiles and sub-theme looks, dressing, pickups, passive visuals, HUD. Lighting and juice apply on all floors.
- All art is authored as SVG in code by the developer (Claude). No external assets.

**Modules**

1. **Paper art library (pure, no Phaser).**
   - Papercut drawing pieces: a layered shape, an offset shadow, a grain filter, an edge roughener, a palette.
   - A sprite catalogue keyed by entity kind. Each entry declares its actions, their frame counts and its foot anchor, and produces an SVG string for any (kind, action, frame, variant) combination.
   - Deterministic: the same input always gives the same SVG.
   - Also covers tile pieces, dressing, pickups and HUD icons.
2. **Animator (pure).**
   - Input: an entity's visual state (idle / moving / attacking / hurt), its velocity or aim, and the time.
   - Output: current action, frame index and facing.
   - The hurt pose overrides the others for its duration. Frames advance at the set fps. Idle and move loop; attack plays once.
   - Enemies report their visual state from their existing core state (e.g. a slime's squash/hop, a seed spitter's wind-up), so tells keep their current timing.
3. **Texture baker (thin Phaser layer).** At boot, renders each catalogue SVG and packs the results into texture atlases with frame names. Supersamples so the art stays crisp when scaled.
4. **Visual layer.**
   - Each entity keeps its existing physics shape, which becomes invisible. A paper sprite follows it, anchored at the feet.
   - Draw order is sorted by foot y.
   - Hit-flash becomes a white flash (replacing today's alpha dip). Champions get a gold paper trim.
   - Anything with no catalogue entry keeps drawing its current shape (the fallback for Caves and Dungeon).
   - The enemy contract gains an optional way to report visual state. No gameplay fields change.
5. **Lighting (pure core plus a thin Phaser layer).**
   - The core holds a registry of light sources (player, player shots, enemy shots, glowshrooms, and later candles), a flicker model (time and seed → radius and intensity, within bounds) and vignette settings.
   - The Phaser layer generalises the Candle Witch's darkness render texture, where light is erased from the dark around each source, into room-wide darkness plus a warm tint.
   - The Candle Witch's existing darkness mechanic keeps working.
6. **Juice (pure core plus a thin Phaser layer).**
   - A hit-stop scheduler: overlapping freezes merge into a single freeze window instead of stacking.
   - A shake budget with a per-event amount and a global cap.
   - A paper-scrap burst generator: rng, position and palette → scrap specs (shape, velocity, spin, lifetime).
   - Ambient motes (dust or fireflies).
   - Visual randomness uses its own random stream, never the seeded gameplay random stream.
7. **Room skin.**
   - 3/4 walls with front faces, plus doors as paper arches and gates.
   - A pure autotile function: neighbour mask → piece, so ponds, tree clusters and thorn patches join up.
   - Sub-theme looks (grove/marsh/bramble) map to their own paper pieces.
   - Dressing placements from the existing dressing module are drawn as paper cutouts.
8. **HUD skin.** Paper hearts (full/half/container), key and bomb icons, the minimap on a paper scrap, and the boss bar restyled with its current behaviour and timings kept.

**Architecture**
- The floor theme data is still the single place for floor-specific looks. Each tile look gains an optional art key, and shapes remain the fallback.
- Art never feeds back into physics: hitboxes, body sizes and collision logic are unchanged.
- The style mockup is a standalone static page built from the same SVG output as the paper art library, so what gets approved is what ships.

## Testing Decisions

Good tests check external behaviour through each module's public interface, never internal structure. For example: "the goblin's move action has 4 frames and every frame is a valid SVG with the declared foot anchor", not "the helper called the shadow function twice". Tests are Vitest unit tests next to the pure modules in the core layer, following the existing pattern of one test file per module (e.g. the slime, dressing, stun and boss bar modules and their tests).

Tested modules:
- **Paper art library:** every catalogued kind declares idle 2 / move 4 / attack 3 / hurt 1, and every (kind, action, frame) produces well-formed SVG. The output is deterministic for the same input. Variants differ. Every forest tile look and sub-theme look has a catalogue entry.
- **Animator:** state + velocity + time gives the expected action, frame and facing. Frames advance at the set fps. Idle and move loop and attack plays once. Hurt overrides everything for its duration. Facing flips by horizontal direction and holds when standing still.
- **Lighting core:** flicker is deterministic per seed and stays within its bounds. The registry adds and removes sources. The player's light is always present.
- **Juice core:** overlapping hit-stops merge (the total freeze is never longer than the longest single request from that moment). Shake never exceeds the cap. Scrap bursts are deterministic per seed and the right size. Visual randomness does not advance the gameplay random stream.
- **Autotile and y-sort:** each neighbour mask gives the expected piece (isolated, edge, corner, inner). Draw order follows foot y, with ties broken consistently.

Not unit-tested (checked by the mockup review and by playing): the texture baker, the Phaser side of the visual layer, the Phaser side of lighting and juice, the room skin rendering and the HUD skin. The existing smoke script should still boot a run without errors.

## Out of Scope

- Paper art for the Caves and Dungeon floors and their enemies and bosses (Iron Maiden, Candle Witch, worm boss). Those floors keep today's shapes and get only lighting and juice. They will be a follow-up.
- Any gameplay change: hitboxes, speeds, timings, telegraphs, enemy behaviour, room generation.
- Shadows cast by lights.
- Smooth animation with more than 4 frames per action, and skeletal or rigged animation.
- Sound and music.
- Title, menu and end-screen art beyond matching colours.
- External or AI-generated assets.

## Further Notes

- Earlier playtest feedback still applies: rooms should feel dense rather than bare, so dressing should be generous. Attack visuals stay uncluttered, with one telegraph and no tints or mid-attack warnings.
- Performance matters: light sources and scrap particles should be pooled, and SVGs are baked once at boot, never per frame.
- The order is: approve the mockup → paper art library and texture baker → visual layer and animator (player first) → room skin → forest enemies → Treant → lighting → juice → HUD.
