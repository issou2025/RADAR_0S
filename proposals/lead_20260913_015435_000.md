# Project Proposal — Bim Modeling

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Revit: the terrain still renders as flat-shaded triangles after the TIN change**

*Project description:*
**Host:** Revit

## What is observed

Every triangle of the terrain surface renders with its own flat tint over the aerial photograph. The photograph itself is clearly present and sharp — trees, the river channel and track edges resolve — but the surface reads as a faceted mosaic rather than as ground.

Observed on a 2026-08-25 import in Revit 2025 at `b71ba2e`, and again on the re-import at `b3aa685`. Both a distant view and a close-up show it.

## Why this is a new issue rather than a reopening

An earlier diagnosis attributed the faceting to **degenerate Delaunay**: the point lattice was 285 by 284 at exactly 5.000 m, so every cell's four corners were cocircular and the triangulator picked slivers and fans by tie-break. Building the terrain from the surface DXF — adaptive vertices, in general position — was expected to resolve it.

**It did not.** The imports above are at and after that change. Whatever remains is a different mechanism, and the vertex-placement explanation is now ruled out by observation rather than by argument.

## What else is ruled out

**Not drape resolution.** The image is 4,730 by 4,710 px across 1,419 by 1,413 m, which is 0.30 m/px. At that density the photograph is not the limiting factor, and the facet edges do not follow image pixels. They follow triangle edges.

**Not placement.** The drape's four real-world texture properties are written and read back, and the import log reports them holding within tolerance.

## The lead, which is a lead and not a finding

Revit 2025's API carries two members the importer never calls, confirmed by reflection over `RevitAPI.dll`:

```
Void    Toposolid.SetSmoothedSurface(Document document, Boolean enable)
Boolean Toposolid.IsSmoothedSurfaceEnabled(Document document)
```

They are named for exactly this distinction: a toposolid rendered faceted per triangle versus smoothed across them. The importer creates the toposolid and never touches either, so whatever Revit's default is, it is a default nobody has chosen.

⚠ **Do not act on that without running it.** It is a plausible name, not a measurement. The sibling investigation that produced the ruled-out explanation above cost two sessions and an 8 to 17 minute import each, to exactly that kind of confidence.

## How to settle it cheaply

`TerrainProbeCommand` already rolls every transaction back and already runs against an imported model. The probe pattern that closed the drape-layering work applies unchanged: add an arm that reports `IsSmoothedSurfaceEnabled` on the imported terrain, calls `SetSmoothedSurface(true)`, and reads it back. Seconds per hypothesis instead of minutes, with nothing committed.

Worth answering in the same session: whether smoothing interacts with the mesh sanitiser, and whether the site subdivisions need the same call, since they are themselves toposolids.

## Definition of done

Either the terrain renders as ground rather than as a mosaic, or there is a decision on file naming the mechanism and saying why Revit will not do better. Measured, not argued.
