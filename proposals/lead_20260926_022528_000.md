# Project Proposal — Bim Modeling

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Revit 2025: the land-cover commit takes 30 minutes on a 1.4.0 bundle**

*Project description:*
**Host:** Revit 2025.4 (25.4.60.9), add-in built from the fix branch for #248 through a scratch harness. The harness imports into a fresh metric project with no window.

On the platform-built MPB 1.4.0 bundle from #248 (a free 1.99 km² metric order, UTM 12N, a 74,855-point terrain), the land-cover step's single commit took **1,782.5 s**: 18 subdivisions, almost 30 minutes. Revit reports "not responding" for all of that time. The step did commit, and nothing was lost.

The same layers in the other two hosts:

| Revit | Site-boundary subdivisions already on the terrain | Land-cover commit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2027, `main` install slot (#248's original import) | 0 (that step had rolled back) | 50.7 s |
| 2027, fix branch | 39 | 177.5 s |
| 2026, fix branch | 40 | 150.1 s |
| 2025, fix branch | 40 | 1,782.5 s |

The site boundaries themselves committed in 35.8 s in 2025. The water bodies (2) took 2.2 s after the land cover.

**What is known:**
- Throughout the commit, Revit 2025's journal shows it regenerating geometry: toposolid extrusion steps and boolean-operation diagnostics. It never shows failure processing. #248's change acts only in failure processing, so it is not the likely cause. That has not been proven: no 2025 import of this bundle from `main` has been run.
- The cost appears to grow with the subdivisions already on the terrain. The land-cover polygons overlap the site boundaries (Revit reported 19 overlaps in 2025 and 22 in 2027), and 2025 pays several times more for it than 2026 or 2027.
- The slow-step notice underestimates this badly. It tells the curator the land cover will be "as slow as the site boundaries were", which in 2025 means 36 s against 30 minutes.

**To settle:**
1. Is this `main`'s behaviour in 2025, independent of #248's change? Settle it with a 2025 import of this bundle from `main`: terrain, land use, land cover and water.
2. Which lever, if any, is acceptable? Clipping or merging published polygons is ruled out, because the add-in applies them verbatim. The remaining candidates are the order the polygon layers are cut in, and each layer's commit strategy. Choosing between them is a maintainer decision.
3. Whatever the answer, `SlowStepNotice`'s wording for the later polygon steps should stop promising the site boundaries' speed.
