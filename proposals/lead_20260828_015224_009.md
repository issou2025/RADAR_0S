## The cost of the two ways to encode inverse — measured on all three corpus brands

**Recommendation up front: keep B.** It is not close on any countable axis, and the one axis where A
genuinely wins is a gap B has not paid for yet rather than a cost A avoids. Details, then the falsifier
that would change the answer, then the one thing I am filing.

Two corrections to the brief's own framing land first, because both change what B costs:

- **"an extra collection" is not a cost of B.** Measured from the emission: 13 collections either way.
  The pointer collection exists under both schemes — #1027 settled that. A puts the inverse names *in*
  it; B puts them in a second *mode* of it. Nothing gains or loses a collection.
- **"behaviour Figma does NOT document" is withdrawn** — the owner's own correction, mid-measurement,
  and it is right: `resolveForConsumer` documents that an alias chain resolves using the selected modes
  of *each* collection in the chain, and `resolvedVariableModes` is typed `{ [collectionId]: string }`
  with a published two-collection worked example including ancestor inheritance. So B carries no
  undocumented dependency. No detector was filed and none is needed.

### Where each number comes from

Two independent sources, joined but never derived from each other:

- **K** comes from the **emission** — `out/figma/<brand>/color.surface.default.json` diffed against
  `color.surface.inverse.json` as data. No engine code runs.
- **M** comes from the **defs** — `figmaAnatomySet` over `componentDefs`, walking every node of every
  projected member. The A-side member count is enumerated a **second** time from the defs' own declared
  grid (`figmaProperties.variantAxes` × `stateAxis.values` × `slotAxes`) without calling the projector;
  all nine projecting defs agree, 973 = 973, which is the only reason I trust either.
- The join is `plan binding tail ∈ K set`, i.e. projector output against emitted files.

### 1. K — the flip count

| brand | pointer roles | K by target NAME | K by resolved VALUE | value-redundant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aurora | 128 | **112** | 106 | 6 |
| nb | 128 | **112** | 107 | 5 |
| wendys | 128 | **112** | 107 | 5 |

The 16 roles that do not flip are **identical on all three brands**: `icon/on-{brand,danger,info,success,warning}`,
`text/on-{brand,danger,info,success,warning}`, and the six `veil/{light,dark}/{body,enhanced,large}` — the
first ten are already "ink on a known fill" and the last six already name their own polarity.

The 112 flipping roles map to **112 distinct** inverse targets — injective, no collisions.

**The brands disagree, and the disagreement is the finding.** `K by resolved VALUE` differs (106 / 107 / 107)
because a handful of flips resolve to the same colour in all four appearance modes. Union of "redundant on at
least one brand" = **6**; intersection of "redundant on all three" = **4** (`border/danger`, `border/info`,
`border/warning`, `interactive/neutral/on-fill`). `border/brand` is redundant on aurora and nb but not wendys;
`border/success` on aurora and wendys but not nb.

So **A's token cost is 112 on every brand and cannot be pruned to K_value.** Pruning by value coincidence
would make the guaranteed name surface brand-dependent — a name present for wendys and absent for aurora
cannot sit in `guaranteed`, and a new brand could break any pruning decision by moving one hue.

**M is total. There is nothing to prune.**

- **2550 of 2551** colour bind sites are on the pointer tier, and **every one of them is on a flipping role.
  Zero bind sites land on a non-flipping role.**
- **29 of 29** parts that carry any colour bind a flipper.
- **8 of the 9** projecting defs are affected. The exception is `icon`, which binds no colour at all — its ink
  arrives from the host as a `descendantFills` override.
- Components bind **48 distinct** pointer roles of 128, and all 48 flip. The other 80 (64 flipping + all 16
  non-flipping) exist for hand-binding and for code.

**The alias chain was walked and came back empty**, which is worth stating because the brief asked for it. No
variable in any collection, on any of the three brands, aliases *into* `color.surface`; no component binds
`core` directly. The chain is `surface → appearance → core`, one hop from any binding, so there is no part that
reaches a flipper *through* a non-flipper. The one binding that is not on the pointer tier at all is
`focus-ring`'s — see below.

**The paste-time cost is worse than linear**, and this is the number I did not expect. Simulating A by cloning
each member with its flipping bindings rewritten to an inverse sibling and the coordinate extended:

|---|---|---|

Button's payload goes 1.69 MB → 4.83 MB, ×2.85 on ×2.00 members. **The driver is the replicated per-chunk
shell, not the member count**, and that is measured rather than reasoned: every chunk carries the whole set's
cell list, which grows 21.4 KB → 25.9 KB (52% → 63% of all bytes), so usable payload per chunk falls
20.6 KB → 16.1 KB. Isolating the two effects — a 1296-member set with a one-character axis name packs into
**107** chunks, and with the axis spelled `surface` also **107**, so name length is irrelevant; the extra 10
chunks in the 117 come from A's longer *binding* names. `SET_CHUNK_BYTES` is unchanged and nothing overflows;
the cost is 90 more postMessage round trips per paste.

### 3. B's cost, in the same units

| | A | B | B − A |
|---|---|---|---|
| collections | 13 | 13 | **0** |
| modes on the pointer collection | 1 | 2 | **+1** |
| distinct variables in the file | 823 | **711** | **−112** |
| variable-mode rows | 1615 | **1631** | **+16** |
| contract-guaranteed names added | **+112** | 0 | **−112** |
| projected variant members | 1905 | **973** | **−932** |
| paste payload chunks | 149 | **59** | **−90** |

**Per frame, B costs one mode selection on the container**, inherited by every descendant — the documented
`resolvedVariableModes` mechanism, and the same designer already sets an appearance mode on that container
under *both* schemes, so it is one extra dropdown in a panel they are already in. A costs nothing per frame and
one variant switch per instance; B can also be set per node when a single instance needs to differ, so the
per-instance action count is 1 either way. **The asymmetry is what happens to layers that are not component
instances**: a hand-drawn frame bound to `color/background/primary` inverts for free under B and must be
rebound token-by-token under A. That is not countable from the corpus — there are no design files — so it stays
qualitative.

B could also **delete** `focus-ring`'s redundant `color` axis (973 → 972), which A cannot.

### What genuinely favours A — stated plainly, because it is real

1. **B has no expression outside Figma, and A is already what code gets.** Measured: the DTCG emission carries
   the 128 pointer-tier leaves, every one hard-aliased to its **default** appearance target, and **zero**
   inverse leaves at that tier. There is no inverse overlay file; the `dark` / `hc-*` overlays contain zero
   pointer-tier leaves. Code reaches inverse only through the appearance tier's 113 inverse leaves — 104
   `guaranteed` + 9 `brandDependent` — and `.ai.json` documents exactly that, with
   `paired_with: [text.on-inverse.primary, border.inverse.default]` and an `avoid_when` at SHOULD level. So
   `prism.color.background.primary` means *default only* in code and *whatever the frame's surface mode says*
   in Figma, and no artifact states that split. Filing it (below).
2. **`focus-ring` already ships A**, and inconsistently with B: `color=default` binds
   `color/border/focus` (pointer tier) while `color=inverse` reaches past it to
   `color/appearance/border/inverse/focus`. Its own header calls this "the honest shape of a def that reaches
   past the layer it should be using" and records the redundancy as exact — the alias row for `border.focus`
   already reads `default → border.focus`, `inverse → border.inverse.focus`. It survives only because
   `figmaPropertyErrors` rejects `variantAxes: []`, so deleting the binding would un-project the def (#1028).
   Not re-filed; #871/#1028 already own it.
3. **Qualitative, and not dressed in a number:** an inverse token name says what it is at the call site,
   whereas a mode says nothing until you select an ancestor and read a dropdown. All four surveyed systems
   chose the name. Under B, "why is this button white?" is an ancestor hunt.

None of the three touches the 932-member / 90-chunk / 112-permanent-name differential, and (1) is a gap in
B's *coverage* rather than a cost A escapes — closing it costs one more overlay file per brand with 112
overrides, using machinery that already exists and ships four times over.

### Recommendation: keep B

The saving the owner hoped for is real and much larger than the cost. B buys back 932 variant members, 90
paste chunks and 112 permanent contract names, for one extra mode, 16 extra variable-mode rows, and one
dropdown on a container the designer is already configuring. The switching window is genuinely open today and
I would still not use it.

**What would change the answer, since a result favouring what we shipped is the one to distrust:**

- If M were partial rather than total. It is 2550/2551 with zero pruning available; if a later def bound
  mostly non-flipping roles, A's variant axis would apply to fewer members and the differential would shrink
  toward the 112-name cost alone.
- If the 112 names were free. They are not — they are permanent, they duplicate 112 names already guaranteed
  one tier down, and they would give every inverse value two public spellings, which is the thing #1027's
  pointer tier exists to avoid.
- If Figma's variant cap were the binding constraint. It is not: 1296 is 32% of 4,000.
- **The one live risk to B is finding 1**, and it is a reason to close a gap, not to switch: if the decision
  were that inverse must be a *code* concept as well as a Figma one, then A's names have to exist for
  consumers regardless — at which point paying for B's mode *as well* needs its own justification. Today they
  do exist, at the appearance tier, and are documented; what is missing is anything saying so on purpose.

---

**Filing separately** (found by this measurement, not fixed here, per principle 3): the inverse mechanism
differs between Figma and code and nothing records that as a decision. `prism.color.background.primary` is
surface-responsive in Figma (128 roles × 2 modes) and hard-aliased to the default appearance target in the
DTCG — 128 pointer leaves, 0 inverse leaves at that tier, no inverse overlay file, and
`skills/prism3-consume/SKILL.md` mentions `surface` seven times and `inverse` **zero** times. Either an
inverse overlay closes it (112 overrides per brand, same shape as the four existing overlays) or the
Figma-only scope is stated where a consumer will read it.
