# Project Proposal — Flutter App

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Preparation is raw or cooked; a preparation that changes the food is its own item**

*Project description:*
## Problem Statement

The catalogue's variant selector is supposed to answer one question: *how was this
prepared?* Today it answers several questions at once, and gets two of them wrong.

A user searching **chicken breast** should find one item and pick between raw and
cooked. Instead the preparation wheel can offer `roasted`, `grilled`, `baked` and
`broiled` as four separate preparations — because the pipeline splits preparations
on macros, and two USDA records of the same cooked chicken disagree on their numbers
by more than raw and cooked disagree. The user is asked to choose between four things
that are the same thing, with no basis for choosing.

At the same time the wheel offers preparations that are not preparations at all.
**Fried chicken breast** is not a state chicken breast is in — it is a different food,
with flour and oil in it, and it belongs in the search list under its own name. So does
dried apricot, canned tuna, smoked salmon and condensed soup. Presenting them as
variants of the plain food hides them from search and makes the plain food's macros a
lie about what the user is about to log.

Third, the grouping over-merges. An item is every food sharing a `base_key`, and a box
of cake mix and the cake baked from it produce the same base name — so they become one
item, and a user logging a slice of cornbread can be shown the macros of the dry mix.
66 description stems in the corpus contain both a prepared and a non-prepared row.

## Solution

**A preparation is `raw` or `cooked`, and nothing else.** When the source does not say,
the item has no preparation and the app shows it as `plain`, exactly as it does today.

Every way of applying heat — roasted, grilled, baked, broiled, braised, stewed,
simmered, poached, steamed, microwaved, rotisserie — is `cooked`. They land in one
preparation regardless of how far apart their macros sit, because they are one answer to
the question the wheel asks. The macros shown for that preparation come from the most
generic member: a USDA row that calls itself literally "cooked" speaks for the
preparation ahead of one that calls itself "roasted".

**A treatment that changes what is *in* the food is not a preparation — it is a
different item.** Frying adds oil, drying removes water, canning adds brine or syrup,
breading adds flour. Those foods get their own row in the search list, under their own
name: *fried chicken breast*, *dried apricot*, *canned tuna*, *smoked salmon*. They may
themselves have preparations — cured bacon is sold raw and eaten cooked — so the two
axes are independent.

**A mix is a product, not a state.** A box of cornbread mix and the cornbread baked from
it are two items with two names, and neither can be reached by searching for the other's
macros.

## User Stories

1. As a user logging chicken, I want **chicken breast** to appear once in the search list, so that I am not asked to choose between four results that are the same food.
2. As a user picking a preparation, I want the wheel to offer **raw** and **cooked**, so that the choice matches the question I am actually answering.
3. As a user who grilled a chicken breast, I want to pick **cooked**, so that I do not have to know whether USDA recorded my cooking method.
4. As a user who baked a chicken breast, I want to pick the **same** preparation as the user who grilled one, so that identical foods log identically.
5. As a user logging cooked chicken breast, I want the macros to come from the record that describes cooked chicken breast generically, so that I am not silently shown one arbitrary cooking method's numbers.
6. As a user who ate fried chicken, I want **fried chicken breast** to be its own item in the search list, so that I can find it by typing what I ate.
7. As a user logging fried chicken, I want its macros to include the oil and coating, so that my day's fat total is not understated.
8. As a user, I want **dried apricot** to be a separate item from apricot, so that logging 30 g of dried fruit does not use fresh-fruit macros.
9. As a user, I want **canned tuna** to be a separate item from tuna, so that the brine and drained weight are reflected in what I log.
10. As a user, I want **smoked salmon** to be a separate item from salmon, so that its sodium is not hidden behind a preparation label.
11. As a user, I want **condensed soup** to be a separate item from the soup made from it, so that a 2× difference in concentration cannot be logged by accident.
12. As a user, I want **cornbread mix** and **cornbread** to be two items, so that logging a slice of bread does not use the macros of a box of powder.
13. As a user, I want **instant banana pudding mix** and **banana pudding** to be two items, for the same reason.
14. As a user searching for "fried", I want fried items to surface by name, so that the search box finds what I ate rather than the plain form of it.
15. As a user browsing an item with only one preparation, I want no wheel at all, so that a single-variant food does not look like a choice.
16. As a user browsing an item whose source never stated a preparation, I want it labelled **plain**, so that the app does not claim a fact it does not have.
17. As a user logging bacon, I want **cured bacon** to be one item offering raw and cooked, so that both the packet and the cooked rasher are reachable.
18. As a user, I want every grade of ground beef to remain one item, so that this change does not undo the fat-level grouping that already works.
19. As a user, I want raw and cooked never to merge into one preparation, so that a raw egg is never logged under the label cooked.
20. As a maintainer, I want `prep_type` to be derivable from a recorded preparation label rather than produced by the LLM, so that the value can be re-derived without another paid run.
21. As a maintainer, I want the preparation vocabulary the model may report to cover the words that actually occur in the corpus, so that the model is not forced to squeeze `pan-fried` or `rotisserie` into a wrong slot or drop it.
22. As a maintainer, I want a row that states several preparations to resolve deterministically, so that `Pork, cured, bacon, cooked, broiled` and `Pork, cured, bacon, unheated` cannot land in two different items.
23. As a maintainer, I want `breaded, fried` and `coated, fried` to produce the same identity word, so that two descriptions of the same food do not become two items.
24. As a maintainer, I want the identity fold to be deterministic in code rather than a prompt instruction, so that it cannot answer differently in two batches.
25. As a maintainer, I want the fold to be idempotent against a base name that already contains the word, so that a model answering both ways does not produce "fried fried rice".
26. As a maintainer, I want macros to have exactly one remaining job, so that the reason a preparation exists is always stateable in one sentence.
27. As a reviewer working the queue, I want the preparation shown for an item to be the one the app will show, so that reviewing the name means reviewing what ships.
28. As a maintainer, I want the change to require no data migration, so that it costs a canonicalization re-run and nothing else.
29. As a maintainer, I want the README's clustering section to describe the design that exists, so that the next reader is not told about a label-family merge that was deleted.
30. As a maintainer, I want the glossary's definition of Preparation to say raw or cooked, so that the domain language and the catalogue agree.
31. As a maintainer, I want the tests that this change invalidates rewritten rather than deleted, so that each keeps describing a real behaviour.
32. As an app developer, I want `prep_type` to keep arriving as a nullable string, so that no client change is required beyond a catalogue version bump.

## Implementation Decisions

### The composition test

A preparation is identity-forming when it changes what is *in* the food — water driven
out, or fat, coating, brine or syrup added. Heat alone is `cooked`. Storage and texture
states are not preparations at all. This test, not a list, is the rule; the table below
is its application to the vocabulary measured in the corpus.

### Configuration

`config` gains a single map from every reportable preparation token to its class, and a
precedence list. `PREP_PARENT` is deleted; `PREP_TYPES` shrinks to the two app-facing
values.

```
COOKED   cooked roasted baked boiled braised broiled grilled steamed stewed
         simmered poached scrambled microwaved sauteed blanched parboiled
         rotisserie toasted heated

RAW      raw unheated

IDENTITY (folds into the key, with its canonical word)
         fried pan-fried deep-fried stir-fried        -> fried
         breaded battered coated crispy               -> breaded
         dried dehydrated freeze-dried sun-dried      -> dried
         concentrate                                  -> concentrated
         smoked cured pickled fermented sprouted canned condensed instant
         brewed popped puffed powdered                -> unchanged

IGNORED  frozen refrigerated fresh drained strained mashed whipped melted
         reconstituted unprepared prepared ready-to-eat ready-to-serve
         ready-to-heat ready-to-drink ready-to-feed

PREP_TYPES = ("raw", "cooked")
```

`unprepared` is ignored rather than mapped to `raw`: a box of cake mix is not raw, and a
`frozen, unprepared` vegetable is placed by the macro pass instead.

Precedence, highest first — verified against every multi-token pair present in the
corpus (`canned+condensed` 43 rows, `coated+fried` 33, `breaded+fried` 22, `crispy+fried`
9, `canned+cured` 6, `cured+smoked` 6, `canned+smoked` 4, `canned+pickled` 4):

```
fried breaded brewed smoked cured pickled fermented sprouted popped puffed
condensed concentrated instant powdered dried canned
```

### Canonicalization (Stage 3b-1)

- `prep` becomes a **list** over the reportable vocabulary: report every preparation
  token the description states, rather than choosing one. 344 corpus rows state both an
  identity-forming and a cooking token (`Pork, cured, bacon, cooked, broiled`); a
  single-valued field forces a coin flip on those, and the coin flip splits items.
- New prompt rule: a **mix, powder or concentrate is a product in its own right**, so it
  stays in `base` (`instant banana pudding mix`); `prepared with X` means the row *is*
  the made food, so `base` names the made food (`banana pudding`). This is where the
  cake-mix over-merge is fixed — a `form` field in the grouping key would separate the
  rows but leave two items both displaying "banana pudding", which the duplicate-name
  audit exists to flag.
- The existing "words that are part of the dish's NAME" carve-out **stays**. For
  identity-forming words the two routes converge — `base="fried chicken breast", prep=[]`
  and `base="chicken breast", prep=["fried"]` both key as `breast chicken fried`, because
  `base_key` sorts a token *set*. For cooking-class words they do not converge
  (`baked beans` keys as `baked bean`; `beans` + `["baked"]` keys as `bean` with prep
  `cooked`), so the carve-out is load-bearing for `baked beans`, `refried beans` and
  `roast beef`.
- Batch ordering already places a mix adjacent to the row prepared from it, so one
  request sees both.

### Clustering (Stage 3b)

- **Fold**: the highest-precedence identity word in a row's preparation list prepends to
  `base_name`, and therefore to `base_key`. Guarded against duplication — never prepend a
  word already present in `base_name`. `food_kind` is untouched: separation comes from the
  key, so forcing `dish` is unnecessary and would cost the word its meaning (dried apricot
  and smoked salmon are not composed of several foods).
- **Bucketing**: `raw`, `cooked`, and unlabelled. Buckets never merge across classes.
  **Macros are not consulted within a class** — grilled and baked are one `cooked`
  preparation whatever their numbers say, which is the primary defect being fixed.
- **Macros keep exactly one job**: placing the unlabelled bucket into `raw`, into
  `cooked`, or leaving it as its own `plain` preparation. 249 of 2,438 description stems
  contain both labelled and unlabelled rows, so this is the common case, not an edge.
  `_prep_vector`, `_prep_distance`, `PREP_DISTANCE`, `PREP_FLOOR_G` and the `variable_fat`
  fat-free basis survive for that one call.
- **Representative selection** gains a leading sort term: a member whose label is
  literally `cooked` (or `raw`) outranks one labelled `roasted`, before the existing
  widest-panel / shortest-description / `fdc_id` order. This is what makes the app show
  generic cooked macros. It costs panel width where the two compete — median 92 nutrients
  on SR Legacy's `cooked, roasted` rows against 65 on a bare `cooked` row.
- **Deleted**: `PREP_PARENT`, the label-family compatibility check, and the widest-label
  computation. The label family is now the class.

### Storage and contracts

- `foods.prep_label` becomes a list-valued column; the write path and the re-derivation
  check that keys off it follow. The Stage 4 cross-check word list re-points at the new
  vocabulary.
- The canon response schema takes a list of enum values rather than a single nullable
  enum; the app-facing preparation literal shrinks to `raw` | `cooked`.
- **No data migration.** `base_name` is NULL on all 13,694 rows and both merged tables are
  empty, so a full canonicalization re-run is required regardless of this change.
- The exported catalogue's `prep_type` stays a nullable string, so no client change is
  needed beyond bumping the catalogue version after export.

### Accepted consequences

- The `prep_type IS 'cooked'` search bonus currently matches only items whose macros
  happened to merge; afterwards it matches nearly every cooked item, so it stops
  discriminating between items and applies broadly. This is closer to its stated intent
  (favour ready-to-eat foods) and is left alone.
- The full-text `prep` column becomes near-constant and contributes almost nothing to
  ranking. Queries like "fried" still match, through the item's name.
- `sprouted + stir-fried` (5 rows) folds to `fried` and loses `sprouted`, unless
  canonicalization keeps it in `base`.
- Precedence ranks `smoked` above `cured`, giving "smoked ham" rather than "cured ham".

## Testing Decisions

A good test here asserts what a user or the app would observe — which items exist, which
preparations they hold, what a preparation is called, and which row speaks for it. It
does not assert bucket ordering, intermediate label sets, or which internal helper ran.
Every existing clustering test is written this way and is the prior art to follow.

**Primary seam: `cluster.build_clusters(frame) -> (items, preps, links)`.** Pure, frames
in and frames out, already driven by the `_cluster_frame` helper in the pipeline tests.
The fold, precedence, the dedupe guard, raw/cooked bucketing, unlabelled placement and
representative choice are all observable through it. Its only shape change is the
helper's preparation field becoming a list.

New cases at that seam:

- Grilled and baked rows of one base, with deliberately distant macros, produce one item
  with one `cooked` preparation — the assertion that macros are not consulted.
- A `fried` row produces a separate item from the plain form, keyed on the folded word.
- `cured bacon, cooked, broiled` and `cured bacon, unheated` produce one item with two
  preparations — the fold and the preparation are independent axes.
- Precedence: `["breaded","fried"]` folds to `fried`; `["canned","condensed"]` folds to
  `condensed`.
- The dedupe guard: a base already reading `fried rice` with `["fried"]` reported does not
  gain the word twice.
- Representative: a bucket holding a thin generic `cooked` row and a wide `roasted` row
  selects the generic one.

Secondary seams, all existing, needing assertion updates rather than new machinery:
`cluster.base_key` for key normalization; the canon batch schema for the list contract and
enum rejection; `cluster.run(con)` with the existing DuckDB fixture for the list-column
round-trip, idempotency and re-queue behaviour; and the SQLite export test for `prep_type`
arriving as raw/cooked/NULL.

Four existing tests state behaviour this change reverses. Each is rewritten, not deleted,
because each still describes something real:

- raw and poached egg: the assertion becomes `raw` and `cooked`, and the reason changes
  from "poached has no parent" to "poached is a cooking method".
- unlabelled salmon joining a labelled preparation: the label it joins becomes `cooked`.
- the egg item holding raw, poached and fried: the fried egg leaves and becomes its own
  item.
- the export test's variant list: `boiled` becomes `cooked`.

No new seam is introduced. The identity fold is asserted through `build_clusters` rather
than exposed as its own function.

## Out of Scope

- Any change to how identity itself is decided. Identity remains a key — `base_key` plus
  `food_kind` — and no similarity score or macro threshold is reintroduced to decide it.
- Re-running enrichment for presentation. Items whose membership changes are re-queued by
  the existing mechanism; naming and review are untouched.
- The Flutter client. It already renders a nullable `prep_type` and falls back to `plain`;
  nothing there changes beyond the catalogue version bump.
- Retuning the search ranking in response to the cooked bonus applying more broadly, and
  removing the now near-constant full-text preparation column.
- `variable_fat` and the fat-level grouping, which keep working as they do today.
- Any new user-facing UI for identity-formed items — they are ordinary items and use the
  ordinary search row.

## Further Notes

The corpus measurements this spec rests on, all taken over the full 13,694-food set:
2,191 rows say `cooked` and 1,709 say `raw`; 675 say `canned`, 601 `frozen`, 495
`prepared`, 483 `dry`, 298 `fried`, 263 `mix`, 162 `dried`. `pan-fried` (37),
`rotisserie` (36), `simmered` (29) and `dehydrated` (20) occur but are absent from the
current vocabulary, while `poached` (2), `blanched` (3) and `sauteed` (14) are in it and
nearly unused. 344 rows carry both an identity-forming and a cooking token; 149 carry two
identity-forming tokens; 249 of 2,438 description stems mix labelled and unlabelled rows;
66 stems hold both a prepared and a non-prepared form.

One framing correction worth recording, because it is easy to get wrong: `merged_preps`
stores mean macros over a preparation's members, but **those means never reach the app**.
The client reads a preparation's macros from the representative row's own USDA numbers.
The requirement that cooked macros come from the most generic record is therefore a rule
about representative selection, not about the averaging — the means only feed the
enrichment prompt and the review UI.

The design record lives in the pipeline README's clustering and preparation sections,
which currently describe the label-family merge being deleted, and in the repository
glossary, whose **Preparation** entry currently reads "raw, boiled, roasted". Both are
updated as part of this work. No ADR: the reasoning is inseparable from the code it
describes, and the README is already named as the record for why identity works this way.
