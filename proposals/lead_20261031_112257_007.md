# Project Proposal — Flutter App

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Explore a Serbian price comparison built on the new open price data**

*Project description:*
### Problem or motivation

Since 1 September 2026, large Serbian retailers have had to publish their full price lists as machine-readable CSV files on the national open data portal (data.gov.rs). About 40 chains already do. The state's own front end is a Power BI report: you pick one retailer, then filter its list. It has no cross-chain comparison of a product, no shopping list, no store map, no accounts and no usable mobile layout. Private apps exist, but the one with a real user base (Cenoteka) has no basket total across chains and no store map, and the newer apps built on the open data have at most a few dozen ratings each.

Disscount already does search, barcode scanning, shopping lists, price history and digital cards for Croatia. Serbia now has open data in a single national schema, which looks easier to ingest than Croatia's per-chain formats. This issue collects what we know so we can decide whether a Serbian edition is worth building.

### Proposed solution

A Serbian edition of Disscount fed by the data.gov.rs price lists:

- Discover the price list datasets through the data.gov.rs uData API on every run and download each chain's CSV from the resource URL listed there, since the URLs point at the chains' own hosts and can change.
- Parse the one national 15-column schema with a single parser (a per-chain unit table and three null tokens are the main per-chain quirks seen so far).
- Map the rows onto the same product, price and chain model the frontend already reads from cijene-api, with Serbia-only extras (VAT rate, discount start and end dates, list type) kept alongside.
- Build our own store list (for example from OpenStreetMap) and map stores to the chains' price formats, because the data has no per-store prices and no store ids.
- Add a country switch in the frontend (Croatia or Serbia), separate from the UI language, plus RSD price formatting.
- Show the required attribution for the open data (publisher, download date, source URL, note of changes).

Where the ingestion lives is an open question (see below), and this issue does not decide it.

### Alternatives considered

- Contribute a Serbian crawler to cijene-api upstream, so Disscount keeps a single price source. It depends on whether the upstream maintainers want a second country in the project.
- Write a separate ingestion in our Spring Boot backend that serves Serbian data in the same shape the frontend expects.
- Reuse an existing Serbian pipeline: `DynamycSound/cene` already rebuilds a daily SQLite database from data.gov.rs, but it has no licence, so it cannot be reused as is.
- Scrape per-store price lists from each retailer's own website (the rulebook requires those), to get store-level prices. This means 40 or so sites with no common schema there.
- Do nothing for now and revisit once the state's national product code list for imported goods exists.

### Blocked on

A decision on where the Serbian ingestion lives (cijene-api upstream or our backend), and a store list we build ourselves, since the open data has none.

### Additional context

#### Legal basis

- Phase 1 (lapsed): a government decree on special trading conditions for certain goods (Uredba o posebnim uslovima za obavljanje trgovine za određenu vrstu robe) capped margins at 20% on 23 categories from 1 September 2025 and made chains send price lists to the ministry. An amendment around 1 December 2025 switched this to CSV on the open data portal, public from 9 December 2025 (27 chains, weekly). The decree lapsed on 1 March 2026 (unverified; from news summaries). Its Sl. glasnik number was not found.
- Zakon o zaštiti potrošača, Sl. glasnik RS 35/2026: in force 1 May 2026, most provisions apply from 2 August 2026. Reported Article 6 requires a sale price, a unit price and a price list on the trader's website "ažuriran u realnom vremenu" and on the open data portal, with details left to the minister. The article number comes from news reports (unverified against the law text).
- Pravilnik o uslovima, sadržaju i načinu objavljivanja cenovnika, Sl. glasnik RS 76/2026 (published around 21 to 23 August 2026), applies from 1 September 2026. Signed by Jagoda Lazarević, Minister of Internal and Foreign Trade.
- Amendment: Sl. glasnik RS 98/2026, published 16 October 2026, in force 24 October 2026. It has four articles, three of which change the rulebook. What it changes is not yet known (unverified).
- Who must publish (Pravilnik Art. 2): retailers selling the listed goods with sales revenue above RSD 3,000,000,000 (about EUR 25.6m) in the last business year. Mobile outlets are exempt. There is no store count or store size threshold. Scope is every product in 25 goods groups (dairy, drinks, fruit and vegetables, bread, meat, fish, sweets, oils, flour, pasta, household chemicals, paper goods, hygiene and cosmetics, baby food, diapers, alcohol, pet food and others), not a fixed basket.
- Retention: a new current list on every price change; the list valid on the 1st of each month is kept permanently as a snapshot, and earlier portal data must not be deleted or changed.
- Enforcement: Zakon o trgovini amendments (in force 1 May 2026) set a uniform RSD 100,000 fine for many offences, price transparency included. Reported consumer law fines are RSD 200,000 for a company, 50,000 for the responsible person and 100,000 for an entrepreneur; the exact article for a missing price list was not found (unverified). Under the decree, market inspection fined Delhaize RSD 24.5m in September 2025. Price accuracy inspections under the new platform are announced for the second half of November 2026.
- Licence: every sampled dataset carries the `sodl` licence (Srpska licenca za otvorene podatke 1.0). The portal publishes no licence text; the terms come from Zakon o elektronskoj upravi (Sl. glasnik RS 27/2018), Art. 26: free reuse "u komercijalne ili nekomercijalne svrhe", including integration into products and services, with a duty to cite the source: the publisher's name, the download date, the download URL, and a clear note of any changes. Art. 27(3) makes access free of charge. No share-alike clause.
- Licence caveats (unverified): the law was amended (Sl. glasnik 80/2026, applies from 8 September 2026) and it is not checked whether Art. 26 changed. Art. 26 binds public authorities, while these datasets are published by private retailers under the `sodl` label, so how far the terms bind them is untested.

#### Data: portal and API

- Portal: data.gov.rs, running uData, with a public JSON API at `https://data.gov.rs/api/1/`. `opendata.gov.rs` does not resolve, and the CKAN API path returns 404.
- Datasets share the Cyrillic title "Ценовници производа према Правилнику о условима, садржају и начину објављивања ценовника - <chain>". Searching `q=правилнику ценовника` returns 41 datasets, one per retailer organisation. Some older datasets still carry the decree-era title in their resource slugs. Search on the Cyrillic title: plain `q=cene` or `q=cenovnik` searches miss them or return unrelated small shops.
- Dataset metadata: frequency `continuous`, licence `sodl`, format CSV with optional XLSX side files (store lists, secondary barcodes). The files themselves refresh daily (HTTP Last-Modified on 31 October 2026 for Idea, Univerexport, Aman and Metro).
- Hosting: the portal is a registry, not a mirror. Most resource URLs point at the chains' own hosts (Lidl's site, Azure blob storage for Idea, Delhaize's own server, Univerexport's cloud, a shared fiscal vendor's Azure blob with long-lived SAS tokens for Aman, Veropoulos, Fortuna and others, Google Drive, WordPress uploads). A few chains upload into the portal itself, with a new URL on every upload. Ingestion should read URLs from the API each run. No auth or bot wall on the hosts tried.
- Publishers seen include Metro, Lidl, Delhaize (Maxi, Mega Maxi, Shop and Go, Maxi Online), Idea (Idea, Mercator, Roda formats), Univerexport, Aman, Gomex, Veropoulos, dm, Aksa, Medius, Trnava Promet, Šumadija market, Senta-Promet, Plus Cash&Carry, Fortuna, Podunavlje and about 20 smaller chains. DIS was not found on the portal; Mercator-S appears only as a format inside Idea's file.
- Size: the largest file is Idea's at about 180,000 rows and 27.6 MB (three list dates). Distinct products range from about 3,500 (Lidl) to 20,400 (Idea).

#### Data: file format

Observed in files from Lidl, Idea, Delhaize, Univerexport, Aman and Metro, downloaded on 31 October 2026. Format is identical across all six; the other chains were not opened (unverified).

- One CSV per chain, fixed file name without a date (`cene_proizvoda_<chain>.csv`), UTF-8 (some with a BOM), `;` delimiter, LF or CRLF, every row exactly 15 fields, Latin script.
- Dot decimal separator, no thousands separator, 0 to 2 decimals, dates `DD-MM-YYYY`, prices in RSD including VAT.
- Each file holds the current list plus every monthly snapshot since 1 September 2026, so files grow every month (how chains will handle growth is unverified).
- Column names vary slightly between chains (Lidl uses a different dash in column 7, Delhaize writes `MESECNI_PRESEK` without diacritics), so header matching has to be loose.

The 15 columns and how they map to cijene-api's Croatian model:

| # | Serbian column | Meaning | cijene-api field |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `KATEGORIJA` | National category code, 1 to 25 | `category` (fixed national list, better than Croatia's free text) |
| 2 | `NAZIV KATEGORIJE` | Category name | `category` |
| 3 | `Naziv proizvoda` | Product name, size often embedded | `product` |
| 4 | `Robna marka` | Brand (sometimes empty) | `brand` |
| 5 | `Barkod proizvoda` | Barcode, normally 13 digits | `barcode` |
| 6 | `Jedinica mere` | Unit of the unit price, free text per chain | `unit` (needs a per-chain normalisation table) |
| 7 | `Naziv trgovca - formata` | Retail format or price zone, not a store | no direct field; closest is `store_type` |
| 8 | `Datum cenovnika` | Date the list applies | no field (Serbia only) |
| 9 | `Redovna cena` | Regular price | `price` |
| 10 | `Cena po jedinici mere` | Unit price | `unit_price` |
| 11 | `Snižena cena` | Discounted price | `special_price` |
| 12 | `Datum početka sniženja` | Discount start | no field (Serbia only) |
| 13 | `Datum kraja sniženja` | Discount end | no field (Serbia only) |
| 14 | `Stopa PDV` | VAT rate, 10 or 20 | no field (Serbia only) |
| 15 | `VRSTA_CENOVNIKA` | `VAZECI_CENOVNIK` (current) or `MESECNI_PRESEK` (monthly snapshot) | no field (Serbia only) |

cijene-api fields with no Serbian source: `store_id`, store `name`, `city`, `street_address`, `zipcode`, `product_id` (the barcode is the only key), `quantity` (parse it from the name or derive it from price divided by unit price), `best_price_30`, `anchor_price`, `anchor_price_date`, `available`, `special_sale_type`, `packaging`, `initial_price`, `date_added`. `chain` comes from the portal organisation, normalised by us.

#### Data: quality quirks

- No-discount markers differ by chain: an empty string (Lidl, Aman, Delhaize, Metro), a literal `0` (Univerexport, in every row without a sale), or a NUL byte (Idea, in the price and both date columns). Treat all three as null.
- Bad rows: Idea has 107 rows where the discounted price is greater than or equal to the regular price, Lidl 3, Metro 2. Lidl has 66 rows with an empty regular price. Delhaize has 15 rows without a barcode out of 84,000.
- Duplicates: 3 to 10 duplicate (barcode, format, date) keys per file, so the dedupe key needs the product name too, or take the first row.
- Barcodes: GTIN check digit valid for about 95 to 98 percent of distinct codes. A few 14 and 20 digit codes in Idea. In-store codes starting with 2 (weighed goods, chain specific): about 2,000 of 9,900 at Metro, 40 to 260 elsewhere. Cross-chain matching by barcode looks at least as good as in Croatia.
- Units: free text such as KOM, KG, LIT, kg, lit, rln, g, ml, kom., br.pranja, listić, Komad, Centimetar, JED, or empty.
- Lidl exception: besides its price CSV, Lidl publishes an `EAN.csv` mapping a primary barcode to sister barcodes (`EANCODE;NAZIV_PROIZVODA;SESTRINSKI_EAN;GTIN_DESC`); one portal resource for Lidl points at that mapping file rather than the price schema. Vum and Prima Nova publish secondary barcode XLS files.
- dm and Plus Cash&Carry exception: their portal resources link to web pages, not CSV files.

#### Data: what is missing

- Per-store rows: prices are per format, not per store. Where a chain's stores differ, the portal file carries the highest valid price. Per-store lists are required only on the retailer's own website, with no common schema there. Delhaize has 5 formats, Idea 3 (Idea, Mercator, Roda), Univerexport 3 (M, S, L); Lidl, Aman and Metro have one national list.
- Store ids, addresses and coordinates: none in the CSV.
- Lowest price in the last 30 days: no column.
- Anchor price: no equivalent of Croatia's 2 May 2025 anchor price.
- Discount type and loyalty card prices: no discount type (sale, clearance) and no loyalty flag.

#### Data: store lists

- Store to format mapping comes only as optional XLSX side files and is not machine friendly.
- Delhaize: 552 rows (`NAZIV, Adresa, NAZIV_CEN`), no city or postcode, and its format names ("Maxi ", "Shop&Go") do not exactly match the CSV ("Shop and Go").
- Idea: 284 rows (`NAZIV OBJEKTA, CENOVNIK`) with codes like `I0` and `Rplus` that do not appear in the CSV.
- Lidl, Univerexport, Aman and Metro publish no store list on the portal. "Near me" therefore needs our own store database, mapped to formats by hand or with fuzzy matching.

#### Government platform

- Name: "Nacionalna platforma za praćenje cena", run by the Ministry of Internal and Foreign Trade (must.gov.rs) with the Office for IT and eGovernment. It is a public Power BI report linked from must.gov.rs, with no domain of its own. Presented on 16 October 2026; it replaces the 9 December 2025 version (27 chains, weekly updates).
- Reported coverage: 41 obligated chains, 39 live at launch, more than 4,000 stores, about 11,000 to 11,750 products with domestic barcodes.
- What it does (seen live on 31 October 2026, desktop): a landing page with about 40 retailer buttons, then one page per retailer with filters for category, product name, barcode, regular or discounted price and price format. KPI cards (products, share discounted, average discount, prices higher, lower or same versus the 1 September 2026 snapshot), a table of discounted products, a price range table and a change table against the monthly snapshot. Serbian Latin only.
- Search is a Power BI slicer over one retailer's list (substring match, uppercase without diacritics), not a search page.
- What it lacks: no cross-chain comparison of one product (you pick a retailer first), no per-product price history chart, no store list, address or map, no per-store prices, no accounts, no lists, no app, no barcode scanner. It is a fixed canvas report, so on a phone it likely scales down to tiny text (not tested).
- Cross-chain matching in the state's data covers only domestic barcodes (prefix 860); a national product code list for imported goods was promised within 3 to 4 months of launch. A map and AI tools are announced.
- Reception: Vreme (December 2025, about the earlier version) quoted a former state secretary calling it a "sirova baza podataka", citing no matching codes, chain-by-chain search and wrong categories. A consumer advocate in October 2026 called the method sound but said real benefit needs a more advanced system.

#### Competitors

- Cenoteka (cenoteka.rs): launched in 2014; 80% owned by WMG Media since January 2026, founder keeps 20%. 2025 revenue just under EUR 500,000, 6 employees. Web plus an Android app (3.7 stars from 281 ratings, 100K+ downloads); no iOS app found in the Serbian App Store. Features: product search, barcode scan, price per store, side-by-side compare, price history chart (30 days to 1 year), price drop alerts inside the account, a saved shopping list (account needed), flyers for about ten chains. No basket total compared across chains, no store map, no city or store-level prices. Scope goes far beyond groceries (tech, tyres, fuel, pharmacies). Serbian Latin only. Data source: historically scraping or chain feeds, current use of the open data unverified. Business model: programmatic advertising (consent dialog lists 210 ad partners) and a B2B price tracking product (pricing unknown). Named as a third party in a 2024 Competition Commission price fixing case against four chains.
- Eponuda Katalozi Srbija (Eponuda): Android 4.2 from 7,863 ratings, 100K+ downloads; iOS 5.0 from 1 rating. Flyers from supermarkets, electronics, drugstores and toy stores, no price comparison. The largest flyer app found. Data source: retailer flyers. Business model: unverified (per-click retailer fees are typical for Eponuda's comparison site). The separate Eponuda comparison app covers webshops, not groceries.
- Tprice: Compare Prices (iOS seller Nikita Iarunin): Android 4.6 from 31 ratings, 1K+ downloads; iOS, no ratings. Claims 400+ cities, 300,000+ products, 30+ chains in Serbia and 25+ in Croatia, up to 6 months of price history. Search, barcode scan, basket with price tracking, Nutri-Score, favorites, own store list. A direct regional competitor because it covers Croatia too. Data source: unverified (likely the open data). Business model: unverified.
- namirnice.rs (developer Nalet / Nikola Jaric): Android 10+ downloads, no rating; also on iOS. Grocery list with a total price per store, adding items by scanning the QR code on a fiscal receipt, nearest stores on a map with directions, promotions, Google or email login. States it uses the official daily price lists. Closest to the planned feature set, with almost no users. Business model: unverified.
- Moja Korpa (nextsoftware.rs): Android, 1+ downloads, no rating. Compares big supermarket prices, scans fiscal receipts, tracks monthly spending, suggests cheaper alternatives. Data source and business model: unverified.
- Pricely - Pametna kupovina (Pricely DOO): iOS 4.6 from 7 ratings, released 3 September 2026. Supermarket price comparison, regular and promo prices, shopping list, cheaper alternatives. Android version, data source and business model: unverified.
- eCenovnik (Milos Krstic): iOS 5.0 from 1 rating. Prices at Univerexport, Maxi, Mega Maxi, SuperVero, Gomex and others, plus the nearest store. Android version, data source and business model: unverified.
- DynamycSound/cene (GitHub): Dart and Flutter, created 15 October 2026, 3 commits, 0 stars, no README, no licence. Pipeline over the data.gov.rs price lists with stores from OpenStreetMap, images from maxi.rs and a daily SQLite database published by GitHub Actions; the Flutter app has search, compare by chain, a list, a map and an AI agent. No evidence it is on Google Play. No business model.

Chain apps (MOJA maxi, Lidl Plus, IDEA, Gomex) show only their own flyers, coupons and loyalty cards.

#### Gaps Disscount could fill

- A shopping list total compared across chains, at scale: Cenoteka does not offer it, and the five 2026 apps that do (Tprice, namirnice.rs, Moja Korpa, Pricely, eCenovnik) each have at most 31 ratings.
- A store map and nearest cheapest store: missing in Cenoteka and in the state platform; namirnice.rs and eCenovnik offer it with almost no users.
- Cross-chain product comparison on a usable, mobile-friendly site: the state platform works one retailer at a time.
- An iOS-friendly option: Cenoteka has no iOS app in the Serbian store, and Disscount's PWA installs without the app stores.
- Cyrillic and Latin search input: Cenoteka and the state platform are Latin only (Cyrillic input in Cenoteka is unverified).
- One wallet for chain loyalty cards (MOJA maxi, Lidl Plus, IDEA and others).
- One app for Croatia and Serbia; Tprice is the only other app found that covers both.
- Price history and price drop alerts are not a gap against Cenoteka, which has both; they are a gap only against the state platform.
