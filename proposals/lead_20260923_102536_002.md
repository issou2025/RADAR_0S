# Project Proposal — Bim Modeling

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Electrical-equipment families carry an NEC working-clearance solid and a Show Clearance Yes/No parameter**

*Project description:*
`Refs #818` (owner steer). Depends on **#819** (the clearance table) for its sizes and on **#690** (visibility bound to a parameter) for the toggle to actually work.

## Why

The owner's verdict on the lighting control panel built for their test (#816): *"Very Good, but for the future you need to know NEC code in order to get the clearances on there"*, and *"clearances need to be toggleable within the family parameters"*. A family that models the equipment but not the working space its code requires is incomplete for coordination and clash detection, and that is the first thing an electrical designer checks.

## What to build

1. **A clearance solid** in front of the equipment's working face, sized from #819: depth by voltage to ground and Condition, width the greater of the equipment width or 30 in, height the greater of 6½ ft or the equipment height. Positioned from the face the equipment is worked from. #816's lighting control panel projects toward −Y, so its working space starts at the door face.
2. **Dedicated equipment space (110.26(E)) only for kinds it applies to.** #819 answers which; do not apply every zone to every kind.
3. **On its own subcategory** (for example *Clearance*), so a project can control it through Visibility/Graphics and clash-detect it as a set. How a generated family declares a subcategory, and whether the engine can today, is part of this issue. State what you find.
4. **A *Show Clearance* Yes/No family parameter.** Yes/No parameters are authorable (#710). **Binding it to the solid's visibility is the open gap** tracked in #690 and recorded in `src/rvt/famgen/parametric.py:501` (`yesno -> an element's visibility (gap)`).
   - Until #690 lands, the family ships the solid **and** the parameter, and the report says in plain words that the parameter is **not yet bound** — the rule `parametric.py` already sets for an unauthorable request (delivered, never refused, never described as working).
   - Once #690 lands, the binding is wired and the note removed — **only after** the desktop verdict below.
5. **Clearance parameters carry their standard.** Voltage to ground, Condition and edition are family parameters too, provenance from #819, so a user can see why the solid is the size it is.

## Order

Start with **#816's lighting control panel** (the owner's own test case), then the other electrical-equipment generators: the panelboard / transformer / switchboard catalog kinds and the `electrical_equipment` archetypes (wireway today). Each kind needs a decision on which zones apply, recorded.

## Hard rule 4

Nothing here is "working" until desktop Revit shows it, batched per S-2026-09-04-a, so the owner is not a click-through test instrument:

- the solid appears in its subcategory and not as ordinary visible geometry;
- clash detection catches an obstruction placed in it;
- once #690 lands, *Show Clearance* hides and shows it.

Until then the route and the report say validated, not certified.
