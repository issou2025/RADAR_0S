# AI Daily Digest — November 21, 2026

**Top story: the Riemann hypothesis was claimed twice in one day — once by an AI, once by a human.** On **November 20**, Anthropic mathematician **Levent Alpöge** posted a four-page note saying that, working with Claude, he has a proof of the **Riemann hypothesis**, releasing not prose but **Lean code** that machine-checks the **Generalized Riemann Hypothesis**. Hours later, **AMS president Ravi Vakil** published a rival draft by **Alessandro Masullo** — a conventional, all-human paper on the same conjecture, pushed out early precisely because the AI claim had landed. Neither is verified. **Terence Tao** called Alpöge's artifact an "(enormous) Lean-only proof," which is the day's real story: the first plausible claim on mathematics' most famous open problem arrives as 14 files of code that compile, and that almost nobody can read.

## Notable Papers & Research

### Two claimed proofs of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, twelve hours apart

**Alpöge's claim (AI-assisted).** Alpöge posted the note and **Lean code split across 14 download files** on his own website on **November 20** ([Scientific American](`` https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/anthropic-mathematician-claims-to-prove-riemann-hypothesis-most-famous-problem-in-all-of-math/ ``); [TechCrunch](``` https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/ ```)). The note says the method settles the **Generalized** Riemann Hypothesis, of which RH is a special case. **Terence Tao** wrote the same day that Alpöge, "using an internal AI model, has generated an (enormous) Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis," pointed readers to the **Lean Zulip thread** as the place to follow verification, and was explicit about the limits of his own reading: "For the middle I have only read statements of declarations." His preliminary read is that the strategy "appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry" ([terrytao.wordpress.com](```` https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/ ````)).

**Where verification stands.** MIT's **Andrew Sutherland** independently compiled the Lean code and told *Scientific American* it compiles and rests only on "the three standard axioms." The harder question — whether the final theorem statement is actually the Riemann hypothesis rather than something weaker — was still **pending** at the time of that reporting, as was a separate Lean-kernel replay of the exported proof. Mathematician **Elliot Glazer** wrote on X that the formalized statement is indeed GRH and put the odds of a Lean bug below 1%. The Clay Institute still lists RH as unsolved; its $1M prize requires broad acceptance plus **two years** of scrutiny.

**Masullo's claim (all human).** Vakil posted Masullo's draft, **"The Music of the Primes,"** on his blog on **November 20**, writing that Masullo "has a proposed proof of GRH" ([OfficeChai](````` https://officechai.com/ai/hours-after-alpoges-ai-assisted-riemann-hypothesis-proof-ams-president-ravi-vakil-rushes-out-a-rival-all-human-proof-by-alessandro-masullo/)). ````` Masullo holds a Stanford PhD under **Brian Conrad**, who Vakil says has scrutinized the argument for months and finds it "so far looking good." Masullo describes it as **12 years** of work. Vakil had planned to bring him to Stanford for a year to polish it before announcing; he released the draft instead and asked mathematicians to check it, formalize it, and fill gaps.

*Why it matters, and the honest caveat:* this is the sharpest test yet of whether formal verification can substitute for human comprehension. A Lean proof that compiles settles *whether* a theorem is true while saying nothing about *why* — and the two claims arriving together means the mathematical community gets to compare a machine artifact and a human manuscript on identical ground. **Both remain unrefereed.** Context from previous digests: OpenAI's October `openai/math` dump claimed a *quasi*-Riemann result (no zeros above real part 7/8), and **James Maynard** suggests this work builds on it.

### A 27B open model edits its own weights, with no gradients

**"Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights"** — Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Changyi Yang, Andy Mao, Xi Zhu, Chaoran Cheng, Kaiqu Liang, Shuaike Shen, Zetian Li, Ruihan Guo, Ge Liu, [arXiv:2611.21885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) (v1 **16 November**, v2 **18 November**). ### Compiling an exact arithmetic circuit directly into frozen weights

**"A CPU Inside an LLM: Zero-Gradient Compilation of Exact Circuits into Pretrained Weights"** — Tanmay Sah et al., [arXiv:2611.26450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26450) (posted **November 19–20**, cs.AI). Closed-form primitives write exact arithmetic and Boolean circuits into pretrained weights with **no gradient training, no training examples and no runtime hooks**, using Mellin-GLU quadrature to handle unknown RMSNorm scaling plus cross-token scale cancellation in attention. A 5-stage compiler synthesizes signed 4–8 digit circuits in constant TC⁰ depth at a reported **100% accuracy**; summaries cite **99.17%** on six-digit arithmetic in Granite 4.2 3B and **100%** in Qwen 3.5 4B, against **42.2–61.7%** for a baseline trained on 12.8K samples ([aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/circuit-grafting-pushes-qwen-35-4b-to-100-on-6-digit-math)). 27 pages, 3 figures, 24 tables. *Caveats:* not peer reviewed; the claim that natural-language competence survives the surgery is the part most in need of outside scrutiny, and the arXiv listing and Papers With Code disagree on the posting date by a day. Read against **Frostbite** below, the week has an odd symmetry — AI designing a CPU, and a CPU being compiled into an AI.

### World models: simulate the points, then render the video

**"Point Dynamics as Proxy for Robotic World Models"** — [arXiv:2611.25946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25946) (submitted **November 19**, cs.CV). Argues video-based robot world models fail to capture how a scene responds to robot motion, and instead lifts the first frame into 3D points, simulates those points with a learned dynamics model, and renders them back to video — an explicit bet that geometry, not pixels, is the right state for a robot's world model.

### Reserved slot: Architect Labs says its agent swarm discovered, designed and verified a new CPU *(a company not covered elsewhere in this digest, or in any previous one)*

**"Introducing Frostbite: A new CPU architecture discovered, designed, and verified end-to-end by AI"** — Architect Labs, published **November 20** ([architectlabs.com](https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite); **629 points** on Hacker News). Frostbite is a general-purpose CPU built around **interaction nets**, a mathematical model from 1990. The company says its agents were given **only interaction net theory** and from that co-designed the **ISA and chip architecture from scratch**, running large swarms to design, verify and optimize **down to the final GDS**, iterating compiler, ISA, architectural model, RTL and verification together with an "ASL Contract" as the source of truth. Headline numbers: a **geometric mean 4.59× the reference core's performance per watt across 25 workloads**, and **8.77× higher performance per unit area**.

*The caveats are the story here.* **No physical Frostbite exists** — every number is pre-silicon simulation, unverified in hardware, and self-reported. The baseline is **a single core of a dual-issue, in-order superscalar CPU**, not a full modern multicore chip, so "4.6× more efficient than AMD and Intel" framing in some coverage is not what the company claimed. Next steps are releasing the **RTL and ISA**, an **FPGA playground**, and a preliminary OS — which is also what would make the claims checkable. A widely shared dev.to writeup describing a "128-core" chip, a "Glacier" engine and EPYC/Grace comparisons **conflicts with the primary source** and should be ignored.

### Apple ships Depth Pro 2 with open weights

**"Depth Pro 2: Sharp and Temporally Consistent Metric Monocular Depth"** — Peiyun Hu et al. (Apple), [arXiv:2611.26603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26603) (submitted **November 19**), with code and weights on [GitHub](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-depth-pro-2) and [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/apple/DepthPro2). Dense **metric** depth — meters per pixel — from an ordinary camera, on a single photo, a video or a live feed, at native input resolution. The authors report the largest model, **Depth Pro 2-H+**, best on zero-shot video metric depth and on boundary sharpness and jitter. *Caveat:* a third-party post says the four models carry a **non-commercial license** — unverified here, and worth checking before commercial use. This matters for the spatial-intelligence thread: open metric-depth weights are infrastructure for everyone building world models and robot perception.

### Signal starts blocking the operating system's own AI agents
