## Project Understanding

## Why
#939 shipped `make_archetype(product="strut_trapeze", nested_hardware=True)` as an opt-in, because the nested version is not yet strictly better than the solid default. Two things hold it back:

1. **No height follow.** The nested washers and nuts stay put when Tier Spacing, Strut Height or Washer Thickness change. The solid parts ride those heights (#787 Case B); the nested ones cannot, because our generated children carry no Center (Elevation) reference to lock. The born census shows only 11 Center (Elevation) locks, too few to author the shape with confidence.
2. **The nut resizes in value only.** `Nut Across Flats` is associated, but no verified mechanism resizes a hexagon. Revit-born nuts are usually driven by a formula or an EQ chain on the hex vertices, which needs its own census.

## DONE
1. **Center (Elevation).** Census its references and locks: the plane flags, the `m_refName` code, and how the lock witnesses look. Author it on the washer and nut children. Lock each nested instance's elevation to the host's height planes (the plane its part rides in the solid version), and extend CG8 to judge it.
2. **Hex resize.** Find a hexagon resize mechanism from the corpus. Candidates are a labelled across-flats dimension with EQ about the centre, or a formula; whichever it is, it must also be desktop-verifiable. Wire `Nut Across Flats` to geometry.
3. **Default choice.** Once both hold on 2026 and 2025 (validate 0 errors, `check_file == []`, every lock judged), re-decide the default with stated reasons.

   Desktop verdicts are still needed before any route calls the nested trapeze flexing (hard rule 4).
