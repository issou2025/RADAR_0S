## Project Summary

**QUESTION:** Summarize the project in 2-3 paragraphs.

**ANSWER:**

The Mimir Private Vault is a zero-knowledge privacy protocol designed to enable shielded deposits, withdrawals, and transfers on EVM-compatible blockchains. Built using Noir/Barretenberg, UltraPlonk proofs, and Poseidon2 hashing, the vault uses UTXO-style notes committed to a Sparse Merkle Tree, with nullifiers preventing double-spending. Transaction proving takes place locally on the user's browser or device, meaning that only verified proofs and the necessary public inputs are submitted to the blockchain.

One of the challenges with private vaults is ensuring users can safely recover their funds if they lose access to their local data. Currently, users are responsible for backing up their encrypted notes themselves. If that client-side data is lost, recovering the associated funds can become impossible. Our proposal addresses this problem by integrating Filecoin as a decentralized, verifiable storage layer for the vault. When a user creates or withdraws a note, the client will encrypt the note data and arrange a Filecoin storage deal. The resulting Content Identifier (CID) will be included as a public input in the ZK circuit. The circuit will prove that the user knows the note's preimage and that the corresponding data is associated with the specified Filecoin CID. Retrieval will also be incorporated into the process, allowing users to retrieve and decrypt their stored notes, with retrieval evidence available for verification and auditing.

This integration creates value for both Mimir and the Filecoin ecosystem. It demonstrates how Filecoin storage deals can become part of a working zero-knowledge application, extending their role beyond general-purpose data storage into privacy-preserving financial infrastructure. It also introduces Filecoin to users and applications in onchain finance, where censorship resistance and reliable, verifiable data persistence are particularly important. The integration is designed to operate entirely from the client side, without introducing an onchain Filecoin dependency. This allows the vault to remain deployable across EVM-compatible chains while using Filecoin as its decentralized storage layer.

## Impact

**QUESTION:** What pain points does this project address?

**ANSWER:**

The project addresses three related challenges at the intersection of blockchain privacy and decentralized storage. First, losing private transaction data can mean losing access to funds. In shielded UTXO-based systems, users are responsible for preserving the private notes needed to manage and recover their funds. If that information is lost, recovering the associated funds can become extremely difficult or impossible. This creates a significant usability and security challenge for privacy protocols and makes reliable note recovery an important part of building systems that people can use with confidence.

Second, relying on conventional cloud storage for backups introduces a different set of risks. Services such as AWS, Google Cloud, and Amazon S3 offer convenient storage, but they also introduce centralized infrastructure, third-party dependencies, and potential censorship or availability concerns. For users who choose privacy protocols because they value control and censorship resistance, simply moving sensitive backup data to centralized providers does not fully address those concerns, even when the data is encrypted. Third, there is an opportunity to demonstrate a more advanced use of Filecoin within zero-knowledge applications. Many storage integrations focus on storing and retrieving data, but there is room to demonstrate how storage information and cryptographic proofs can work together inside a ZK application. By connecting encrypted private-note backups with Filecoin storage and verification mechanisms, Mimir aims to demonstrate a practical use case for decentralized storage in which data persistence and retrieval can become part of a broader cryptographic verification process.

**QUESTION:** What are the benefits if this works?

**ANSWER:**

If successful, the project would give Mimir vault users a more reliable way to back up and recover their private notes without depending on a single centralized storage provider. By using Filecoin as the decentralized storage layer, users would have a way to preserve encrypted note data while maintaining the privacy and censorship-resistance principles of the vault.

For the Filecoin ecosystem, the integration would demonstrate a practical application of decentralized storage within privacy-focused financial infrastructure. It would introduce a new potential source of storage demand from DeFi and privacy applications, while showing how Filecoin storage deals can be incorporated into zero-knowledge workflows rather than used solely for general data storage.

The project would also produce reusable open-source components and implementation patterns for other developers. The approach of incorporating a Filecoin CID as a public input in a ZK circuit, alongside the relevant storage and verification evidence, could provide a foundation for other zero-knowledge applications that need to reference, preserve, and verify off-chain data. This would allow the work to benefit developers beyond Mimir and contribute to broader experimentation across the Filecoin ecosystem.

**QUESTION:** What are the risks if it does not happen?

**ANSWER:**

If this project does not move forward, users of privacy-focused vaults will continue to face a significant risk: losing access to their private notes through device failure, user error, or lost backups could mean permanent loss of access to their funds. Without a reliable, decentralized recovery mechanism, privacy systems remain difficult to use safely over the long term.

Filecoin could also miss an opportunity to establish ZK privacy applications as an early-adopter use case for decentralized storage. As privacy-focused vaults and other onchain financial applications grow, they could create recurring demand for secure, verifiable data storage. Without this integration, that potential source of ecosystem growth remains unexplored.

Finally, the broader concept of combining zero-knowledge proofs with verifiable decentralized storage would remain largely theoretical in this application. The project is intended to turn that concept into a working, open-source implementation that other developers can study, adapt, and build upon. Without it, that practical reference point would not be delivered.

**QUESTION:** What is the impact in specific verticals?

**ANSWER:**

DeFi privacy: Recoverable private notes could make shielded transfers more practical for institutional and high-net-worth users who may otherwise be reluctant to use privacy infrastructure because of the operational risks associated with losing access to funds. Reliable backup and recovery would help address an important barrier to adoption.

Personal security: Individuals operating in high-surveillance or restrictive environments could benefit from private financial transactions combined with encrypted, verifiable backups. This would help protect sensitive financial information while reducing the risk that losing a device or local data results in permanent loss of access to funds.

Filecoin storage market: Each vault user could generate recurring storage demand through activities such as note creation, backup rotation, and compliance-related disclosures. As adoption grows, these activities could create ongoing storage demand from the privacy and onchain finance sectors, giving Filecoin a practical use case beyond general-purpose data storage.

## Outcomes

**QUESTION:** What will you deliver?

**ANSWER:**

The project will deliver an extended Noir circuit suite, a TypeScript client integration, updated Solidity verifier contracts, technical documentation, and a dual-licensed open-source release. We will extend the existing shieldedPool architecture and Noir circuit libraries with new modules for verifiable storage and retrieval, including a storage_backup.nr circuit to prove knowledge of a note preimage and its association with a valid Filecoin storage deal CID, and a storage_retrieval.nr circuit to support verification of note retrieval and decryption. The existing spend.nr and withdraw.nr circuits will also be modified to accept the storage deal CID as a public input and enforce the required backup conditions. All circuits will compile to UltraPlonk through Barretenberg, targeting approximately 2 million constraints, subject to browser-proving performance testing.

On the client side, we will integrate Filecoin storage into shieldedPool through a FilecoinStorageManager, which will negotiate storage deals using the Boost and file-client libraries, encrypt note payloads using AES-GCM with a note-derived key, and return the resulting CID. A corresponding FilecoinRetrievalManager will retrieve and decrypt stored payloads and support verification of retrieval evidence. The client will also provide automatic backups when notes are created or withdrawn, a manual recovery interface, and configurations for both the Filecoin Calibration testnet and mainnet. We will update MimirVaultVerifier.sol to support the new public inputs required by the storage and retrieval workflow, including the storage deal CID and retrieval proof, and deploy the updated verifier contracts to Ethereum Sepolia and the Filecoin Calibration testnet for end-to-end integration testing.

The project will also deliver a technical specification covering the circuit architecture, Filecoin storage deal workflow, and threat model, along with a user guide explaining the backup and recovery process. To demonstrate the complete experience, we will produce an approximately 10-minute recorded demonstration showing a deposit, automatic Filecoin backup, simulated device loss, and recovery of the note from Filecoin. Finally, all grant-funded repositories will be released under both the MIT and Apache 2.0 licenses, with the corresponding LICENSE-MIT and LICENSE-APACHE files, allowing other developers to use, review, and build upon the work under either license.

**QUESTION:** How will you measure success?

**ANSWER:**

We will measure success through a combination of technical performance, storage reliability, security, and early adoption. The extended Noir circuits will target fewer than 2.5 million constraints, with proof generation completing in under 30 seconds on modern hardware to demonstrate that browser-based proving is practical. Filecoin storage deal creation and retrieval on the Calibration testnet will target a success rate above 95%, while the complete backup and proof-generation process will aim to finish in under 60 seconds. Security will be measured through a community review period, with a goal of reaching release with zero unresolved critical findings. We will also track GitHub stars, repository watchers, developer feedback, and community contributions as early indicators of interest in the open-source tools and their potential adoption by the wider ecosystem.

## Data Onboarding

**QUESTION:** How much data will the project onboard to Filecoin?

**ANSWER:**

The project aims to onboard approximately 50 GB of data to Filecoin in Month 1 through testnet activity, based on an estimated 100 users storing 500 MB each. By Month 3, we target approximately 250 GB on the Calibration testnet, based on 500 users. Following mainnet launch, we aim to onboard approximately 1 TB by Month 6, based on 2,000 users, growing to approximately 4 TB by Month 12, based on 8,000 users and continued note rotation deals.

These projections are based on an average encrypted note payload of approximately 2 KB, with each user creating an estimated 10 notes per month through deposits, withdrawals, and note rotations. Each note would generate one Filecoin storage deal, with an estimated minimum deal size of 50 MB due to Filecoin sector padding. This translates to approximately 500 MB of storage deal volume per user per month. The figures represent total deal volume onboarded during each respective month, rather than cumulative storage. Compression and deduplication may reduce the amount of unique logical data stored, but they do not reduce the overall deal volume.

Our vault protocol specification was completed in September 2026, and we have developed three open-source Noir circuit libraries covering Sparse Merkle Tree functionality, Poseidon2 interoperability, and nullifier handling. These libraries have been published under the MIT license. The proposed Filecoin integration will extend this existing foundation with storage backup and retrieval functionality. The grant will fund the additional engineering and integration work required to connect these components and develop a working end-to-end implementation.

We are committed to keeping the development process open and accessible to the wider ecosystem. All grant-funded work will be developed publicly through branches in the Mimir Collective GitHub organization, with progress updates provided through the grant's designated GitHub issue. We also commit to dual-licensing all grant-funded work under MIT and Apache-2.0. The three existing circuit libraries, currently published under MIT, will receive the additional Apache-2.0 license, and the necessary license files and source-file headers will be added to the relevant repositories before grant-funded contributions begin.

Finally, we understand that grant funding comes with clear responsibilities. We will not begin grant-funded development until the Open Source Software Grant Agreement has been signed by both parties and the initial $15,000 Milestone 1 funding allocation has been received. Subsequent milestone development will begin only after the preceding milestone has been completed and approved and the corresponding funding allocation has been received. We will follow the agreed milestones, maintain transparent reporting, and make the resulting work available for other developers and projects to examine, use, and build upon.

Our goal is not simply to complete a Filecoin integration for Mimir. We want to produce an open-source implementation that demonstrates how decentralized storage and zero-knowledge privacy technology can work together to solve a practical problem in onchain finance.
