## The tension this spike must resolve — do not skip it

The obvious reading of the numbers is "trim `CLAUDE.md`". **That is probably wrong, and a spike that concludes it without argument has failed.**

The 572-line Verification-lessons section is not verbosity. Nearly every entry records something that cost real CI cycles, a wrong merge, or a multi-round debugging session to learn, and several explicitly exist to stop a measured-and-rejected idea returning as a fresh proposal. Deleting one does not save 25 words; it re-opens a road that was already walked. The file's own history shows the failure mode: a fact once went stale across six artifacts and took four correction rounds.

So the real question is not "how do we make it shorter" but **"what is the right structure for durable, expensive-to-acquire knowledge that must be available but need not all be resident in every prompt?"** Those are different questions with different answers.

## Part A — the `CLAUDE.md` improvement run

### Two tools, not interchangeable — use the right one

- **`claude-md-management:claude-md-improver` (SKILL)** — input is the files and the codebase. Discover → score against a rubric → **report before any edit** → show the diff → apply on approval. This is the audit tool and the primary instrument for this issue.
- **`/revise-claude-md` (COMMAND)** — input is the **session transcript**; its first step is "review this session for learnings". It captures what only a conversation knows. Not a substitute for the audit, and not usable for a cold review.

### Constraints on running them — each has bitten before

- **Main session only.** Dispatching `/revise-claude-md` to a subagent is structurally broken, not merely wasteful: a subagent cannot see the parent conversation, so its reflection input is empty and it degenerates into transcribing whatever the brief already said. A subagent may gather evidence; the report and the approval stay in the main session.
- **`/revise-claude-md` declares no Bash**, so it cannot verify anything empirically and cannot commit. Verify every empirical claim *before* invoking it; commit afterwards.
- **Point the improver at the COMMITTED file** (`git show HEAD:CLAUDE.md`). Reading the working tree makes it mistake uncommitted edits for already-merged fixes and report "nothing to update".
- **The improver's Currency criterion cannot catch a SELF-STALING fact.** It asks "is this true now", so it verifies at the instant the text was written and is structurally blind to an ahead/behind count, a tip SHA, a diffstat, or an "as of" snapshot that decays on the next commit. Pair it with a reviewer pass asking **"when does this become false"**, and prefer formulations that decay on the same schedule as the thing they describe.

## Part B — the automation assessment and recommendation plan

Use `claude-code-setup:claude-automation-recommender` for the forward-looking half, but **the assessment of what already exists is the more valuable half and the recommender does not do it.** Judge the existing 13 committed artifacts (4 hooks + 3 agents + 6 skills) on their own evidence.

### The open-source constraint, which applies to every committed artifact

This is a public repository. Personal tooling directives belong in gitignored local config, never in a tracked `CLAUDE.md` or `.claude/settings.json` — **a committed hook executes for contributors who cannot act on it.** Every one of the four hooks should be examined against that bar: does it protect something a contributor would recognise, or does it encode one maintainer's workflow? `artifact-guard.sh` at 1,276 lines is the obvious case to examine first, not because it is wrong but because it is the largest and the most opinionated.

### Skills

Any new or edited skill should also go through the `claude-plugins-official` **skill-creator** plugin (`quick_validate.py` plus its authoring lens). Known false positive to keep, not fix: its allow-list flags `disable-model-invocation` as invalid, which is correct for the portable `.skill` spec but wrong for Claude Code.

## Questions to answer

### Structure and cost

1. **What is the per-request cost** of the current `CLAUDE.md`, in tokens, and what fraction of a typical context window is it? Measure it; do not estimate.
2. **Does Claude Code's `@path` import mechanism apply here**, and would a small always-resident core plus referenced appendices preserve the knowledge while cutting resident cost? If it does not work as hoped, say so with evidence — that is a useful negative result.
3. **Which sections are genuinely needed on every request** versus needed only when touching a specific subsystem? The Domain-rules and Code-conventions sections plausibly differ from the Release & branching section on this axis.
4. **Does a directory-scoped `CLAUDE.md`** (e.g. under `app/` or `pipeline/`) load only when relevant, and would that help?

### Content quality

5. **How many of the 354 issue references are still live**, and how many point at closed issues whose content has been superseded? Sample rather than exhaustively check, and state the sample size.
6. **Which entries are SELF-STALING** by the definition above? Enumerate them — this is the class the improver cannot find on its own.
7. **Which entries are duplicated** between `CLAUDE.md` and a hook's own comments, a skill, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, or a spike doc? Note that duplication in prose is a **correctness check**, not a smell — the second copy is what catches drift — so the recommendation must distinguish deliberate twins from accidental copies.
8. **Is anything in `CLAUDE.md` that belongs in `CONTRIBUTING.md`** instead, i.e. a rule that binds humans as much as agents?

### Automation

9. **For each of the 4 hooks: what has it actually caught?** Evidence, not intent. A guard that has never fired and has no selftest row for its motivating case is a different proposition from one that fires weekly.
10. **Is any hook over- or under-firing?** There is live evidence to start from and it should be treated as input rather than re-derived from scratch.
11. **For each of the 3 subagents and 6 skills: is it still used, and does its content still match the code?** A skill describing a procedure that has changed is worse than no skill.
12. **What is missing?** `.claude/workflows/` does not exist, and no committed workflow is available for repeatable multi-agent tasks. Is that a gap or a deliberate absence?
13. **What should be REMOVED?** A recommendation that only adds is not an assessment.

### The cost question, asked explicitly

14. **For every recommended change, does the benefit exceed the cost** given that this is a single-maintainer project with a working, deployed product? A restructure that risks losing a hard-won lesson to save context is a bad trade. Say plainly where the answer is "leave it alone" — and if the honest overall answer is "this is fine as it is", that is an acceptable and valuable verdict.

## Deliverable

A decision document at `docs/spikes/<this-issue>-<slug>.md`, matching `docs/spikes/244-buoyed-fairways.md`, `245-depth-mask-resolution.md` and `296-lazy-load-map-data.md`:

- a **verdict line** up front,
- every factual claim carrying a `file:line` or a quoted command output,
- an explicit **considered-and-rejected** section,
- a **prioritised recommendation table** — change, cost, benefit, risk-if-wrong,
- follow-up issues filed for whatever the verdict says should be done.

The document is the deliverable. **Any actual `CLAUDE.md` edit is a separate, approval-gated main-session change**, not part of this issue.

## Non-goals

- Not a rewrite of `CLAUDE.md`. Producing a shorter file is not the objective; producing the right structure is, and "unchanged" is a permitted outcome.
- Not deleting hard-won lessons to hit a line count. Any recommendation to remove an entry must say what would go wrong if the lesson were re-learned the hard way.
- Not adding automation for its own sake. Every proposed hook or skill costs maintenance and executes for contributors.
- Not touching `docs/superpowers/specs/` — that path is approval-gated and is not what this is about.
