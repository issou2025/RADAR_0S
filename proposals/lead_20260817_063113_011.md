# Project Proposal — Structural Drawings

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Gym Notes v1 spec**

*Project description:*
## Destination

A buildable v1 product spec for **Gym Notes**: a cross-platform mobile workout
tracker that is a deliberately-incomplete remake of FitNotes plus live shared
session recording between friends. The spec covers the domain model, the
fast-logging UX, the friend/identity model, the live shared-session mechanics,
the chosen tech stack and backend, and the one-time remove-ads monetization —
enough that build sessions can start without further product decisions.

## Notes

**Domain**: consumer mobile app, solo developer working with coding agents,
both iOS and Android from day one.

**Skills every session should consult**: `/grilling` and `/domain-modeling` by
default; `/research` for the research tickets; `/prototype` for the prototype
ticket.

**Standing preferences for this effort**:

- Recommend technologies on **problem fit**, never on the developer's existing
  language familiarity. They have ~10 years' experience and pick up new stacks
  quickly.
- **Vocabulary is fixed**: an `Exercise` is a reusable named movement (e.g. lat
  pulldown). A `Session` is one dated training instance owned by exactly one
  user. A `Set` is a logged row inside a session against an exercise. The user
  may say "workout" to mean *exercise* — translate, don't adopt. `User data` is
  the complete collection of one user's sessions — the unit that gets backed up,
  exported and deleted. **`Log` is reserved for its ordinary programming meaning
  and must never name a domain concept.** The full glossary lives in
  `CONTEXT.md` at the repo root; keep it current as tickets resolve.
- **Zero-friction first run is the differentiator.** The app must be fully
  usable with no account, no sign-in wall, and no onboarding questionnaire. Any
  proposal that compromises this needs to justify itself loudly.
- **No trainer/trainee roles.** The peer model is symmetric: anyone can record
  for anyone, and two people can record for each other simultaneously while each
  owns their own session.
- This map is **planning**, with one sanctioned execution ticket at the end that
  assembles the spec document itself.

## Decisions so far

- **Destination** — a buildable v1 spec covering fast logging, friend-delegated
  live recording, and monetization; not a shipped app, not a viability study.
- **Platforms** — iOS and Android from day one via a cross-platform stack.
  Delegated recording is inherently cross-device, so a single-platform v1 would
  fail half its pairings.
- **Monetization** — v1 is a static bottom banner ad plus a one-time in-app
  purchase to remove it. No subscription; no interstitial or video ads. A
  subscription wedge remains wanted but unfound, and stays in the fog.
- **Local-first with optional sign-in** — the app works fully offline with no
  account. Google or Apple sign-in is required only to add friends or attach a
  friend to a session.
- **Cloud backup is free** — signed-in users get all their user data backed up and
  restorable on a new device at no charge. Charging to prevent "my phone died
  and I lost two years of lifts" was judged a 1-star-review feature.
- **Not-signed-in users get manual export/import**, plus a dismissible sign-in
  nudge. Never a wall. Refined by the friend-graph ticket below: the nudge fires
  on session completion rather than app open, and dismissal always resolves to an
  explicit "remind me in 7 days" or "don't remind me again".
- **Friendship is separate from session access** — scanning a QR code adds a
  *friend* (a durable contact). Attaching a friend as a *recorder* is a
  per-session act by the session's owner.
- **Live sync, not merge-at-end** — a recorder's entries must appear on the
  owner's phone within seconds, or users will not trust the app and will
  double-log.
- **v1 is FitNotes minus cardio plus sharing** — a deliberately incomplete
  remake, favouring a tight hot path over faithfulness.
- [What exactly is being remade from FitNotes](https://github.com/kingdragonfly43/gym-notes/issues/3)
  — inventory captured in `docs/research/fitnotes-inventory.md` on branch
  `research/fitnotes-inventory`. Model is `Category → Exercise → Set → Comment`,
  with a workout being simply a date holding ordered exercises and **no
  planned-versus-actual concept at all**. The hot path is fast because entry
  fields are pre-populated from the first set of the last workout and the same
  fields serve both create and edit. FitNotes is closed-source, so everything is
  graded Confirmed / Community / Inferred.
- [Cross-platform stack evaluation](https://github.com/kingdragonfly43/gym-notes/issues/4)
  — comparison in `docs/research/stack-comparison.md` on branch
  `research/stack-comparison`. The sharpest discriminator is not SDK quality but
  the **Windows development machine**: all three stacks require macOS to compile
  iOS, and they differ only in how much of the loop runs off-machine. Flutter has
  the best SDK coverage by a distance (first-party Firebase, IAP and AdMob);
  Kotlin Multiplatform's gaps cluster exactly on this app's requirements, most
  sharply **no AdMob SDK at all**. Advisory pick is React Native / Expo, with
  Flutter a close second — and Flutter flips to the better answer the moment a
  Mac exists.
- [Backend and live-sync platform evaluation](https://github.com/kingdragonfly43/gym-notes/issues/5)
  — comparison in `docs/research/backend-comparison.md` on branch
  `research/backend-comparison`. The load is tiny, so no vendor scaling ceiling
  binds; the differentiators are free tier, offline story and SDK coverage.
  Firebase matches this project unusually well — offline persistence on by
  default, an anonymous-to-Google/Apple upgrade that preserves the UID and
  therefore the user's data, and a linear cost curve from zero. Supabase
  has no first-party offline persistence, which is this app's hardest
  requirement, and steps to a hard $25/month on idle auto-pause alone.
  Peer-to-peer was assessed genuinely and found structurally additive rather
  than alternative: free cloud backup is already committed, so a cloud path
  exists regardless, and client/AP isolation — a default on gym guest wifi —
  kills every local transport.
- [Friend graph, identity and sign-in flow](https://github.com/kingdragonfly43/gym-notes/issues/6)
  — QR-only, symmetric, request-gated. A scan is a *search*: it sends a friend
  request that the other party accepts, so the QR code can be permanent and
  static because the accept is the real consent gate. Removal is symmetric and
  silent; recorded sets always stay, since a recorder is provenance, not
  ownership. Identity is an anonymous account from first launch that links to a
  Google or Apple credential; an empty cloud account adopts the local user data
  silently, a non-empty one makes the user pick one side and discard the other
  (mobile-game save pattern), offering an export first. Sign-out keeps the local
  user data. **Account deletion is a v1 store-submission gate**, and is granular —
  cloud, local, or both. The sign-in nudge fires on session completion, gated on
  a suppression period. Vocabulary settled here is written down in `CONTEXT.md`.
- [Live shared session semantics](https://github.com/kingdragonfly43/gym-notes/issues/7)
  — a session has exactly **one writer at a time**, holding the session's **pen**.
  That replaced the append-only question rather than answering it: with one
  writer there is no conflict to resolve at all. The pen conveys every write
  including creating exercises in the owner's library, but **never delete**, which
  is owner-only and also requires the pen; deletes are mark-deletes so a removed
  exercise never orphans its sets. The **owner is the sole grantor** — attaching a
  recorder hands them the pen, recorders cannot hand it back or pass it on, and
  multiple recorders may be attached but there is still one pen. Attachment is a
  **notification, not a permission request**, and only ever to a live session. A
  recorder sees the owner's **whole record** — current session, all past sessions,
  statistics — and that access dies with the attachment. Mutual recording resolves
  to each person writing only into their buddy's session, which makes the
  recorder's screen the *primary* surface. An offline pen holder's writes **fail
  visibly** with no local queue and no automatic transfer. Ending a session
  detaches everyone; it can be reopened within **one hour**; **a date may hold
  several sessions**, told apart by start time. Two concepts came out of it and are
  now in `CONTEXT.md`: the **agenda** (free text describing what a session or split
  day is about, describing rather than prescribing) and the **split** (a recurring
  cycle of days each carrying an agenda), the latter ruled **into v1** and given
  its own ticket.

## Not yet specified

- **Store and developer account provisioning** — Google Play Console and Apple
  Developer enrolment, AdMob (or alternative) account setup. Gates any billing
  or ad configuration, and the Apple annual fee is a real input to a
  passive-income app's economics.
- **Privacy policy and store data-handling disclosures** — an app carrying ads,
  third-party sign-in, and body-related data needs these before submission.
  Shape depends on the chosen backend and ad network.
- **The subscription wedge** — still wanted, and now with its first plausible
  candidate: **AI-assisted training suggestions** — recommending the next set's
  weight and target reps from past performance and elapsed rest, and recommending
  rest length between sets. It fits the shape a subscription needs in a way cloud
  backup never did: recurring value, genuine per-use compute cost, and squarely
  aimed at power users rather than gating a table-stakes feature. Not yet a
  decision — it needs sizing against the domain model and the backend before it
  can be ticketed.
- **The standing view grant** — a durable, read-only window onto your training
  record granted to a chosen friend, independent of any live session. Deliberately
  parked when the shared-session ticket resolved: it is a *second* sharing model
  layered on the first, with its own scope, revocation and consent surfaces, and
  v1 already has one novel sharing mechanic to get right. One piece was carved out
  and is live now — a granted friend can see your **split**, which is what the
  coaching case actually needed. Sharpens once the split ticket lands.
- **Multi-device restore, schema versioning and migration** — once cloud backup
  exists, every schema change needs a migration story. Sharpens after the domain
  model lands.
- **Post-v1 feature backlog** — drop-sets, half-reps, cardio, FitNotes import,
  routines/programs, analytics. Deliberately deferred, listed here so the v1
  data model does not paint them into a corner.
- **Body measurement tracking** — FitNotes ships a Body Tracker (bodyweight and
  body measurements over time), which is effectively a second data model
  alongside the training data. It was never considered when the v1 line was drawn
  and needs an explicit in-or-out call.
- **The manual export/import format** — already committed to for users who never
  sign in. FitNotes' own CSV export is flat, lossy and one-way, so this format
  cannot simply copy it: it must round-trip losslessly. Sharpens once the domain
  model lands. **Now load-bearing well beyond its original case**: the identity
  ticket made it the escape hatch offered before the sign-in collision discard and
  before account deletion, so it is on the path of the two most destructive
  actions in the app rather than a convenience for the account-averse.
- **Whether the ad-supported model survives contact with the category** — the
  FitNotes inventory found it carries zero ads and markets itself on "free to
  use, and no ads - ever", monetizing instead through a separate paid companion
  app. This does not overturn the committed decision, but it means v1 is a
  strictly worse deal than the incumbent on the axis its own users chose it for.
  Revisit alongside the still-unfound subscription wedge.

## Out of scope

Ruled beyond this map's destination. These do not graduate; revisiting any of
them means redrawing the destination as a fresh effort.

- **Cardio logging in v1** — FitNotes ships it, but it is a different
  interaction shape and would compromise the resistance-training hot path that
  the whole "extremely easy" claim rests on.
- **Drop-sets and half-reps in v1** — wanted eventually, but they complicate the
  set-entry screen, which is the single most performance-critical surface.
- **FitNotes backup import in v1** — considered as an adoption lever and
  declined; the user does not need it themselves and is content to keep FitNotes
  installed for historical data.
- **Trainer/client roles and a trainer-tier subscription** — superseded by the
  symmetric peer model, which removes the role distinction entirely and with it
  the business case for charging one side.
- **Interstitial, video, or any non-banner advertising** — hostile mid-set, and
  not worth the revenue at this scale.
- **Shipping v1 to the stores** — this map ends at a buildable spec.
