## Problem Statement

Live and export share one engine today, but only because export is frame-locked to it. Every feature except tempo depends on the frame rate. An export starts cold, with no pre-roll. Section changes arrive a bar late, and drops arrive only once the bass is back. So a render cannot land a move on the real boundary.

The engine knows a new track has started only after 2 s of silence. So a crossfaded or gapless song inherits the last song's tempo, key and sections. During a crossfade the visual never sees the fade.

A take is look automation rendered offscreen afterwards. It leaves out the director, the reactions, the camera, touch and the seed, so the MP4 can differ from what the performer saw.

2D styles and simulations are isolated from the coming 3D Scene. The 3D families only look right facing forward. MilkDrop sits apart. Style packs and the user's own models have no delivery path and no limits.

## Solution

- **Live hears causally, export hears everything offline.**
  - The live visual hears only what has already played, for every input: track, mic, other apps' audio, wallpaper and dream.
  - An export analyses the whole file first with a separate offline analyser. It is non-causal and covers every feature: bands, levels and onsets, plus the beat grid, sections and drops.
  - The owner accepts that export can look different from live.
  - The resulting feature timeline is cached per track file and analyser version.
- **The player tells the engine what it knows.**
  - An explicit track-boundary command, carrying the new play seed.
  - The current fade gain, sent as a value.
  - The PCM tap stays first in the chain.
- **A take is a screen capture of the live visual.**
  - The video is the GL surface only, at screen size and frame rate. No UI is drawn.
  - The audio is what was heard, from any input.
  - The take also keeps the performer's choices on song time, so a track take can be rendered again.
  - **It ships before launch** and replaces wave 1's offscreen take (#227).
- **2D styles and simulations become generators inside Scenes.**
  - A Scene paints them on its sky, its walls, an object's material or a camera plane.
  - Full screen is the plane filling the view.
- **The 3D families run on "rails"** (today's camera limits) until each one is ported to a full 6-DoF environment.
- **MilkDrop stays a separate full-screen scene.**
- **Renders use the play seed.**
  - A render reuses the seed of the play on screen, or rolls a fresh one stored with the render.
  - Its settings are frozen when Render is tapped.
  - Whether a render follows Auto's Scene and style changes is a director setting, off by default.
- **Packs come from an in-app online store** of our packs only, each sold through Play Billing. This is new internet use beyond Cast.
- **The user's own glTF models import with research-based limits.** Import fits a model to the limits automatically where it can.

## Implementation Decisions

### Analysis (C++, `core/sound`)
- **Live analysis is causal and runs on the hop (ADR 0006).** Wallpaper and dream use the same path.
- **A separate offline analyser** is written fresh, not as a mode of the live detectors.
  - It ports librosa-style whole-track methods: beat tracking with a fitted beat grid, downbeats, CQT chroma, HPSS, onset strength with whitening, and recurrence-based section segmentation.
  - It writes the **same feature schema and ids** as live, so routes, Scenes and director rules read both alike.
  - It is designed and checked in the Python lab (#305).
- **Feature timeline:** the offline analyser's per-frame feature values for the whole track.
  - It is built as the first step of a render and cached on disk.
  - The cache key is the track file's identity plus the analyser version. Any version change invalidates it.
  - Batches reuse cached timelines.
  - The library never reads it.
- **Track-boundary command:** the player sends a new command carrying the new play seed.
  - On it, the engine resets Rhythm, Harmony and Form.
  - The Scene receives it as an event as strong as a section. Under #302's non-deterministic rules it may keep its world or change it.
  - The 2 s silence rule stays for inputs with no player: mic and capture.
- **Fade gain:** the player sends the current fade gain (crossfade, pause fade) as a value.
  - It becomes a signal source routes can read.
  - The analysis tap stays first in the chain, before DSP and volume, as `AUDIO_CHAIN.md` requires.

### Visual engine (C++, `core/viz`, `geode::scene`)
- **Generators:** every 2D shader style and simulation becomes a generator. It renders to a texture that a Scene binds to a surface: sky, wall, an object's material, or a camera plane.
  - Full screen is the camera plane filling the view, so today's look survives.
  - Generators keep their typed params, open to routes (#302, #305).
- **Who assigns generators:** a user setting. The default is "only I change them". The alternative lets the C++ director swap unpinned generators at sections and drops, from the play seed.
- **Rails:** an environment declares whether it is 6-DoF or on rails.
  - On rails, the camera stays inside today's rig limits: forward flight, yaw ±0.6, pitch ±0.45, roll ±0.8, no flips.
  - Families are ported one at a time to 6-DoF, following Morph World's `lib_world` pattern (PRs #314, #348). Each port flips its flag.
- **MilkDrop stays separate:** a full-screen projectM scene with its own beat detection and Geode's post pass. It is not a generator, and it has no Scene camera or objects.
- **Auto and the director move into C++,** so live and renders share them. Auto's style switching is still Kotlin today, which ADR 0003 forbids.

### Record (wave 1, before launch)
- **The capture:** a take encodes the live GL surface through a MediaCodec input surface, at screen size and display frame rate.
  - The renderer draws each frame twice: once to the screen and once to the encoder surface.
  - UI layers are never in the frame.
  - Frame pacing and timestamps come from the engine's frame clock, not from Kotlin.
- **Audio:**
  - Track takes take the processed output (EQ, ReplayGain, crossfade) from a second, record-only tap at the end of the audio chain. The analysis tap stays first. The chain stays integer PCM, never float.
  - Mic and capture takes mux the input's audio.
- **Choices:** a track take also stores the performer's choices on song time: style switches, params, touches, and the seed.
  - This is the take timeline in `core/signal/timeline` (ADR 0006).
  - "Render again" renders those choices through the offline export.
  - Mic and capture takes store their choices on wall time, and "Render again" is hidden for them.
- **Pause, seek and track change during a take** follow a Settings preference with three choices:
  - **Default:** pause holds the capture, a seek is a hard cut, a track change ends and saves the take.
  - Continuous: keeps rolling, and a take can span tracks.
  - Pause holds, and any seek ends the take.
- **Unchanged:** Record stays Pro. The first-take "where to save" sheet stays. A take still becomes a project that My videos lists.

### Export (C++, `core/output`, frozen render job)
- **The render job** freezes every engine setting when Render is tapped: look, routes, macros, director, Auto, generator assignment and seed. Live edits never reach it.
- **Seed:**
  - Rendering the track that is playing uses the current play seed.
  - Any other track (batch, playlist, chosen tracks) gets a fresh seed per track.
  - The seed is stored with the render.
- **"Renders follow Auto"** is a director setting, **off by default**. Off, a render keeps one Scene and style unless a take supplies switches. On, the C++ Auto runs inside the render on the offline sections.
- **Features:** an export reads every feature from the feature timeline, at the render's frame times.
- **MilkDrop renders** pin projectM's fps to the render's fps and feed it exactly that frame's PCM. projectM's own analysis is accepted.
- **The frame clock comes from samples, with pre-roll** (ADR 0006 `core/output`).

### Packs (store) and models
- **The store:** an in-app catalogue of **our packs only**. Each pack is a Play Billing in-app product, sold per pack and not included in Pro.
  - It is a static catalogue we host. There are no accounts and no analytics.
  - Each download is checked against a signature and a licence manifest before import.
  - Networking stays in one Kotlin package, as Cast's does.
  - It **needs a new ADR**, because it is internet use beyond Cast. The CI network and manifest gates, the privacy policy and Data safety change in the same PR as the store.
- **glTF import is C++,** using cgltf, meshoptimizer and the Basis Universal transcoder. Draco is re-encoded once on import, or rejected.

| Limit | Flagship | Simplified mode |
|---|---|---|
| File per model | 15 MB (refuse over 50 MB) | same |
| Triangles per model | 50k | 15k |
| Triangles, all imported models in a Scene | 300k | 75k |
| Textures | 2048² base colour, 1024² others | 1024² / 512² |
| Texture memory per model / per Scene | 32 MB / 192 MB | 8 MB / 48 MB |
| Materials per model | 8 | 4 |
| Model draws per Scene | 128 (repeated models instanced) | 48 |
| Skinning | 4 weights per vertex, up to 128 joints, 16 skinned models | 4 skinned models, 64 joints |
| Morph targets | up to 8 per mesh, 2 active | off |
| Animation | one clip plays at a time | same |

- **Import rules:**
  - Textures are downscaled, mipmapped and transcoded to ASTC (ETC2 where ASTC is missing).
  - A model up to 4× over the triangle cap is decimated automatically. The user sees the before and after counts and can undo it.
  - Import refuses a file that is malformed, uses an unknown required extension, or is still over after decimation.
  - A low-detail copy is made for the simplified mode.
  - Extensions:
    - Accepted: `KHR_texture_basisu`, `EXT_meshopt_compression`, `KHR_mesh_quantization`, `KHR_texture_transform`, `KHR_materials_unlit`, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`.
    - Ignored with a warning: the heavy `KHR_materials_*` extensions and punctual lights.
- **The numbers are final only after a device frame-time check** on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 and a Tensor G3. Sources: Google Scene Viewer, Meta Quest budgets, Adreno texture guidance.

### Changes to #302 and #305
- #302's line "Export only replays the same live scene faster than real time" no longer holds. An export renders the same Scene from the offline feature timeline.
- #302's open items are settled: style packs come from the in-app store, and the glTF limits are in the table above.
- #305 gains its loaders: cgltf, meshoptimizer and Basis Universal (all MIT or Apache-2.0).

## Testing Decisions

A good test checks behaviour a caller or user can see, not how it is built. Engine seams are C++ host tests in `core/tests`, each wired into `android.yml`.

1. **Offline analyser goldens:** compared with librosa goldens from the Python lab, within stated tolerances.
2. **Feature-timeline cache:** a round trip, the cache key (file plus analyser version), and invalidation.
3. **Track boundary and fade gain:** the boundary command resets tempo, key and form. The fade gain appears as a source. Extends the sound engine tests.
4. **Render job:**
   - Frozen settings.
   - Play seed versus a fresh stored seed.
   - "Renders follow Auto" off and on.
   - Milk on the export clock.
5. **Generator assignment:** generator-to-surface assignment, user pins, and director swaps only when the setting allows. Plus glslcheck stills.
6. **glTF import:** fixture .glb files for every limit, decimation and Undo data, refusals, and the low-detail copy.
7. **Capture take (journey test):** records a 2 s screen-capture take and checks that the MP4 has video frames and an audio track. This is the instrumented seam, like the existing journeys.
8. **Pack store:** JVM tests for the pack catalogue parse and signature check, plus the CI network gate updated for the store.
9. **Kotlin keep-out gate:** `tools/engine-kotlin-gate.sh` is extended to the new areas: the take's capture and choices, the Scene model, glTF and pack import, and the render job. New logic there in Kotlin fails CI.
10. **Analysis accuracy bench in CI:**
    - Both analysers are scored against labelled clips: beat, downbeat and section F-measure, and key accuracy. The metrics are mir_eval-style, as fosfora report 03 designs.
    - A Python CI job, with librosa pinned, regenerates the goldens and fails on regressions.
11. **End-to-end host test:**
    - An audio file goes through `core/output`, producing frames and the feature timeline, at 24, 30 and 60 fps.
    - It checks determinism (the same seed gives the same frames) and frame-rate invariance.
12. **Visual quality gates:**
    - glslcheck stills of every environment and generator. They fail on black frames, blown highlights or a flash-limit breach.
    - A device frame-time check per Scene in `DEVICE_CHECKS.md`.

**Prior art:**

- The journey suite and the chapter-markers export test
