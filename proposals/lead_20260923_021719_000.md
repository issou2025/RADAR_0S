# Project Proposal — Bim Modeling

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**IFC export carries almost no 3D: only conduit gets a solid**

*Project description:*
Found by an end-to-end audit of the IFC adapter against two real RoomPlan scans and the synthetic full-chain fixtures.

Entity survival and relationship integrity are fine — every entity is found by deterministic GUID, nothing is dropped, and 20/20 relationship checks pass. The problem is what those entities *look like* once exported.

## What actually has geometry

| Canonical entity | IFC representation |
|---|---|
| Route (conduit) | solid |
| Wall, Slab, Ceiling, Space, Conductor | **Axis curve only** |
| ElectricalDevice, ElectricalEquipment, panels, RouteFitting, Opening, Obstacle | **none at all** |

On real scan A, all 22 scanned objects export as `IfcBuildingElementProxy` with **no geometry and no placement** — their canonical `Box3D` pose and size are written nowhere native. Openings are worse than absent: `IfcRelVoidsElement` points at a shapeless opening, so it cuts nothing.

## Why this matters beyond aesthetics

The stated product goal is a usable 3D visual "down to the wires and devices and boxes and conduit". The IFC path delivers conduit. Everything else is a line or an invisible placeholder.

This is also the difference between an IFC file that *round-trips* and one that is *usable in a BIM tool*. A consumer opening this in Blender/Bonsai, Revit or Navisworks gets a wireframe with invisible equipment. Round-trip fidelity through our own adapter does not establish that, because our adapter reads the canonical JSON blob we also embed — it never has to rely on the native representation being there.

## What to build

Native representations for the entities that have the geometry to support one:

- **Walls, slabs, ceilings** — swept solids from centerline/footprint plus thickness and height. The canonical model already carries everything needed.
- **Spaces** — a volume from footprint and height, which is what makes a space navigable in a viewer.
- **Devices, equipment, panels, obstacles** — these carry a `Box3D` pose and `Size3` today and get nothing. Writing that as a placement plus an extruded or bounding solid is the smallest change with the largest visible effect.
- **Openings** — a real void solid, so the `IfcRelVoidsElement` relationship does something.
- **Conductors** — a swept solid along the route, or a deliberate decision that a curve is the right representation for a wire, recorded rather than defaulted.
- **Fittings** — decide and record; a fitting may legitimately be a placement-only occurrence.

## Rules this must respect

- **Never invent geometry a source does not support.** An entity whose canonical extent is unknown gets no solid and an explicit reason, not a default box. This is the same fail-closed rule the rest of the lane follows.
- **Provenance must survive whatever is written.** A solid built from an assumed dimension is an inferred solid. See the companion issue on provenance legibility — the two land together or the visual lies about its own confidence.
- **Validate strictly.** `ifcopenshell.validate` **without** `express_rules=True` reports "no diagnostics" while real IFC4 EXPRESS violations are present. That is how a missing-`ObjectPlacement` violation survived until this audit. Any work here must validate with `express_rules=True`.

## Acceptance

- Every canonical entity with sufficient geometry exports a native representation; every one without gets an explicit recorded reason.
- Strict EXPRESS validation clean.
- Evidence on real input, not only fixtures.
- The Blender/Bonsai leg is currently **unproven** — Blender is not installed on the audit host. Closing this issue honestly needs a host with Blender and Bonsai, opening the real-scan IFC and confirming the spatial tree, systems and geometry render.

Related: the boxes lane (`feat/c6-boxes-as-solids`) derives box solids that have nowhere to go until #84 lands, and this issue is what would make them visible.
