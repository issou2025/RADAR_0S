# Project Proposal — Structural Drawings

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Spec: Realtime transport, presence & typing**

*Project description:*
Part of #1 · Implements the decisions from #6 ([ADR 0005](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/Convoze/blob/main/docs/adr/0005-realtime-transport-and-flutter-socket-integration.md)). It builds on the auth spec #20 (access token, auth middleware, session-revoked hook) and the Flutter architecture in [ADR 0001](https://github.com/Dhruvivek/Convoze/blob/main/docs/adr/0001-flutter-client-architecture.md). It also completes features #4 (presence) and #5 (typing indicators) from `docs/features.md`. Glossary: `CONTEXT.md` (**Session**, **Device**, **Presence**).

**Depends on:** #20 (Phone + OTP authentication & sessions).

## Problem Statement

Once signed in (#20), a Convoze user still has no live connection to the server. Nothing tells them who is online, when someone was last around, or that the person they're talking to is typing. Signing out of a Device, or being signed out from another one, only takes effect on the next HTTP call. A long-lived realtime connection would keep a revoked Device live and listening. Messaging, receipts and group features in the next specs all need an authenticated, always-on channel that recovers from backgrounding and flaky networks without the user noticing, and that channel doesn't exist yet.

## Solution

While the user is signed in and the app is in the foreground, the app holds **one** live connection to the server:
- The connection **opens** automatically after sign-in and whenever the app comes back to the foreground.
- It **closes** whenever the app goes to the background.
- If the network drops, it **reconnects** by itself, with a small "Connecting…" indicator while it does.
- **Sign-out is immediate:** logging out, or being logged out from another Device, cuts the connection at once and sends that Device to the login screen.

Over this connection the user sees:
- **Presence:** whether the people they share conversations with are online, or when they were last seen. The app never falsely claims someone is "offline" just because its own connection is down; it shows "last seen" instead.
- **Typing:** "X is typing…" in a conversation, which disappears on its own within about 6 seconds if the typist stops or drops off.

On the server, a Socket.IO layer authenticates each connection with the #20 access token. It keeps a live-connection registry (Session → sockets, User → Sessions) and auto-joins each socket to the rooms for every Conversation the user participates in. It derives presence from the registry, relays typing without storing it, and force-disconnects every socket of a Session the moment that Session is revoked.

## User Stories

1. As a signed-in user, I want the app to connect for live updates automatically after I sign in, so that I never have to "go online" by hand.
2. As a user, I want only one connection per app no matter how many screens I open, so that the app doesn't waste battery or data.
3. As a user, I want live updates to reach my conversation list even when no chat is open, so that presence and notifications work everywhere in the app.
4. As a user, I want the app to disconnect when I put it in the background, so that it doesn't drain battery while I'm not using it.
5. As a user, I want the app to reconnect as soon as I bring it back to the foreground, so that I'm up to date immediately.
6. As a user who backgrounded the app for hours, I want it to reconnect without asking me to sign in again, so that my access token having expired in the meantime is invisible to me.
7. As a user on a flaky network, I want the app to keep retrying the connection in the background, so that I don't have to restart it after a dropout.
8. As a user, I want retries to back off (starting around 1 second, capped around 30 seconds), so that a long outage doesn't hammer my battery.
9. As a user, I want a subtle "Connecting…" indicator while the app is reconnecting, so that I understand why live updates have paused.
10. As a user, I want the indicator to disappear on its own once the connection is back, so that it's never left showing by mistake.
11. As a user, I want to be sent to the login screen if my Session has become unusable (refresh token expired or revoked) while reconnecting, so that I'm never stuck in an endless "Connecting…" state.
12. As a user who taps "Log out", I want my live connection closed immediately, so that this Device stops receiving my data at once.
13. As a user who taps "Log out other devices", I want every other Device's live connection cut immediately, so that a lost phone stops receiving my messages right now, not whenever it next makes a request.
14. As a user whose Session was revoked from another Device, I want my app to go straight to the login screen, so that I'm not left looking at stale data.
15. As a user, I want to see whether the people I share a conversation with are online, so that I know whether to expect a quick reply.
16. As a user, I want to see when someone was last online if they aren't online now, so that I can judge how active they are.
17. As a user, I want presence to be correct the moment the app connects, without waiting for people to come and go, so that I don't see wrong statuses after opening the app.
18. As a user, I want someone to count as online if they have the app open on any of their Devices, so that presence reflects the person, not one phone.
19. As a user, I don't want someone to flicker offline when they only switch from their phone to their tablet, so that presence is stable.
20. As a user whose own connection has dropped, I want other people shown as "last seen at <time>" rather than "offline", so that my app doesn't make claims it can't verify.
21. As a user, I want presence to become accurate again as soon as my connection is back, so that the "last seen" state is only shown while it's needed.
22. As a user, I want only people I share a conversation with to see my presence, so that strangers can't track when I'm online.
23. As a user, I want to see "X is typing…" in a conversation when someone is composing a message, so that I know a reply is coming.
24. As a user in a group, I want to see which members are typing, so that I know who is about to reply.
25. As a user, I want the typing indicator to disappear within about 6 seconds if the other person stops typing or loses connection, so that it never gets stuck.
26. As a user, I want the typing indicator to disappear immediately when the other person sends their message, so that the indicator and the message don't overlap.
27. As a user, I don't want my own typing shown back to me on my other Devices, so that I don't see phantom indicators.
28. As a user who reconnects, I want any typing indicators from before the drop cleared, so that I never see stale "typing…" left over from before.
29. As a user, I want typing to be sent sparingly while I type, so that a long message doesn't flood the network.
30. As the product owner, I want only authenticated Sessions to be able to open a connection, so that anonymous clients can't listen in.
31. As the product owner, I want the access token sent in the connection's auth payload, never in the URL, so that it doesn't end up in server or proxy logs.
32. As the product owner, I want a user to receive events only for Conversations they participate in, so that no one can eavesdrop by joining arbitrary rooms.
33. As the product owner, I want typing events from a user who isn't a participant of that Conversation ignored, so that clients can't inject fake indicators.
34. As a backend developer, I want a function that joins all of a user's live sockets to a Conversation's room (and one that removes them), so that group and new-conversation features can add live members without reconnecting them.
35. As a backend developer, I want one shared "authenticate an access token" routine used by both the HTTP middleware and the socket handshake, so that REST and realtime can never drift apart on what counts as a valid Session.
36. As a backend developer, I want the live-connection registry to be the one place that answers "is this user online" and "which sockets belong to this Session", so that routing, presence and revocation stay consistent.
37. As a Flutter developer, I want `lib/core/` to expose only the raw connected socket and a connection-status stream, so that each feature owns its own events, as ADR 0001 and ADR 0005 require.
38. As a Flutter developer, I want presence and typing exposed as Riverpod providers (presence per user, typers per conversation), so that the conversation list and chat screens built later just watch them.

## Implementation Decisions

### Backend

**Schema change**
- Add `User.lastSeenAt` (timestamptz, nullable). It records **when the user's last live socket disconnected**. "Online" stays derived from the registry and is never stored (per `CONTEXT.md`); only the timestamp is persisted.
- As part of implementing this spec, update the `User` block of ADR 0004's schema and the **Presence** entry in `CONTEXT.md` to mention `lastSeenAt`.

**Modules**
- **Access-token authenticator** (refactored out of #20's auth middleware): takes a raw access token and returns `{ userId, sessionId }` or a failure. It checks the signature, expiry, and that the Session exists with `revokedAt` null. Both the HTTP middleware and the socket handshake call it.
- **Realtime server**: Socket.IO attached to the same HTTP server that the #20 app factory builds, with the same injected dependencies (Prisma, clock). Transport is WebSocket only; no HTTP long-polling fallback is needed because the only client is the Flutter app.
- **Handshake middleware**:
  - It reads the token from the handshake **`auth` payload** (`{ token }`) and ignores query strings entirely.
  - On failure it rejects the connection with a `connect_error` whose message is `unauthenticated`. The server never accepts a connection and then boots it.
  - On success it attaches `userId` and `sessionId` to the socket.
  - A validated socket is **not** re-checked when its token's 15-minute TTL passes, because revocation is pushed instead (ADR 0005).
- **Live-connection registry**: in memory, one server process. It keeps three maps:
  - `sessionId → Set<socketId>` (a Session can briefly own two sockets during a reconnect handoff);
  - `userId → Set<sessionId>`;
  - `socketId → { userId, sessionId }`.

  It exposes: register socket, unregister socket (reporting whether the user just went from 1 to 0 live sockets), sockets for a Session, whether a user is online, and the online users among a given set of user IDs.
- **Room model**:
  - Each Conversation has a room `conversation:<id>`.
  - Each user has a personal room `user:<id>`, which later features (delivery #17, membership changes) use to reach all of a user's Devices.
  - **Auto-join:** on connect, the server joins the socket to its personal room and to every Conversation room that the user has a `Participant` row for. Clients can't join or leave rooms themselves; there is no client join/leave event.
  - **Membership helpers** for later specs: *join user to conversation* adds all of a user's live sockets to that Conversation's room, and *remove user from conversation* takes them out. Group-chat and new-conversation work must call these whenever `Participant` rows change.
- **Presence service**:
  - A user is **online** when they have ≥1 live socket on any Session.
  - The **audience** for a user's presence is the set of users who share at least one Conversation with them.

  | Event | Server behaviour |
  |---|---|
  | Socket connects and the user goes from 0 to 1 live sockets | Emit `userOnline` to the user's Conversation rooms. |
  | Every successful connect | After auto-join, send the connecting socket a **`presenceSnapshot`** covering everyone in its presence audience (see the event contract). |
  | Last socket disconnects (user goes from 1 to 0) | Write `User.lastSeenAt = now` (via the clock), then emit `userOffline` to the user's Conversation rooms. |
  | Switching between Devices (one socket closes while another is open) | Nothing is emitted. |
  | Graceful shutdown (SIGTERM) | Write `lastSeenAt` for every currently online user before closing sockets. |

- **Typing relay**:
  - The server is **stateless** for typing and remembers nothing.
  - It checks that the socket is in `conversation:<id>`, and silently drops the event if not.
  - Otherwise it broadcasts to that room, excluding the sending socket, with the sender's `userId` added.
  - Clients ignore typing events whose `userId` is their own, which covers the sender's other Devices.
- **Revocation subscriber**:
  - It subscribes to #20's **session-revoked hook**.
  - For each socket the registry holds for the revoked `sessionId`, it emits `sessionRevoked` and then force-disconnects the socket.
  - Normal disconnect handling then updates the registry and presence.
  - This is what makes ADR 0003's "revocation is immediate" true for sockets. Weakening it silently breaks that guarantee (ADR 0005 consequences).

**Event contract**

Server → client:

| Event | Payload | When |
|---|---|---|
| `presenceSnapshot` | `{ users: [{ userId, online, lastSeenAt \| null }] }` | Immediately after every successful connect |
| `userOnline` | `{ userId }` | A user in a shared Conversation goes from 0 to 1 live sockets |
| `userOffline` | `{ userId, lastSeenAt }` | A user in a shared Conversation goes from 1 to 0 live sockets |
| `typing` | `{ conversationId, userId }` | A participant started or is still typing |
| `stopTyping` | `{ conversationId, userId }` | A participant stopped typing (paused, cleared the input, or sent) |
| `sessionRevoked` | `{}` | Just before the server force-disconnects a revoked Session's socket |

Client → server:

| Event | Payload | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `typing` | `{ conversationId }` | Throttled by the client (see below) |
| `stopTyping` | `{ conversationId }` | |

**Scaling note:** the registry and rooms assume **one Node process**. Running more than one instance needs a Socket.IO adapter and a shared registry, which this spec doesn't cover (see Out of Scope).

### Flutter client

- **Dependency added:** `socket_io_client`.
- **Socket factory** (a Riverpod provider in `lib/core/`, overridable in tests): builds a configured, not-yet-connected socket for `AppConfig.socketUrl`. The configuration is:
  - transport: WebSocket only;
  - auto-connect: off;
  - the library's built-in reconnection, tuned to a ~1 s initial delay, a ~30 s maximum delay, randomisation on, and unlimited attempts.
- **Connection manager** (a `keepAlive` provider in `lib/core/`):
  - It watches the #20 auth state. It creates and connects the socket when auth becomes `authenticated`, and disconnects and disposes it on `unauthenticated`.
  - It exposes the **raw socket** and a **connection-status stream** with the states `connecting`, `connected`, `reconnecting` and `offline` (backgrounded or signed out).
  - It doesn't parse any feature events. That stays in each feature's repository.
- **Token freshness, proactive first:** before every connect and reconnect attempt, the manager checks the stored access token's expiry. If the token is expired or within ~60 s of expiry, it refreshes first, through #20's **same single-flight refresh** that the Dio interceptor uses, then puts the fresh token into the socket's `auth` payload.
- **Token freshness, reactive fallback:** on a `connect_error` of `unauthenticated`, the manager refreshes once and retries once. If the refresh fails (refresh token expired or revoked), it stops reconnecting and goes through **#20's sign-out path**: clear the tokens, keep the Device ID, set auth to `unauthenticated`, and the router shows `/login`. There is one dead-Session path shared by HTTP and the socket.
- **Revocation:** when the client receives `sessionRevoked`, it goes straight to the same sign-out path, with no refresh attempt and no reconnect.
- **App lifecycle** (a `WidgetsBindingObserver` owned by the connection manager):

  | Lifecycle state | Action |
  |---|---|
  | `paused`, `hidden` or `detached` | Disconnect deliberately; status becomes `offline`. |
  | `resumed` | Run the proactive token check, then connect. |
  | `inactive` (iOS transient states, e.g. the control centre) | Ignored. |

- **Connection indicator:** a small, non-blocking "Connecting…" banner shown in the signed-in shell while the status is `connecting` or `reconnecting`. It isn't shown for `offline` caused by backgrounding.
- **Presence feature** (`features/presence/data` plus providers):
  - The presence repository takes the core socket and turns `presenceSnapshot`, `userOnline` and `userOffline` into a per-user state: `online`, `lastSeen(time)` or `unknown`.
  - A snapshot **replaces** all previously held presence.
  - While the client's own status isn't `connected`, a provider for each user's presence **downgrades** `online` to `lastSeen(<moment our own socket dropped>)`. It never shows "offline" for anyone during that gap (`CONTEXT.md` **Presence**).
  - The next snapshot restores the real state.
  - A shared formatter renders the state as "online", "last seen today at 14:05", "last seen yesterday" and so on.
- **Typing feature** (a typing repository under `features/chat/data`, plus providers):
  - **Sending:** while the input changes, emit `typing` at most once every 3 s. Emit `stopTyping` on send, when the input is cleared, after 5 s without keystrokes, or when leaving the chat.
  - **Receiving:** keep the set of typers for each Conversation. Each entry expires **6 s** after the last `typing` event for that user, and `stopTyping` removes it at once.
  - Also remove a user when their `userOffline` arrives, and ignore events carrying our own `userId`.
  - **On any disconnect of our own socket, drop all typing state unconditionally** (ADR 0005); the typing timeout covers anything missed.
- **UI surfaces:**
  - This spec delivers the providers and the connection banner.
  - Presence and typing get rendered in the conversation list rows and the chat header when those screens are built by the messaging spec, which only has to watch these providers.
  - Until then, an interim debug-only surface isn't required.

## Testing Decisions

**What makes a good test here:** it asserts on behaviour visible at the network boundary (events received, connections accepted or rejected, disconnects) or in the user's app (screens, indicators, presence and typing state as the UI would read it). It never asserts on registry internals or on how many times a private function ran. Swapping the registry's data structures, or reorganising the providers, should break no test.

**Backend: one seam, a real server with real clients.**
- The tests start the #20 app factory, with the realtime server attached, on a random port against a **real Postgres test DB**. The fakes are the same as #20: a fake Verify client and a controllable clock.
- Sessions are created **through the real OTP endpoints**. Conversations and Participants are seeded in the DB.
- Tests connect real `socket.io-client` sockets and assert only on events, connect errors, disconnects and HTTP responses.
- Cases to cover:
  - **Handshake:**
    - A valid token connects.
    - A missing, malformed or expired token, or a revoked Session, gets a `connect_error` `unauthenticated`.
    - A token sent only in the query string is rejected.
    - A connected socket stays connected after the clock moves past the 15-minute token TTL.
  - **Rooms:**
    - A socket receives Conversation events only for Conversations it participates in.
    - After *join user to conversation*, an already-connected user's sockets start receiving that room's events without reconnecting.
    - After *remove user from conversation*, they stop receiving them.
  - **Presence:**
    - The first socket fires `userOnline` to people who share a Conversation, and nobody else.
    - A second Device doesn't fire a second `userOnline`.
    - Closing one of two Devices fires nothing.
    - Closing the last Device fires `userOffline` with the clock's time and persists `lastSeenAt`.
    - `presenceSnapshot` on connect lists exactly the audience with the correct online and `lastSeenAt` values.
    - Presence of users without a shared Conversation is never revealed.
  - **Typing:**
    - Typing is relayed to the other participants with the sender's `userId`, and not echoed to the sending socket.
    - Typing for a Conversation the sender isn't in is dropped.
    - `stopTyping` is relayed the same way.
  - **Revocation:**
    - `POST /auth/logout` sends `sessionRevoked` and disconnects every socket of that Session, and only that Session.
    - `POST /auth/sessions/logout-others` disconnects every other Session's sockets and leaves the caller's alone.
    - Refresh-token reuse detection (from #20) disconnects that Session's sockets.
    - After revocation, a reconnect with the old access token is rejected.
- **Prior art:** #20's HTTP-seam tests, which use the same app factory, fakes and DB setup.

**Flutter: one layer, an end-to-end suite against the real backend.**

The user chose this during spec review. The client has no fake-socket widget tests; every Flutter behaviour in this spec is verified end to end.

- **Harness:**
  - Flutter `integration_test`, running the real `ConvozeApp` on an emulator or simulator.
  - It talks to a **real local backend and Postgres**, with the backend started in **e2e mode**.
  - App lifecycle (background/foreground) is driven through the test binding.
  - Presence and typing state are read through the app's providers (the same public interface the future screens will use), and the connection banner and routing are asserted on screen.
- **"Other users" in a test:**
  - Each test signs in additional users or Devices through #20's test-side helper (real HTTP API).
  - This spec extends the helper to open real Dart Socket.IO client connections for those users.
  - This is how a test makes someone else come online, type, or call logout-others.
- **Backend e2e mode:** introduced by #20, which provides the production guard, the fixed-code fake Verify client, configurable token TTLs (used here to reach token expiry, proactive refresh and reactive refresh-and-retry), and the reset, refresh-count and fault-injection endpoints. This spec adds two test-only endpoints:
    - seed Conversations and Participants;
    - **drop the transport** of a user's live sockets without a server-initiated disconnect, which simulates a network loss so the client's automatic reconnect runs.
- **Cases to cover:**
  - **Connection lifecycle:**
    - After sign-in, the socket connects and a presence snapshot arrives.
    - Signing out disconnects the socket.
    - Backgrounding disconnects it, and the other users see `userOffline`.
    - Foregrounding reconnects it and presence is restored.
    - `inactive` doesn't disconnect.
  - **Reconnect:**
    - Dropping the transport shows the "Connecting…" banner, reconnects automatically, then hides the banner.
    - Restarting the backend process leads to automatic reconnection.
  - **Token freshness:**
    - With a short TTL, reconnecting after the token expired refreshes *before* connecting.
    - A refresh from the socket and one from the Dio interceptor happening together cause exactly one refresh call, read via the control endpoint.
    - A rejected handshake causes one refresh then a retry.
  - **Dead Session:**
    - A second Device calling logout-others sends the app to `/login` within a few seconds, with the Device ID kept.
    - An expired or revoked refresh token during reconnect also lands on `/login`.
  - **Presence:**
    - Another user coming online or going offline updates presence.
    - While the app's own transport is dropped, previously online users read as `lastSeen(...)` and never as offline.
    - After reconnecting, the snapshot restores the true state.
    - A user with no shared Conversation never appears.
  - **Typing:**
    - Another user's typing appears. It clears on `stopTyping`, clears within about 6 s when they go silent, and clears when they go offline.
    - Typing from our own second Device is ignored.
    - All typers are cleared when our transport drops.
    - While the user types in the app, outgoing `typing` events are throttled (observed by the other user's client) and `stopTyping` is sent on send and when idle.
- **Speed:** these tests are slower than widget tests. Keep the suite focused on the cases above, share one backend process across the run, and reset state through the control endpoint rather than restarting.
- **Prior art:** #20's Flutter e2e suite (the harness, e2e mode and helper). Later specs (messaging #17, media) extend it the same way.

## Out of Scope

- **Sending and receiving messages:** send, ack, dedupe, delivery queue, catch-up cursors and `markAsRead` events are all #17 (delivery pipeline). This spec only provides the authenticated channel and rooms they run on.
- The local database and offline caching (#18).
- Push notifications for backgrounded or offline Devices (#8). While backgrounded, the Device has no socket by design.
- **Running more than one backend instance:** Socket.IO adapters, a shared or distributed registry, and sticky sessions. The in-memory registry assumes one process.
- Presence privacy settings (hiding last seen or online status), and "last seen" rounded to fuzzy ranges.
- Rendering presence and typing in the conversation list and chat header. Those screens come from the messaging spec, which watches the providers defined here.
- A "list my active Devices" settings screen.
- Group-membership features themselves. This spec only provides the room join/remove helpers they must call.
- Rate limiting of socket events (feature #15, specced separately).

## Further Notes

- **Decisions made while writing this spec** (beyond ADR 0005; flag them if you disagree before implementing):
  - `User.lastSeenAt` is stored and set when the user's last socket disconnects. The user chose this during spec review; ADR 0004 and `CONTEXT.md` get updated as part of implementation.
  - The presence audience is "users who share at least one Conversation".
  - A personal room `user:<id>` exists alongside the Conversation rooms.
  - WebSocket-only transport.
  - The event names follow `docs/features.md` (`userOnline`, `userOffline`, `typing`, `stopTyping`), with `presenceSnapshot` and `sessionRevoked` added.
  - The typing timings: send throttled to one per 3 s, 5 s idle before `stopTyping`, receiver timeout 6 s.
  - The ~60 s near-expiry margin for the proactive token refresh.
  - Flutter testing is an e2e suite only (the user's choice), reusing #20's backend e2e mode and adding the seed-conversation and drop-transport endpoints.
- **Library behaviour to verify while implementing:**
  - Whether `socket_io_client` lets the `auth` payload be updated between automatic reconnect attempts, or re-reads it on each attempt. If it doesn't, the connection manager should update it whenever the token store changes.
  - Whether a server-initiated disconnect suppresses the library's automatic reconnect. It should, so that `sessionRevoked` isn't followed by a doomed retry loop.
- **Known limitation:** if the Node process crashes (as opposed to shutting down gracefully), users who were online don't get `lastSeenAt` updated for that disconnect, so their "last seen" shows an earlier time until they next connect and disconnect.
- **Effect of backgrounding on presence:** every background/foreground cycle makes a user go offline and online again for their contacts. That follows from ADR 0005's lifecycle policy and matches "online = app open".
- **Next step:** the delivery pipeline (#17) plugs into this spec's rooms, personal room and registry. The messaging UI spec renders presence and typing from the providers defined here.
