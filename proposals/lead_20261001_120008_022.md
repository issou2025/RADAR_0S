## Status / type
**Transfer + Paradigm-Challenge research idea — unverified.** This is about whether the visible order of already-permitted interface steps communicates a pending goal early enough for a human observer to notice or correct a mismatch. It is not consent, authorization, a product UI design, or evidence that the current runtime harms human oversight.

## Source and what it establishes
- Dragan, Lee & Srinivasa, “Legibility and Predictability of Robot Motion,” HRI 2013, DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/HRI.2013.6483603; [author/institution record](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/legibility-and-predictability-of-robot-motion). The paper distinguishes predicting a motion from inferring its goal and reports that the two can conflict in robot trajectories. It does not study GUIs or software-agent actions.
- Faria, Melo & Paiva, “Guess what I'm doing: Extending legibility to sequential decision tasks,” arXiv:2209.09141 (2022 preprint; later *Artificial Intelligence* article), https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09141 . It extends goal-legibility beyond motion to sequential decision policies and evaluates simulated tasks and human goal inference. It does not establish the proposed Agent Interface effect or a universal benefit of legible action order.

External legibility research already exists. The candidate novelty is a repository-specific, **effect-preserving GUI action-order** test, not a new legibility theory.

## Repository problem and structural analogy
README/ROADMAP seek efficient local continuation with fewer model boundaries, and #57/#59 require end-to-end correctness. Such a continuation may be opaque to a human watching the shared UI until the final effect. #5324 covers transfer of responsibility, #5830 tests human takeover after an unexpected stop, #5698 tests reliance on final outcome receipts, and #5800 tests whether agent-facing cues interfere with human/assistive-technology use. None of those inspected Issues measures **pre-effect human inference of the agent's pending target/effect from an otherwise valid action prefix**.

Robot motion: the observer sees a partial trajectory and infers a goal. GUI analogue: a person sees a partial sequence of focus, navigation, selection, and non-effectful preparation, and infers which document/target/effect the agent is about to pursue. Both have multiple possible goals and a time window for correction. The analogy breaks when actions are instantaneous, the human is not watching, semantic targets are invisible, or a legible prefix changes app state/safety. No human inference is assumed from a private model-only trace.

## Proposed idea
For a small declared desktop task family, identify two or more **semantically equivalent, authority-valid, independently scored** ways to order preparatory steps before the consequential effect. Candidate policies:
1. `EFFICIENCY_ORDER`: shortest safe route under the same task contract;
2. `LEGIBLE_ORDER`: reorder only independent, non-effectful steps so the intended target/effect is visible earlier to an observer;
3. `EXPLICIT_PREVIEW`: a separate communication control with the same effect claim, charged for its display/attention cost.

Do not add fake cursor theatrics, unrelated detours, or shared overlays as a free cue. The action sequence must remain correct without a human watching. Treat early human disagreement as a potential stop/request-for-clarification signal only if ordinary authority and safe-stop contracts permit it; silence or failure to object is **never** consent. Keep model-only cues separate from the shared surface under #5800.

## H / T / D / C / U
- **H:** On a frozen disposable GUI task family with at least two plausible target/effect goals, a valid legible-order prefix improves preregistered early human goal/effect inference or earlier correct mismatch detection over a matched efficiency-order prefix, without increasing wrong/collateral effects or materially worsening completed-task time. It may fail if the interaction is too fast for human observation.
- **T0 (no participants):** Build a finite task/action DAG and two prefix schedules from existing safe operations. Independent scorer verifies both schedules have the same required and forbidden effects, authority/lease/focus constraints, cleanup, and total action set; a purposely misleading prefix is a negative control. Freeze exactly when the human-visible prefix is cut and what facts are available. T0 establishes schedule equivalence, not human benefit.
- **T1 (separate voluntary consent/privacy approval):** In an isolated non-sensitive GUI fixture, randomize display of recorded prefixes with matched task/goal distribution and counterbalanced order. Ask participants to infer the intended target/effect at frozen prefix cut points and choose whether to allow, query, or stop in seeded goal-mismatch controls. Keep the same underlying task truth and independently scored final effect; measure early inference accuracy, calibrated uncertainty, correct mismatch interception, false interventions, viewing time, total task wall time, and accessibility/attention burden. No live personal desktop, real user file, or consequential action is authorized here.
- **D:** `METHOD_PASS_SCOPED` only if T0 independently establishes effect/authority equivalence and the misleading-prefix control is distinguishable in the oracle. `H_PASS_SCOPED` only if eligible T1 improves a frozen early-inference/mismatch endpoint with no safety regression and an acceptable preregistered time/attention cost. `H_FAIL_SCOPED` if the legible order is no better, too slow, misleading, or causes extra intervention/wrong effects. `HOLD` without comparable prefixes, consent, human-visible timing, independent effect truth, or adequate participant evidence. T0 does not validate human legibility.
- **C:** A direct, source-bound intent/footprint preview may dominate reordering; or the human may never watch a subsecond local-control prefix, so final typed receipts and takeover packets are the right interface. The apparently legible sequence may merely be slower, allowing more viewing time rather than conveying intent.
- **U:** GUI goals can be ambiguous even to experts; expertise, language, accessibility, workload, and prior familiarity affect inference. Recorded-prefix vignettes may not predict live intervention. A legible but incorrect intent is worse than an opaque safe stop. No human inference result grants authority or proves final task success.

## Expected value / cost / risk
This tests whether efficiency-only ordering omits a human-coordination property relevant to the ROADMAP's user-facing preview and real-time control, while respecting #57's correctness gate. If positive, it could motivate bounded ordering of already-valid preparation steps or a more direct intent channel; if negative, it supports keeping control fast and relying on explicit outcome/takeover artifacts instead. T0 is a low-cost DAG/oracle audit; T1 requires participant governance, disposable fixtures and independent effect scoring. Main risks are unverified equivalence, hidden state changes during “non-effectful” steps, false confidence from simulations, and turning legibility into a deceptive or coercive cue.
