# Project Proposal — Flutter App

## Project Understanding
You need assistance with: 
**Foreground download service restarts itself extremely fast (sub-second), sometimes killing the whole app process — chapters never download, high CPU/battery, notification flashes**

*Project description:*
## Summary
On at least one device (Google Pixel 9a, stock Android — not an aggressive-battery-management OEM skin), the offline-download foreground service (`DownloadTaskHandler` in `download_task_handler.dart`) enters a restart loop: it starts, gets torn down before a single chapter download can complete, and gets restarted again — sometimes within under a second, sometimes with a few seconds between attempts. The Android notification for "Downloading chapters" visibly flashes/reappears in sync with this. CPU load from this loop is high enough to make image scrolling elsewhere in the app noticeably janky. In the worst observed case, the entire app process was also killed and relaunched from scratch twice in under 15 minutes, with no user action.

## What we've ruled out
- **Not our own retry/reconcile logic tight-looping.** Instrumented the drain loop with an unconditional per-restart heartbeat; on the rare restart that survived more than ~1 second, it correctly advanced past the first chapter and moved to the next one — the loop logic itself works fine when given time to run.
- **Not an uncaught Dart exception in the service isolate.** Wrapped the entire `onStart` body in try/catch and the isolate entry point in `runZonedGuarded`, reporting any exception back to the main isolate's diagnostic sink. Across many reproductions, zero exceptions were ever reported.
- **Not aggressive OEM battery/process management.** Device is a Pixel running stock Android — not one of the manufacturer skins known for killing background services outside Doze-exempt allowlists.
- **Not caused by our own recent commits.** Audited the last 3 days of history (37 commits) — nothing touches `download_task_handler.dart`, `notification_worker.dart`, `main.dart`, `AndroidManifest.xml`, or Gradle config. The only recent commit touching the foreground-service controller is a cosmetic toast-on-failure addition.
- **Not simple lock contention alone**, though it does happen: we also see the service legitimately fail to acquire a cross-isolate file lock against the WorkManager catch-up executor and back off correctly (`lock-acquire-failed` after the full 30s retry window) — but this is a separate, correctly-handled path, not the main symptom.

## What the logs show
Every restart is attributed `TaskStarter.developer` (i.e., the plugin reports our own app code called `startService`, not an OS-triggered restart) — but restarts recur far faster and more often than any of our own retry/backoff logic would explain, and with the whole app process itself occasionally restarting too, we suspect this label may not be reliable for distinguishing "our code decided to restart" from "the OS killed the service and something reactive on our side restarted it immediately."

Example excerpt (timestamps in ISO8601, one boot sequence followed by a burst of restarts):
```
offline-fgs: onStart-entered starter=developer
offline-fgs: heartbeat iteration=1 queueLen=3 nextChapterId=152323 inFlight=null
offline-fgs: onStart-entered starter=developer
offline-fgs: heartbeat iteration=1 queueLen=6 nextChapterId=152323 inFlight=null
offline-fgs: onStart-entered starter=developer
offline-fgs: heartbeat iteration=1 queueLen=3 nextChapterId=152323 inFlight=null
... (repeats every ~0.7-1s, same chapterId, never advancing) ...
```

## Current mitigation (not a root-cause fix)
A defensive circuit breaker (not yet upstreamed — flagging here first): if the same chapter is the first thing attempted across 5 service restarts within a 20s window, it's forced to `error` state so it stops blocking the rest of the download queue. This stops the practical symptom (the loop does terminate) but does NOT explain or fix why the service keeps getting torn down, and it has the side effect of marking innocent chapters as "error" even though nothing is actually wrong with their download.

## Suspected next step
Since we've now instrumented everything reachable from Dart, and nothing shows up, this likely requires native Android `logcat` during a live reproduction to catch a system-level signal (e.g. `ForegroundServiceDidNotStartInTimeException`, an OOM kill, an ANR) that wouldn't be visible to any Flutter-level error handler. Haven't captured this yet — will follow up if/when we do.
