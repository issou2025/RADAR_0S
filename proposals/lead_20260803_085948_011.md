## Stage result

At completion, the learner should be able to:

- sustain connected conversation about familiar and moderately abstract subjects;
- retell events and source material with accurate sequence, viewpoint, and attribution;
- explain causes, consequences, contrasts, concessions, expectations, and uncertainty;
- distinguish observation, inference, hearsay, probability, judgment, and personal stance;
- use intermediate nominalization and subordinate clauses without translated English structure;
- describe change, continuation, completion, preparation, and developing tendencies;
- use the common `わけ`, `はず`, and `べき` families with their restrictions and pragmatic force;
- summarize interviews, articles, explanations, and short narratives;
- compare alternatives, qualify opinions, disagree tactfully, and repair misunderstandings;
- follow and produce ordinary conversational reductions, ellipsis, omitted arguments, and sentence-final stance;
- distinguish ordinary conversation, polite interaction, messages, and neutral written exposition;
- read and type normal intermediate Japanese with cumulative growth toward roughly 4,000–5,500 useful words/chunks and 700–900 high-value kanji across stages 00–03.

This is a listening-and-production curriculum, not an N3 test-preparation list.

## Stage boundary

The coverage matrix must assign every included item to one or more slices and state the exact prerequisite slices. A card may not use a later Japanese 03 construction in its supporting sentence unless that dependency is explicitly declared and precedes it.

### Subordination and connected clauses

Cover high-frequency intermediate clause relations in natural contexts, including:

- cause and grounds beyond beginner `から/ので`;
- contrast and parallel development with forms such as `一方で` and stage-appropriate alternatives;
- concession with common N3-range forms and clear pragmatic distinctions;
- purpose, result, sequence, simultaneity, and backgrounding;
- `うちに`, `間/間に`, `最中に`, `たびに`, and other justified temporal relations;
- `につれて/にしたがって` for linked change where appropriate;
- `ところ` patterns for action phase and timing;
- clause chaining that remains readable and natural rather than imitating English prose.

Do not teach connectors as interchangeable translations. Make the relation audible and visible from the complete situation.

### Probability, evidence, inference, and reported information

Develop controlled contrasts among:

- appearance and hearsay `そうだ` from Japanese 02;
- `らしい`;
- `ようだ`;
- conversational `みたいだ`;
- `かもしれない`;
- `でしょう/だろう`;
- `はず`;
- other common N3-range probability or inference expressions justified by the matrix.

Every card must make the evidence source, report source, probability, or speaker inference clear. Do not collapse these forms into “seems” or “probably.”

### `わけ`, `はず`, and judgment families

Teach the common families through direct contrasts:

```text
わけだ
わけではない
わけがない
わけにはいかない
はずだ
はずがない
はずだった
べきだ
べきではない
```

Cover:

- logical consequence versus explanation;
- partial negation versus complete impossibility;
- social or practical impossibility;
- expectation based on evidence;
- failed expectation;
- advice, obligation, and moral judgment;
- differences in force, relationship, and register.

Do not overproduce `べき` where ordinary advice or a softer expression is more natural.

### Nominalization and information structure

Develop:

- `の` and `こと` beyond the elementary patterns;
- topic/focus contrasts in connected discourse;
- `ということ`, `というの`, and stage-appropriate explanatory nominalization;
- compact abstract reference to events, claims, decisions, and problems;
- natural use of omitted heads and recoverable arguments;
- avoiding excessive explicit subjects, pronouns, and repeated nouns.

Reserve dense formal nominalized prose and advanced academic syntax for Japanese 04.

### Aspect, completion, continuation, and change

Develop and contrast:

- `〜ていく` and `〜てくる` for movement, change, and developing tendencies;
- `〜ようになる` and `〜ようにする`;
- `〜ことになる` and `〜ことにする`;
- `〜始める`, `〜続ける`, `〜終わる`, and stage-appropriate compound-verb patterns;
- completion and exhaustion with common `〜きる` uses;
- incomplete or interrupted action with high-value `〜かける` uses where justified;
- `〜てしまう`, `〜ておく`, `〜てある`, and `〜ている` in connected narratives;
- habitual, ongoing, resultant, preparatory, completed, and regrettable readings.

### Stance, qualification, and sentence endings

Cover ordinary intermediate control of:

- agreement, disagreement, reservation, and qualification;
- confidence, uncertainty, expectation, surprise, and recollection;
- `ね`, `よ`, `よね`, `かな`, `だろう`, and other stage-appropriate endings;
- common conversational forms such as `んだ/んです`, `って`, and `っていう` where natural;
- stage-appropriate reductions such as `〜てる`, `〜ちゃう`, `〜とく`, and `〜なきゃ`;
- concise reactions and backchannels;
- unfinished or softened turns where ordinary conversation requires them.

Casual endings require an explicit relationship. Do not imitate anime, theatrical role speech, strongly gendered stereotypes, dialect, or aggressive slang.

### Spoken and written differences

Introduce controlled contrast among:

- ordinary polite conversation;
- relationship-appropriate casual conversation;
- messages and informal written communication;
- neutral explanatory prose;
- introductory `だ/である` written style where motivated;
- spoken reductions versus their canonical written forms;
- paragraph connectors and source attribution.

Do not introduce systematic business keigo, formal reports, proposals, academic argument, or advanced correspondence; those belong to Japanese 04.

### Retelling, summary, explanation, and repair

Include repeated production of:

- chronological retelling;
- reporting what another person said or believed;
- explaining why a result occurred;
- summarizing the main point and supporting details;
- comparing two alternatives or accounts;
- giving an opinion with evidence and qualification;
- asking follow-up questions;
- checking interpretation;
- correcting a misunderstanding;
- reformulating when the listener does not understand;
- tactful disagreement and partial agreement.

## Communicative and lexical domains

Cover high-frequency intermediate words, collocations, and chunks across:

- education, study methods, examinations, research, and learning problems;
- work tasks, schedules, teamwork, meetings, workplace problems, and career plans;
- housing, moving, repairs, neighborhood life, and household decisions;
- health, appointments, recovery, habits, stress, and well-being;
- travel, transport disruptions, reservations, tourism, and problem resolution;
- relationships, invitations, favors, disagreement, apology, gratitude, and advice;
- hobbies, sport, books, film, music, events, and recommendations;
- technology, devices, online services, communication, and common digital problems;
- environment, weather, disasters, safety, and community rules;
- news, interviews, public information, simple social issues, and local institutions;
- goals, habits, personal change, choices, expectations, and reflection;
- connected narratives, messages, explanations, comparisons, and summaries.

Prefer reusable collocations and discourse chunks. Do not create one card per dictionary headword or pad the stage with tense, name, number, date, counter, or noun substitutions.

## Card contract

Use the existing schema and sequential zero-padded IDs:

```text
ja-03-001
ja-03-002
...
```

Example shape:

```json
{
  "id": "ja-03-001",
  "sentence": "電車が遅れているので、会議には間に合わないかもしれません。",
  "cloze": "間に合わないかもしれません",
  "answer": "間に合わないかもしれません",
  "accepted_answers": [],
  "lemma": "間に合う",
  "reading": "まにあわないかもしれません",
  "meaning": "might not make it in time",
  "audio": "work/audio/ja-JP/03/ja-03-001.mp3"
}
```

Rules:

- `sentence` contains `cloze` exactly once.
- `answer` equals `cloze`.
- Test one focused primary production target per card.
- Full-sentence audio must identify the exact hidden span.
- Keep visible context useful for viewpoint, evidence, source, register, and interpretation.
- Use a focused word, collocation, inflected form, or compact construction as the cloze.
- Do not hide a long multi-clause sentence. Whole-utterance clozes are allowed only for compact fixed high-value expressions.
- Keep non-target language within stages 00–02 or earlier accepted Japanese 03 slices.
- Use contemporary standard Japanese orthography.
- Keep `accepted_answers` empty unless a genuinely acceptable alternative exists.
- Do not accept rōmaji, arbitrary all-kana fallbacks, homophones, or approximate paraphrases automatically.
- Use `reading`, `grammar`, `register`, or `note` only when they materially support the answer.
- Omit unused optional fields entirely. Never emit empty optional strings.
- Keep `meaning` concise and in English.
- Do not copy textbook, corpus, web, earlier-stage, or discarded-project sentences.
- Do not translate another deck card by card.

## Register, pragmatics, and audio fairness

- `です/ます` remains the productive baseline with unfamiliar adults, teachers, staff, and professional contacts.
- Casual forms require an explicit friend, family, or close-peer relationship.
- Neutral written forms require an explicit message, article, notice, summary, or explanatory context.
- Avoid excessive `私`, `あなた`, and repeated subjects.
- Preserve Japanese information order, ellipsis, viewpoint, and beneficiary perspective.
- Make disagreement, advice, judgment, refusal, correction, and uncertainty socially plausible.
- Use `べき` and categorical claims only where their force is justified.
- Questions, invitations, confirmations, and short reactions must be punctuated or ended so the TTS cue is unambiguously interrogative when required.
- Do not let a statement-looking sentence depend on undocumented rising intonation.
