## How I read it

**This is standing guidance, not a one-off fix to one family.** "For the future" and "you need to know NEC code" make it a capability every generated electrical family should have. So it gets a `docs/STEERING.md` row, and the work becomes task issues (`Refs #<this>`).

**"The clearances" = NEC working space**, the thing an electrical designer checks first on any panel: NEC (NFPA 70) **110.26** — the working-space *depth* in front of equipment (set by voltage to ground and by Condition 1/2/3, i.e. what faces the equipment across the space), its *width* (at least 30 in or the equipment width) and *height* (at least 6½ ft or the equipment height), plus the **dedicated equipment space** above switchboards and panelboards (110.26(E)). In a Revit family that is conventionally a **clearance solid** — invisible in normal views, on its own subcategory, present for clash detection and coordination.

**Where it sits against the standing rules:**

- **S-2026-08-11-c** — generations are held to a *named* standard and say which one they followed. NEC is the named standard here, and **the edition must be named too** (2017 / 2020 / 2023 are all in force somewhere, since jurisdictions adopt at different times). Default to the newest edition, state it, make it overridable.
- **S-2026-08-11-c / O12** — the clearance depends on inputs a bare prompt usually does not give: voltage to ground and the Condition. Those are exactly the "residue" questions: default honestly (state the assumption, e.g. the Condition 1 minimum) and ask only when the prompt leaves it undetermined and a default would mislead. Never gate delivery on it (hard rule 1).
- **Provenance** — the clearance values are standard-practice values from the code, so they carry the standard and edition as their source, never `fact` about a manufacturer and never `given` unless the user stated them.
- **Hard rule 4** — a clearance solid is only "working" once desktop Revit shows it behaves (hidden in normal views, visible in the clearance subcategory, caught by clash detection). Until that verdict exists it ships labelled validated-not-certified.
- **Hard rule 6 (public repo)** — the code's *numbers* go in as data, cited by article and table. **No NFPA text is copied into the repository**: NFPA 70 is copyrighted, and a push is not retractable.
- **Evidence discipline** — the clearance table must not be written from a model's memory and stamped as settled. Every value is cited to its article/table and edition, and checked, not recalled.
