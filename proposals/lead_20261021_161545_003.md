## Why the data model has to change

Today an artifact is a single point (`lat`, `lng`) plus a `parent_id`. A campus, a building outline, a solar field, or anything in two places can't be represented.

## 1. Real geometry for artifacts (the foundation)

- **PostGIS:** move the database to the PostGIS build of Postgres 17 (same major version, so existing data carries over).
- **A geometry column** (SRID 4326) holds a Point, a Polygon or a MultiPolygon. Existing points migrate as they are.
- **An area's "location"** is a point guaranteed to be inside it (`ST_PointOnSurface`), not the geometric centre, which can fall outside an L-shaped building. Pins, distances and routes use that point; maps draw the whole shape.
- **Several separate parts:** one place in several parts is **one artifact with a MultiPolygon**. Topics stay for ideas without a place of their own (LEED, solar power). If each part needs its own page, the parts become children.
- **Spatial queries in the database:**
  - "what contains this point" (`ST_Contains`), for suggesting a parent;
  - "within N metres" (`ST_DWithin`);
  - "what's inside this area".

## 2. Map-first authoring in the Studio

- **The map is the main way to create:** draw an area, click a building to adopt its outline, or drop a pin.
- **Suggested parent:** the smallest existing area that contains the new artifact. A pin inside the campus lands under UMass; a pin inside a building's outline lands under that building. The author can change it.
- **Building outlines are imported, not traced:** about 100 come from the Energy Benchmarking layer (see #81 and `backend/seeds/umass/`), and OpenStreetMap has most of the rest. "Click a building" creates an artifact with that outline. Drawing by hand is for campus, gardens, solar fields and the like.
- **Styling:** areas get a very light fill and buildings a slightly stronger one, so the hierarchy reads on the map.
- **Map library:** move the Studio map from Leaflet to **MapLibre GL** (open source) with **Terra Draw** for drawing. MapLibre is what makes 3D buildings possible later, so switching now avoids building the editor twice. Tiles from OpenFreeMap or a self-hosted Protomaps file.

## 3. Inside buildings (staged; depends on data UMass may or may not share)

| Stage | What you get | Data needed |
|---|---|---|
| Levels as structure | Artifacts pinned to a level (basement, 1, 2, …, roof) and optionally a room; a floor picker | None. It's how OSM indoor tagging and Apple's IMDF work. |
| 2D floor plans | Each floor's plan aligned to the outline; pin rooms on it | Floor plan PDFs from Facilities / Space Management |
| 3D stack | Rotatable building with floor slabs extruded from the outline; click a floor to open its plan | Outline plus floor count and heights (MapLibre does this natively) |
| True 3D rooms | Rotate the real model and click a room | A BIM model (Revit/IFC). Newer buildings (CSL 2025, Olver) likely have one; older ones don't. Viewers: That Open Engine, xeokit. Pilot on one building. |

For a few showcase rooms with no plans, iPhone LiDAR room scans (Apple RoomPlan) are possible, but not for a whole campus.

**Privacy:** UMass may not want interior floor plans public, for security. Interior geometry would be visible to authors only, and the public app would show something coarser ("2nd floor, east wing"). To ask Facilities early.

## How this affects search and Q&A (#24, #30–#35, #64–#68, #73, #78)

For @TahsifTajwar to confirm or correct:

1. **Geospatial search (radius queries).**
   - PostGIS gives indexed `ST_DWithin` and distance ordering instead of hand-rolled maths on `lat`/`lng`.
   - Areas change what "near" means: distance to a building's *edge*, not its pin, and "inside the campus" as a filter.
   - Does your planned geospatial search assume points?
2. **The searchable text.** `canonical_text` (in #64) includes the ancestor chain ("Parents: …"). With UMass at the top and floors and rooms below buildings, chains get longer, for example `UMass Amherst > Integrative Learning Center > Floor 2 > Room 220`.
   - Does that help or dilute matching?
   - Should levels or rooms be indexed as fields instead?
   - Re-parenting many artifacts under a new UMass area would re-queue their whole subtrees. That's fine, but worth knowing for the backfill rate (#68).
3. **Q&A over places.** "What's in this building?" or "what's on the roof?" become spatial or hierarchical lookups, not only text similarity. Should retrieval combine text matches with spatial containment?
4. **Generated trails (#34).** "A short energy trail on campus" can pick stops *within* an area, and use routes (#91) for distances.
5. **Export/import (#73, #78).** The exchange format would need geometry, as GeoJSON, plus levels and rooms. Better to settle that before #78 is built than after.
6. **Topics vs multipart artifacts.** #67 asks how topic artifacts appear in search. Multipart places are a new kind: one artifact, several areas. How should they rank, and which part is "nearest"?
7. **Privacy.** If interior geometry is author-only, search and Q&A for the public must not leak it: no "room 220" coordinates in answers or snippets.
8. **Data volume.** Imported building outlines, and perhaps every room, add many artifacts with little text. Should outline-only artifacts be excluded from the semantic index until they have a description?
