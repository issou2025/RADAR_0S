### Integrated product-experience direction — discussion synthesis (owner review, 15 November 2026)

**This section captures the full product/design discussion following the owner's screenshots, not only the agreed overview-vs-reader decision.** It explains *why* the individual UX-A…UX-G findings matter and gives the future Work pass coherent product outcomes. Do not interpret this as blanket approval of every illustrative implementation choice. The **normal entry overview + direct exact-link reading** model is explicitly accepted; other specifics below are strong owner preferences or design hypotheses to validate together before implementation.

#### 1. Project responsibility: an ongoing endeavour, not a content-management system

The current TCP Project looks as though the owner must *administer* its materials: Add material, Edit this material, Save a return/source, Working context, Connections, historical provenance and nested outline all compete before reading. Each has a legitimate purpose, but exposing every mechanism at once defeats the GPT-first product goal.

**Desired separation of responsibilities:**

| Activity | Responsible experience |
| --- | --- |
| Substantive explanation, structured canonical authoring, updates, references, link maintenance and truthful continuity | **Ordinary connected GPT** via guarded Minamo MCP; not the user's routine Site form workflow |
| Owner-supplied terminal observations, reflections, personal working notes and original writing | **Independent user-owned Notes**, optionally pinned to exact canonical Project parts |
| Correcting a typo, improving an already saved passage, editing actual code/table/dialogue or making a limited direct correction | **Optional contextual Site Edit mode**, preserving native rich types and proper protected revisions |
| Reading, continuing, discovering and comparing real work | **Project Reader**, with management controls secondary or hidden until relevant |

- **Owner decision:** demote/remove the prominent manual **Add material** affordance; **do not remove the underlying canonical writer or prevent an owner-authorised GPT from adding material.** Preserve independent Notes/contributions and accurate source authority.
- **Owner preference to develop:** one discreet Edit mode from top-right breadcrumb/header. Hover/focus/select the exact material to expose a local, type-aware correction, with safe Save/Cancel; support keyboard/mobile equivalents. Avoid scattering edit/save-source controls through every reading section.
- **Design constraint:** make the productive action obvious rather than requiring the user to choose internal models, layout types, links, section IDs, source statuses, data forms, checkpoints or publication roles.

#### 2. Give Projects a distinct visual identity without making them heavy

The existing [Music & hobbies Workspace](https://minamo.jscymru.chatgpt.site/?view=workspaces) is visibly more pleasant because its title, purpose, sections and meaningful navigation are easy to follow. Projects are more complex because they own changing work, historical decisions, revision history and sources—but that complexity **should not become permanent chrome**.

**Agreed hybrid (specific owner approval):**
- **Normal Project URL:** concise **working overview** presenting genuine purpose, meaningful current material, optional “Where we got to” and a single exact Continue action where available. It should take negligible time/effort to enter the content, not become a task dashboard.
- **Saved Continue/direct part URL:** open **that exact Project passage directly** in the content-first Reader; no detour through the overview, no first-topic reset or generic/historical Reader that loses Project context.
- **Switch and return:** obvious subtle Overview/reading navigation, source-return path and browser history that preserve exact position and rendition.

**Visual direction to assess, not a rigid mock:** on spacious desktops a compact, optionally collapsible **right-side contextual region** may make useful use of otherwise wasted width for Where we got to/Continue/related information; on mobile stack/demote it appropriately. Authentic reading content stays primary. Avoid oversized first-screen banners, title repetition, narrow content with acres of wasted width, tiny labels, toolbar interruptions, or fixed cards on every Project.

**Do not simply copy Workspaces:** they are GPT-composed temporary/purpose-built *presentations*; Projects own durable substantive material and continuation. Borrow the Workspace's excellent visual hierarchy, readable tab/section interactions and low-friction progression without introducing a second arrangement engine, compulsory tabs or redundant overview/reading layers.

#### 3. Navigation must express meaning, not metadata or internal links

The Project outline currently mixes meaningful titles with raw `Reference · ms-...`, repeated group/page labels and sometimes a pseudo-section target such as `linked-0`. These are **source identity / navigation projection leaks**. The owner's actual click on a networking source promised a Library explanation but reached a disconnected Project/old Reader state showing **“The saved passage is unavailable in this material.”** Avoid treating correct underlying IDs as proof of usable navigation.

**Experience rule:** Project-owned work stays recognisably in the Project. A linked Library Article opens either as its **real original Article** with correct title and exact edition, or an explicitly labelled, faithful inline preview. It must offer a natural way back to the precise originating work. Historical/unavailable sources must be honestly identified; valid sources must not become "unavailable" because a pseudo-section is routed incorrectly.

- Present authored human titles and real relationships, never ID prefixes as ordinary visible labels when the original source is available.
- Avoid presenting identical titles twice for one actual page; hide unnecessary nested level for one-child groups.
- Keep all deliberate named-page destinations, source-to-translation relations, exact part links and rich content accessible without an overwhelming menu/sidebar.
- Keep **saved Continue** distinct from local last-opened position and from source links.

The general issue is **semantic fidelity**: durable internal identity is necessary for correctness but must not dictate the normal mental model.

#### 4. Japanese: authoring and rendering must faithfully preserve the requested *experience*

The new camera scenario is a genuine, substantial authored resource, yet the owner sees dialogue as basic speaker labels and plain paragraphs rather than a conversation experience, translations are not exposed, and some displayed furigana contradict author-verified canonical readings. Working comparison tables demonstrate that rich rendering *is possible*. A polished-looking Page alone is not success if it silently drops or changes educational meaning.

**Distinguish the two failure modes rather than assuming one:**
- **Authoring/intent:** Was an actual Workspace requested but GPT submitted a Library Article? Was dialogue/flashcard material authored as genuine native interactive parts or only described in prose/semantic annotations? This must be traced to the exact prompt and tool calls, not automatically called a backend conversion bug. (A previous assistant-proposed prompt requested an Article, so the request context matters.)
- **Rendering/presentation:** If canonical data actually contains correct English translations, ruby spans and valid interactive structures, the Reader and publisher must expose them properly. **Author-verified readings take precedence over automatic fallback**; test the concrete `間(あいだ)` / `間(ま)` and `方(かた)` / `方(ほう)` cases.

**Target:** substantial Japanese reading that feels like real dialogue, with correctly aligned contextual furigana, readable translations (visible or deliberately revealed), sensible speaker/turn presentation and audio controls only where genuinely supported. Dialogue, cards, code and tables should each retain their own native meaningful affordances. A Workspace explicitly requested for practice should use or compose source material into an actual Workspace without silently changing canonical Library sources or duplicating bodies.

**Product principle:** **Minamo should never accept meaningful semantic data then silently hide, discard or contradict it in the reading experience.** Distinguish stored-but-not-rendered from never-authored-as-rich-part, and report acceptance accordingly.

#### 5. Library: start from a body of knowledge, not a filing cabinet

The owner expects to enter **Languages → Japanese** and easily locate useful material according to its actual purpose—for example grammar, expressions/vocabulary, kanji/reading and natural conversation—rather than only see a generic flat catalogue of Articles or legacy level names. The V197 work appears to have concentrated on **curated Subject groups**, while the broader **domain/language entry and discovery experience** remains weak.

Apply the original [domain-adaptive Library RFC](docs/rfcs/LIBRARY-DOMAIN-ADAPTIVE-IMPLEMENTATION-PROPOSAL.md)'s **three levels**, not a fabricated permanent taxonomy:
1. **Broad domain/language entry:** lightweight navigation defaults, e.g. Languages → Japanese; distinct Computing entry. Make the real source corpus approachable.
2. **Authored Subject editorial structure:** genuine subject-specific overview, deliberately grouped and ordered original references; this **overrides generic defaults**. A grammar Article differs from a Japanese conversation Article; Japanese N4 differs from network diagnostics.
3. **Actual rich content capabilities:** faithful Japanese, furigana, translation/voice where present; authentic code, diagnostic/evidence table, command and exact source affordances for computing.

**Caveat:** grammar/vocabulary/kanji/conversation/reading are *illustrative useful discovery routes*, not a mandated curriculum or compulsory empty cards. Only offer routes backed by real, eligible content/metadata or explicit editorial relationships. Avoid automatic bulk regrouping, hidden reclassification, duplicate source text and breaking the curated-vs-retained boundary. Old Articles should remain discoverable even if not featured.

The success question is **“Where can I find and understand Japanese knowledge?”**, not simply “Can I open a saved N4 Subject?”

Preserve the V197 backend, canonical IDs, edition/source rights, MCP contracts, user Notes/pins/contributions and safeguarded publication. Avoid a speculative redesign, big parallel framework, fixed task dashboard, new course templates or an engineering-driven forced product taxonomy.

**Acceptance must be behavioural and visual**, not only schema checks: user finds real work immediately, follows Continue and exact source links, sees correct rich material, can write independent Notes/pins, and can make optional native-type corrections without exposure to a CMS.
