*Project description:*
# Quizonia: split out of #7 — blocked on art and audio production

Quizonia is the co-op, beat-driven dungeon crawler designed across #7 (comments G, G1–G7, the "Quizonia World" revision, the presence protocol, and "Quizonia game design v1" sections 1–15 and 16–24). The design is in good shape. The build is not blocked on design, architecture or Flutter work — it is blocked on **producing the art and the audio**.

This issue exists so that part can be picked up by a developer or an agent that can actually generate images and sound. Quizonia is **cancelled in #7** and tracked here instead. No Quizonia code has been written; nothing needs to be reverted.

## Why it was split out

The design (game design v1, §12) asks for:

- **Art:** 16×16 pixel art, palette-indexed, every sprite drawn in 4 grey tones and recoloured at runtime by a fragment shader. Needed: the player classes (Warden and Arcanist at launch, Ranger and Chorister in the content milestone) × 4 facings × idle / walk / attack / hit / death, the Hush enemy family, Sphinx bosses, Spire City buildings, floor tilesets, props, items, UI and particles. Bundled in every build, 3 MB budget, CI fails over it.
- **Audio:** short SFX pitched to the current tempo, with volume independent of the music, plus a built-in soundtrack of about 5 original tracks (Opus 96 kbps) delivered as a GitHub release asset on first Spire entry.
- **Licensing (G1):** clean room. Every file logged in `ASSETS.md` with source, author and licence; CC0 / CC BY 4.0 / CC BY-SA 4.0 / GPL-3.0 / own work only; a credits screen generated from the ledger.

Two things were tested before splitting this out, rather than assumed.

**Test 1 — procedural SFX.** Four effects (correct-answer arpeggio, wrong-answer buzz, UI click, sword swing as a swept filtered-noise burst) were synthesised from scratch in one pass, correct WAV format, sensible file sizes. This part is genuinely workable. The catch: they cannot be *heard* during authoring, so every tuning pass is blind — the mix, the harshness and the length can only be guessed at and then checked by a human. Composing the 5 original soundtrack tracks is out of reach entirely.

**Test 2 — sprites, at the exact spec.** A 16×16 four-tone warrior was hand-authored as a pixel map and rendered, then a second frame for a two-frame walk cycle. Result: the single frame reads as a small armoured figure — placeholder tier, usable to prove a renderer works. The animation does not read at all; the two frames are nearly indistinguishable, the silhouette is mushy where helmet meets shoulders, and there are stray pixels. Each frame was typed out row by row. Hundreds of frames at that rate, with no ability to judge motion, silhouette consistency or game feel, is not a realistic path to a shippable sprite set.

So: **placeholder art and blind-tuned SFX are reachable; the actual asset set is not.**

## What a capable agent or artist should do first

1. Decide the sourcing strategy. The design already leans one way: a CC0 16×16 one-bit tile pack as placeholder (it recolours trivially under the palette shader), then replace, in this order, player classes → the Hush → bosses → city buildings. That order covers what players look at most.
2. Produce the class sprite sheets first. They are the identity of the game, and everything else can stay placeholder while they exist.
3. Keep `ASSETS.md` correct from the very first file, not retroactively. The credits screen is generated from it.
4. Respect the clean-room rules in #7 G1 absolutely: no code, art, audio, names or text from Astonia or any other game, and Astonia source is never opened while working on Quizonia.
5. Hold the 3 MB bundled-art budget. Palette indexing and 4 tones exist precisely to make that budget achievable.
